# Running Combat — Basic D&D

Combat is resolved in rounds of about 10 seconds. Use **side initiative**: all player characters share one roll and all opponents share another. Keep descriptions concrete, make morale matter, and do not assume every enemy fights to the death.

## Before the first round

1. **Establish positions and distances.** Say who is in melee, who has cover, and which exits are open.
2. **Check surprise when the situation is uncertain.** Each side rolls 1d6; a side is surprised on 1–2 and cannot act in the first round. If both sides are surprised, neither acts before normal initiative begins.
3. **Resolve conversation or reaction before combat when appropriate.** Encountering an armed creature does not automatically mean initiative.
4. **Declare spells.** A caster says which spell is being attempted before initiative is rolled.

## Each combat round

### 1. Roll initiative

Each side rolls 1d6. The higher roll acts first. On a tie, actions are simultaneous: attacks still land even if the attacker is killed during the round.

Two-handed weapons strike after other weapons regardless of their side's initiative. A crossbow normally fires only once every two rounds.

### 2. First side acts

Resolve the side's actions in this order:

1. **Morale** — make any required monster or NPC morale checks.
2. **Movement** — advance, withdraw, retreat, take cover, or change position.
3. **Missile attacks** — bows, thrown weapons, and similar attacks.
4. **Magic and turning undead** — resolve declared spells and clerical turning.
5. **Melee attacks** — resolve hand-to-hand attacks.

Characters may generally move up to their combat movement and attack in the appropriate phase. A spellcaster must concentrate and cannot also move or attack while casting.

### 3. Second side acts

Resolve the same phases for the other side. Then apply end-of-round effects and begin a new round.

## Making an attack

1. Roll 1d20.
2. Add the attacker's applicable bonuses or penalties.
3. Compare the result with the number needed to hit the target's Armor Class on the [character](../reference/reference-tables.md#character-attacks) or [monster](../reference/reference-tables.md#monster-attacks) attack table.
4. On a hit, roll the attack's listed damage and subtract it from the target's hit points.

A natural 20 always hits and a natural 1 always misses. Basic D&D has **no automatic critical-hit damage** unless the DM deliberately adds that house rule.

For missile attacks, apply +1 at short range, no modifier at medium range, and –1 at long range. Apply room-specific cover modifiers, such as the –2 protection enjoyed by kobolds firing from behind a corner. Do not fire through a crowded melee without a clear line of fire unless the DM rules it possible.

## Spells

- The caster declares the spell before initiative.
- If the caster loses initiative and is hit, fails a saving throw, or is otherwise disrupted before the magic phase, the spell is lost.
- The caster must be able to speak, gesture, and concentrate.
- Resolve the spell exactly as written. If it calls for a saving throw, the target rolls 1d20 and must equal or exceed the relevant number on the [saving throw table](../reference/reference-tables.md#saving-throws).

The Concordant Staff and other campaign artifacts follow their own descriptions where those descriptions differ from an ordinary spell.

## Turning undead

Turning takes the cleric's action in the magic phase. The cleric presents a holy symbol and must be able to confront the undead.

1. Consult [Clerics vs. Undead](../reference/reference-tables.md#clerics-vs-undead).
2. If the table gives a number, roll 2d6 and equal or exceed it. **T** means success is automatic; **No effect** means the attempt cannot work.
3. On success, roll 2d6 again for the total Hit Dice of undead affected, beginning with the nearest eligible undead.
4. Turned undead flee from the cleric and will not attack while the turning holds unless cornered.

Some Shrine undead wear amulets that make them turn as a stronger category. Use each area's specific description; this protection does not apply to every undead creature in the Shrine.

## Movement, withdrawal, and retreat

Typical combat movement is:

| Character | Movement per round |
|---|---:|
| Unarmored and unencumbered | 40 feet |
| Metal-armored or encumbered | 20 feet |
| Metal-armored and encumbered | 10 feet |

For a monster, divide its listed base movement by three unless its area sheet already gives combat movement.

- **Fighting withdrawal:** move backward up to half combat movement while continuing to defend normally.
- **Retreat:** move away faster than a fighting withdrawal and make no attack that round. Adjacent enemies gain +2 on attacks against the retreating creature, and the retreating creature receives no Armor Class benefit from a shield that round.
- Basic D&D has no general opportunity-attack rule. The retreat rule handles the danger of breaking from melee.

## Morale

Roll morale when the encounter description calls for it and, as a useful default, when a side suffers its first death and when half its force is dead or unable to fight.

Roll 2d6:

- A result **equal to or below** the creature's Morale score means it holds.
- A result **above** its Morale score means it breaks.

A failed check does not always mean blind flight. Intelligent opponents may retreat, surrender, bargain, barricade a door, or abandon wounded allies. Animals usually flee toward safety. Undead with Morale 12 normally fight without checking.

## Defeat and recovery

- At **0 hit points**, a creature dies under the Basic rules. There are no death saves.
- A creature may surrender before reaching 0 hp. The victor decides whether to accept.
- Healing restores hit points but cannot raise the dead unless an effect specifically says it can.
- After combat, account for ammunition, spells, charges, casualties, prisoners, noise, and alerted nearby rooms.

## Fast procedure for this campaign

- Ask the players for intentions together, then roll side initiative.
- Roll identical monster attacks together and use the room sheet's listed hit points and morale.
- Describe injuries without hiding the numbers needed for informed decisions.
- Check morale promptly. A surrender, retreat, or negotiation is often more useful than playing out several foregone rounds.
- Let alarms and reinforcements follow the location sheets. Combat should change the caves rather than occur in an isolated room.

## Quick round reminder

> **Declare spells → initiative d6 → morale → move → missiles → magic/turning → melee → other side → end-of-round effects.**
