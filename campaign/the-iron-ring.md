# The Iron Ring & the Black Eagle — the conspiracy layer

The horizon the whole campaign is pointed at, hidden under the famine. This page holds the **DM truth**, the **evidence trail** (built as a puzzle, not a handout), and how the party's findings move the world toward a **Duke–Baron confrontation.**

> **Design rule:** no single smoking gun. The conspiracy is a **puzzle the party assembles** from scattered pieces, several of which they may carry out *without understanding* (the [curdle at beat 8](../narrative/progression.md)). Redundancy is deliberate — see [open decision #3](../narrative/progression.md).

---

## The DM truth (what nobody on stage knows)

- A **cult of Thanatos** in the Shrine manufactures the famine: undead destroy the caves' food at night, the caves raid to survive, everyone blames each other and the land.
- The cult believes it serves **Traladaran liberation** against the colonizer Duke. It is in fact a node of the **Iron Ring**, the slaving network run out of the **Black Eagle Barony.**
- **Bargle** — the Baron's mage, a secret Thanatos worshipper — is likely the real hand behind it. The cult's rage is real and **aimed exactly wrong.** Its leader, **[Vesna the Hierophant](../characters/the-hierophant.md)**, is a sincere Traladaran patriot who has no idea they serve the Baron — and is built to suicide-ascend rather than be interrogated.
- The **bugbears**, under chief **[Vargoth "the Welcomer"](../characters/faction-leaders.md#bugbear-chief--vargoth-the-welcomer)**, are the witting northern intake end: a slave-station delivering captives into the Barony's covert route through the fens and western waterways.
- The Barony pays the bugbears for captives in **Eagles**, its gold-washed lead scrip. When its representatives return, they bring trade goods and accept Eagles, so the bugbears receive real value—but at prices set entirely by the Barony, and they vastly overpay. They regard the Eagles as gold because, uniquely among the caves, they have a channel through which the coins spend. They use some to hire **[Brundt](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md)** as muscle. Brundt accepts them as gold but has no access to the Barony's trade visits, so his Eagles simply accumulate. The chain is **Barony → bugbears → Brundt**, with diminishing value and increasing isolation at every step.
- **The geography is the plot.** The caves sit at the northern end of the Baron's concealed collection route, while isolated Riverfork Keep watches the legitimate roads and waterways without recognizing the traffic moving through the swamp margins and forest tracks around it. The Duke's “simple monster raids” commission becomes the investigation that first exposes the pattern. See [The Borderland](the-borderland.md).
- **One population the famine can't touch.** A wetland's food base can't be destroyed the way game-trails and stores can, so the [marsh lizardfolk](the-lizardfolk-of-the-marsh.md) are the people *least* vulnerable to Thanatos — and the federation's one larder the cult can't sabotage, valuable exactly when the caves' supply is under attack.
- **The players never meet Thanatos**, and **Bargle is built to escape** (canon-friendly). The win is to *name and hold* the work, then force it into the light — not to end it.

## The evidence trail (the puzzle)

Pieces, where they're found, and what each *alone* proves (deliberately: not enough). Only **assembled** do they implicate the Barony.

| Piece | Where | Alone it proves… | The gap it leaves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Iron-ring cord-symbols** | the Shrine | the cult's faith is *not* genuinely Traladaran | but not *whose* it is — needs the Church of Traladar to name it |
| **The pricing anomaly** | bugbear slave-station | slaves cost more than the food market can pay → a **richer buyer upcountry** exists | but not *who* the buyer is |
| **Eagles and the payment chain** | the bugbear lair and Brundt's hoard | someone connected to the Barony paid the bugbears, who then paid Brundt | Eagles circulate near the frontier; the coins alone don't prove what purchased them |
| **[Skarn's](../characters/skarn-the-rebel-bugbear.md) testimony** | pen 41 | the station answers to an outside oath/structure; insider detail on the node | a defector's word — needs hard backing |
| **[Khaeros's](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md) route** | his story | a Black Eagle slave-ship landed him near the Barony, then overland | one captive's account — circumstantial |
| **[Aldric Venn's](the-bandit-camp.md) testimony** | the bandit camp | the corridor, the cove, how the Ring handles "acquisitions," and that the Baron traffics in *thinking creatures* | a wanted outlaw's word — but he *drove the cart* |
| **The famine pattern** | the caves at night | the starvation is *manufactured*, not natural | points at the cult, not yet the Baron |
| **[Mara Sokol's testimony](../characters/mara-sokol.md)** | Shrine room 54 | the undead raids were deliberate; Vesna received aid and orders from “friends beyond the southern fens” | she knows neither the Iron Ring's name nor the identity of its patrons |

**How it locks together:** Mara confirms that the famine was deliberate and the Shrine's support came through the southern fens; the rings say *not Traladaran*; the pricing says *a rich buyer exists*; Khaeros, Aldric, and the ship trace the route through Black Eagle territory; the rebel bugbear says *there's an oath-bound structure*; the Eagles trace payments from the Barony through the bugbears to Brundt; the Church of Traladar supplies legitimacy to the reading. No one piece convicts. Together they're a case the Duke can't wave away.

**The social shortcut — Brundt.** The ogre has been for hire to every faction for years, so he's the ravine's living index: he knows Khaeros's history (he's the one who *asked*), and he knows enough to refuse jobs near the Shrine. He'll *sell* leads for coin and cheese — but he can't read what he's selling, so he points the party at pieces without ever assembling them himself. A guide to the puzzle, not a solver of it. See [Brundt](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md).

### The bugbear-lair clues (keep it a puzzle)
There are tangible links here — but make the party **work to assemble them**, not trip over a confession:
- **Eagles** — the Black Eagle Barony's gold-washed lead scrip. The bugbears receive sacks of Eagles in payment for slaves and can spend them when Barony representatives return with overpriced trade goods. They use the coins to pay Brundt when they need hired muscle, but Brundt has no access to that market. Eagles are known near the frontier and lawful to possess, so even this payment chain proves commerce with someone tied to the Barony, not by itself what was bought.
- **A tally / manifest** of "stock" delivered upcountry: counts, dates, a destination glyph the party can't read yet.
- **Standardized iron-ring fittings** matching the Shrine's cord-symbols — the same supplier behind both faces of the operation.
- **A courier's route or a name** that means nothing now and everything once the Church of Traladar and Khaeros's story are added.

The point: each clue is a *fragment*. The detective work — and the redundancy that keeps the plot from dying on one missed roll — is the players fitting them together.

## Before the Duchy starts watching

Stefan knows the Black Eagle Baron is severe, self-interested, and a source of recurring trouble. He has heard frontier accusations, but they arrive as rumor, refugee testimony, missing cargo, and incidents that can be blamed on bandits or humanoids. At the campaign's start, neither the Duke nor Riverfork Keep understands that these fragments describe an organized slave pipeline tied to the Iron Ring.

- **He is not complicit, and he is not yet fully informed.** His cousin's status makes Stefan too ready to accept ordinary explanations, but there has not previously been a coherent case for him to suppress. Play the flaw as complacency and familial benefit of the doubt, not an active cover-up.
- **The party creates the turning point.** Eagles, manifests, witnesses, and route evidence give Stefan and Valerian something they have never had: multiple independent pieces that predict the same hidden structure. Their detective work causes the Duchy to begin watching the Baron systematically and moves the world toward a Duke–Baron confrontation.
- See his full profile: **[Duke Stefan Karameikos](../characters/duke-stefan-karameikos.md).**

**The first institutional response begins at Riverfork.** [Valerian](../characters/castellan-valerian.md) starts with disconnected gate logs, boat reports, and routine frontier incidents. Once the party shows him the payment chain and witness accounts, he changes patrol routes, audits traffic through the river country, and begins producing corroboration. His reports give Stefan reason to broaden the inquiry and eventually assign Radlebb Keep a more deliberate watch over the Westron Road and the Baron's northern approaches.

## Canon note (Mystara)

- **Baron Ludwig von Hendriks**, the Black Eagle Baron of **Halag / Fort Doom** (SW Karameikos); patron of **Bargle the Infamous**; kin to Duke Stefan. Tie to the slave trade is canonical; the **Iron Ring / Thanatos** framing is this campaign's own.
- At the **default ~AC 1000** start the Baron is entrenched and untouched. The canonical fall comes later (**~AC 1009–1012**, *Wrath of the Immortals* era): the Barony is invaded and von Hendriks deposed, **Bargle escapes.** *(Confident on the window; fuzzier on exact year and which army.)*
- **Upshot:** the party's evidence at Riverfork is the early event that causes Stefan to begin sustained scrutiny of the Baron and later strengthens Radlebb's anti-Baron mandate. It starts the historical process that eventually turns open suspicion into confrontation; Bargle's canonical knack for escaping keeps the root evil unkillable by design.
