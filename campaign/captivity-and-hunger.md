# Captivity & Hunger — the economic puzzles

Three of the caves' ugliest behaviors — slavery, prisoner-keeping, and eating humans — look like one moral problem and are really **three different economic puzzles.** Sorting them by *why* is what tells you which ones trade can fix and which one leads straight to the Baron.

> **The governing principle:** you don't moralize the caves out of these behaviors. You make the behavior *uneconomical* by putting something better on the cart. Self-interest does the rest — the same way it does for everyone. **The lone exception is the Iron Ring,** which has a richer buyer than grain can outbid, so it doesn't resolve at the cave level at all.

---

## Part 1 — Captivity: two puzzles that share a word

Both the bugbears and the hobgoblins hold captives. The word "prisoner" hides that these are **opposite** problems.

### Bugbears — *slavery (labor / commodity).* Unsolvable locally.
*Pop. ~22.* They put up welcome signs, capture the guests who answer, and hold them in **two pens** (B2 rooms 40 and 41). The captives are a deliberately **mixed lot** — kobolds, a goblin, orcs, humans, optionally a dwarf and elves in one pen; hobgoblins, gnolls, a rebel bugbear, and a berserk human "Hero" in the other. The indiscriminate mix is the tell: to the bugbears, **everyone is just stock** — human and demi-human alike, no species line at all.

In this campaign they are **witting Iron Ring** — their slave-station is the **intake** end of the Black Eagle's pipeline. That's what makes them the one living faction you *can't* integrate: they've chosen the worst side freely, and they weaponize their welcome to betray the peace.

- **Why trade can't fix it — the buy-and-free trap:** their slaves are priced *above what the food market can pay* (proof of a richer buyer upcountry). The party can't afford to buy-and-free — and even if they could, **paying slavers for slaves funds the next capture.** The bugbear slavery doesn't resolve in the ravine. It resolves **upcountry, at the Baron.** The bugbears are the symptom; the Black Eagle is the disease. *(Pays off toward [beat 8](../narrative/progression.md).)*
- **You fight them, you don't feed them.** This is the deliberate hard "no" that makes the leeway elsewhere mean something.

> **The gem — the rebel bugbear (pen 41).** A bugbear his own kind enslaved for refusing or breaking the Iron Ring's oath. Living proof that "bugbear" isn't a moral category and the allegiance was a *choice some refused.* Free him and you don't get a grateful pet — you get a **defector with a grievance and inside knowledge of the Iron Ring node.** *(Also a redundant clue path toward the conspiracy — see [open decision #3](../narrative/progression.md).)*

### Hobgoblins — *prisoners as food.* Solvable immediately.
*Pop. ~90 + 1 ogre.* They keep captives too (the room with two big hobgoblins, the whip, six chained prisoners), but their captives are **food, not labor** — scheduled to be eaten, not worked or sold.

- **Why trade fixes it instantly:** a prisoner-as-*meat* is worth more **alive and sold back** than dead and eaten, so a food-for-prisoners market **flips them on the first trade.** This is the campaign's humanitarian pilot program, and it *works.*
- Contrast the bugbears: a prisoner-as-*labor* keeps producing value, so grain can't outbid it. **Same word, opposite puzzle.**

**The split, one line:** bugbears keep *slaves* (commodity, Iron Ring, unsolvable locally → the Baron); hobgoblins keep *prisoners as food* (solvable immediately by trade). Only one of them leads to the Baron.

## Part 2 — Cannibalism: who eats humans, and on what logic

None of the easy cases eat humans because they're *evil* or because they *prefer* it. They eat humans because **humans are the most plentiful food and the caves are hungry** — and that hunger is *manufactured* by the cult's undead destroying the local food at night. So: cannibalism is downstream of the famine, the famine is downstream of the cult. **The cure isn't a sermon — it's feeding them,** which is the federation's whole economic project. You make humans the *worst-value* food by putting something better on the cart.

### Khaeros the minotaur — *subsistence. The proof of concept.*
He says it himself, openly, with a roasted pig in front of him: *you eat the flesh of other animals; this isn't that different; there isn't much food; humans are the most plentiful source; everyone here does it.* Not a confession of horror — a subsistence argument, and a good one the party can't really refute. His key line: **it isn't preferred.** He'd rather eat civilized food and would happily trade for it — and he **abandons the practice the instant a real alternative exists.** That's the whole approach in one creature. *(See [Khaeros](../characters/index.md), profile pending.)*

### Brundt the ogre — *subsistence. The easiest case.*
Same logic, but his bag already tells you he's eating better when he can (the hard cheese, the keg of brandy). An appetite that keeps what he likes and what shines; if humans are around and he's hungry, he eats — but he's the creature most visibly **trading up** to civilized food the moment it's available. He barely needs convincing; he's already halfway out of the practice on his own. *(See [Brundt](../characters/index.md), profile pending.)*

### Lizardfolk (swamp) — *alien pragmatism. Tradeable.*
A *probable* yes, on a third logic again: the pragmatic, survival-keyed morality D&D lizardfolk tend toward — the dead are food, sentiment doesn't enter into it. Neither desperation nor cruelty, just practicality that doesn't flatter human feeling. That makes them an **easy federation candidate** — their "evil" is only values that don't comfort us. *(They're a [swamp thread](../narrative/progression.md), framed here as a reptilian offshoot of kobold.)*

### Hobgoblins — *the one to watch. Subsistence or culture?*
Theirs is **institutional**: prisoners kept specifically for the pot, scheduled banquets, a torture chamber that doubles as food storage. That raises the question the others answer for themselves and they don't —
- **If subsistence** (the marsh is thin, meat is meat): trade dissolves it like it dissolves Khaeros.
- **If cultural/ritual** (woven into status, ceremony, how a feast is marked): you're asking the most organized faction in the ravine to *stop being who they are* — the Traladaran problem at maximum, and the knot diplomacy genuinely snags on.

**You must decide which before an envoy walks in.** This is the campaign's biggest open decision → [progression, open decisions](../narrative/progression.md).

## The clean sort

| Who | Logic | Trade fixes it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Khaeros** (minotaur) | Subsistence — and says so | Instantly. Proof of concept. |
| **Brundt** (ogre) | Subsistence — already trading up | Instantly. Easiest case. |
| **Lizardfolk** | Alien pragmatism | Yes — easy federation candidate. |
| **Hobgoblins** | Possibly *institutional* | **Only if subsistence.** The open decision. |
| **Bugbears** | Not hunger at all — Iron Ring commerce | **No.** Resolves at the Baron, not the cave. |
