# The Audience with Duke Stefan

**Spine beat:** [1 — The Commission](../narrative/progression.md) · **Type:** scene (social, no combat) · **Party:** level 3, provisional trust already earned

> **The payload, one line:** *Go and see what it actually is, before deciding what it is.* Everything else in this scene is delivery and seeds. If the table only takes away one thing, it's this.

---

## At a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | The commission — and a test the Duke is running on the party as much as the party will run on the caves. |
| **Where** | A working chamber, not the throne room. Maps on the table, a window toward the wild country. Business, not pageantry. |
| **The ask** | Go to the Caves of Chaos and *open relations.* Find the leaders, learn what they need and offer, broker a peace. **If they won't even talk, clear them out.** |
| **The tone** | Stefan is sincere about *both halves* and resolves neither. Don't play him as secretly cruel. Play him as a good man who means every word. |
| **Run it** | Short. This is a hook, not a lore dump. The Duke's *idea* is the payload; the rest is one-sentence seeds you plant and don't water. |

---

## Setting the scene

They've earned **provisional trust** — mirror that exact word back at the table, because it's the same trust the whole campaign will ask everyone to extend. Level 3 means *proven but not important.* The Duke isn't sending his army. He's sending a small, deniable crew he can disavow if the experiment fails — which is itself the first quiet truth about what he's asking.

Stage it so it reads *real but not ceremonial:* a working room, maps spread out, the Black Eagle's barony conspicuously **not** on the table, a window that happens to look toward the borderland. Stefan does business here.

## Running the audience

**1. The surface (any duke would say this).**
Raids on travelers have worsened along the roads near a frontier keep — Karameikos has many places where the writ runs thin, and "the borderlands" is wherever the tamed country gives out. Merchants are losing goods and lives. The garrison holds the wall but can't police the wilderness. The source: humanoid lairs in a cave complex within striking distance of the road. He wants it *handled.* Standard.

**2. The turn (this is the campaign).**
Then he chooses to trust them with an unorthodox idea — lowering his voice not from secrecy but from the weight of saying something he knows sounds naive:

He doesn't think the raiders are simply evil. He thinks they're **hungry.** From what little is known, they have leaders, crafts, territory — and they *don't* destroy each other. That's not chaos; that's people in bad land with no other way to eat. People with specialized skills, settled in a place, can be *traded with.* A kingdom is stronger when each of its peoples contributes what it does best. He'd rather have the borderland's inhabitants holding his frontier *for* him than bleeding it.

So the commission: **go and open relations.** Who leads them? What do they need, what can they offer? Broker a peace if one can be brokered.

**3. The contradiction (don't resolve it).**
Then, unsoftened — because softening it would be a lie — the closed fist: *if they won't even talk, clear them out.* The road has to be safe. The wilderness will be tamed one way or another. He's offering the better way; he is not pretending it's the only way.

Let that sit in the air. A lesser duke would be secretly cruel. Stefan is sincere **and** will still issue that order, and a sharp player should feel the discomfort of a man who means both halves.

---

## Seeds to plant (quietly — don't flag them)

Drop these as texture. Let the players decide what matters. Each pays off later.

| Seed | How to play it | Pays off |
|---|---|---|
| **"One way or another."** | Say it once, plainly, move on. | The campaign's whole moral tension; returns when the party finds people in the caves worth saving. |
| **The Duke's own legitimacy** | If asked by what right he tames the borderland, be *honest* — his title traces to a grant, and he knows what grants are built on. He's offering the caves the same deal he took: lose some autonomy, gain a place inside something that would otherwise roll over you. Not charity. | The whole "who draws the line between person and monster" thread. |
| **The Church comes along** | He's sending **[Father Demetrios Kallistos](../characters/father-demetrios-kallistos.md)**, a cleric of the **Church of Karameikos** — for healing and the *speak languages* staff they'll need to parley at all. Frame it as generosity. | It's also the empire embedded in the expedition: the party will *hear the caves through a Karameikan throat.* Nobody names this yet. |
| **Don't mention the Black Eagle** | If a player raises the slaver-baron rumors, he deflects — heard them, doesn't condone what he's heard, fighting a hundred battles, doesn't *want* it true. A good man's blind spot, not a villain's cover-up. | His denial is the keystone the later conspiracy rests on — the same denial Darokin's guilds will have about their ledgers. |

## What the Duke offers (practical terms)

Enough to start, not enough to feel safe. He's funding a *negotiation,* not a campaign.

- **A letter of authority** — his sanction. Means something at the Keep, nothing in the caves.
- **The Karameikos cleric** — translation (*speak languages*) and healing.
- **Seed resources to open trade** — coin, or the promise of supply from the Keep if they can establish a reason for it. Not a war chest.
- **Honest intelligence** — the road raids and rough location, plus an admission that he knows almost nothing reliable about who's actually *in* the caves. That ignorance is the point: he's sending them to *find out,* and the finding out is the adventure.

## The note to end on

Close not on a threat but on something that reveals the man — and gives the party a reason to *want* the harder path. In his words, roughly: he'd consider it a greater victory to bring the borderland under one banner than to empty it and start over with strangers he understands even less. Killing them is temporary; *something* moves into good caves. He'd rather know his neighbors.

Then the dismissal: the Keep's castellan is expecting them. Go and see what's actually out there before deciding what it is.

---

## DM notes

- **The player seat is still open, and this scene sets it.** Are they Stefan's loyal envoys, or freelancers who might broker *for* the caves against the crown? Don't force it — let how they react to "one way or another" tell you which way they lean, and let the politics follow.
- **Keep it short.** The Duke's *idea* is the payload. Legitimacy, the Church, the Baron are each one sentence, planted and not watered.
- **Provisional trust cuts both ways.** He's testing whether they can do something subtle. If they're murder-hobos, this scene is where you find that out — useful information, not a problem to fix. The caves teach the lesson regardless.
- **Top of the vertical.** The Duke's denial about the Baron here is the *same denial* Darokin's guilds will have about their ledgers — one blind spot at three scales. See [Overview → the vertical](../overview.md).
