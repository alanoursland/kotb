# Faction Negotiations — Running the Caves in Any Order

*A DM-facing playbook for the party's dealings with the kobolds, hobgoblin–goblin bloc, bugbears, and Shrine, including how every earlier choice changes the next encounter.*

**Use:** spine beats 4–7 | **Core locations:** [Caves of Chaos](../locations/caves/index.md) | **Leaders:** [Yiznak, Karrgan, and Vargoth](../characters/faction-leaders.md) | **Relief option:** [The Food Convoy](the-food-convoy.md)

> **The structure:** Food creates room to negotiate, but it does not settle anyone's politics. The party may approach the core factions in any order. Kobolds and hobgoblins require different bargains; the bugbears offer a false bargain; the cult offers none. Earlier successes supply witnesses, leverage, and evidence for later encounters.

## At a glance

| Group | What the party first sees | What is really happening | Playable result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Yiznak's kobolds** | Traps, rats, frightened guards, nearly empty stores | An overlooked engineering community losing food and people | Direct food and tool access in exchange for safe passage, engineering, and an end to raids |
| **Karrgan's hobgoblin–goblin bloc** | A disciplined military power holding prisoners for the pot | Famine managed through hierarchy, coercion, and captive-taking | Food for all prisoners, a ceasefire, patrol obligations, and limits on Karrgan's control of the goblins |
| **Vargoth's bugbears** | The warmest welcome in the ravine | A slave-intake cell gathering information for an ambush | Exposure, rescue of the captives, destruction of the cell, and evidence carried to the other factions |
| **The Shrine** | A hidden religious enemy directing undead | The famine engine and Vargoth's covert partner | Destroy the operation, save Mara if possible, and recover evidence of the cult–bugbear conspiracy |

## Run negotiations as three accomplishments

Do not reduce a faction to one reaction roll. Give every real negotiation three accomplishments:

1. **Contact:** reach the leader without a fight and establish a shared language or safe procedure.
2. **Proof:** do something that demonstrates the party understands the faction's actual problem.
3. **Terms:** exchange promises concrete enough that both sides know what happens tomorrow.

A good argument can accomplish one of these. So can food, returning a missing resident, exposing a lie, clearing a shared danger, or keeping an earlier promise. Roll reactions only when the players have created uncertainty rather than supplied a compelling answer.

The [Concordant Staff](../characters/father-demetrios-kallistos.md#the-concordant-staff) can stop a living attack long enough to reopen contact. It cannot make Vargoth bargain honestly, compel truthful answers, or affect undead.

## Track four pieces of state

These are enough to make order matter without running a complicated clock.

### Food

- **None:** hunger dominates every conversation.
- **Promised:** leaders want proof that the party can make Riverfork deliver.
- **Delivered once:** immediate desperation eases; permanent terms remain unsettled.
- **Bridge established:** raids caused by hunger can end without relying on goodwill.

### Missing residents

Vargoth holds **three kobolds** and **three hobgoblins**. At the start, both communities assume their people disappeared in the wilderness.

- **Unknown:** Vargoth can exploit the disappearances.
- **Evidence found:** Shrine papers or bugbear records show that the missing residents are captives.
- **Returned:** the rescued residents are living witnesses and the party's strongest proof of good faith.

### The cult–bugbear connection

- **Suspicion:** matching cord, guest traces, strange trade goods, or Vargoth's evasions.
- **Local proof:** Shrine correspondence, torture records, framing packets, Skarn, or bugbear tallies establish cooperation.
- **Wider case:** Eagles, manifests, Khaeros, and route evidence point beyond the ravine. This is not required to settle the cave negotiations.

### Vargoth's sabotage

- **Quiet:** before the party secures its first agreement.
- **Watching:** after one faction agrees to terms. Bugbear observers learn who the party trusts.
- **Preparing:** after a second agreement, a convoy announcement, or a public meeting. Vargoth and the cult prepare a false incident.
- **Exposed:** the party finds the Shrine's papers, framing packets, Vargoth's prisoners, or Skarn's testimony.

Advance this state because the party becomes visible, not merely because time passes. Evidence can move it directly to **Exposed**. A false incident should complicate a negotiation, never erase it.

## Yiznak's kobolds

**Leader:** [Yiznak the Deepwright](../characters/faction-leaders.md#kobold-chief--yiznak-the-deepwright) | **Meeting place:** [area 5](../locations/caves/05-yiznaks-chamber.md) | **Population:** [71 kobolds](../locations/caves/population-kobolds.md)

**Expanded playable branches:** [Kobold Negotiations scenario set](../scenarios/kobold_negotiations/index.md)

### What Yiznak wants

- Food delivered directly to his people rather than through Karrgan or another larger faction.
- Recognition that kobold excavation, drainage, traps, and repair are useful work worth payment.
- Tools, lamp oil, cord, scrap metal, and access to trade.
- Safety for the common chamber and information about the three missing kobolds.

### Contact

Approaching openly still risks the covered pit and tree ambushers. Calling from outside in Kobold, showing food without tossing it like animal bait, or allowing a kobold escort to control the approach establishes contact. Brynja's Kobold and tunnel vocabulary is ideal; the staff also works.

The guards ask three practical questions: **How many are you? Who sent you? What do you want dug?** The last question reveals how the kobolds understand their own value.

### Proof — give Yiznak a problem to solve

Show Yiznak a road sketch, convoy plan, collapsed drain, cave approach, or proposed market site and ask what is wrong with it. He identifies a real flaw: runoff will wash out the wagon ground, a turn is too narrow, a sentry line has a blind approach, or the storage plan invites rats.

Taking his correction seriously is the proof. Brynja can discuss the engineering as a peer. Tavin can translate it into loads and labor. A character who merely praises Yiznak without trusting his work has not yet understood him.

### Terms

A strong agreement contains all of these:

- an immediate ration allotment and a named route for future deliveries;
- tools and consumable supplies bought or advanced against work;
- kobold labor paid for drainage, road repair, tunneling, and inspection rather than demanded as tribute;
- safe passage for recognized travelers and an end to hunger raids;
- direct standing in later meetings instead of representation through Karrgan;
- a promise to investigate the missing kobolds.

Yiznak can mark the agreement on matched tally-sticks. He does not need to trust ducal writing.

### Failure and recovery

- **If the party patronizes him:** he becomes ceremonially agreeable and practically unhelpful. Recovery requires asking for and using his professional judgment.
- **If they trigger the defenses:** survivors retreat behind area 4. The staff, medical aid, surrender of captured weapons, or payment for damage can reopen contact.
- **If they offer only one meal:** he accepts it, but correctly calls it food rather than peace.

### Vargoth's sabotage against Yiznak

The prepared version is vandalism of a tunnel marker or food cache with ducal-style material left behind and a stolen kobold tool planted at a second scene. Vargoth wants Yiznak to conclude that the party used kobold hospitality to map the lair and then attacked it.

Make the manipulation discoverable:

- broad boot impressions are too large for the party and match the Shrine guest room;
- packing cord matches Cave H bundles;
- the kobold tool belonged to one of the three missing residents;
- the ducal wax or arrowheads are unused trade stock, not equipment carried by the party;
- a tracker finds that the intruders approached from above the ravine rather than Riverfork's road.

Yiznak suspends terms and demands an explanation; he does not launch a suicidal attack. Solving the frame can produce more trust than no sabotage at all.

### How earlier choices change this meeting

- **Kobolds first:** Yiznak sees untested envoys. He asks for a small delivery, one useful tool exchange, or investigation of the missing before committing fully.
- **Hobgoblins first:** he asks whether the party has already made Karrgan ruler of the proposed peace. Direct delivery and an independent tally-stick become non-negotiable.
- **Bugbears first:** returning the three kobolds completes Proof immediately. A rescued kobold can identify Vargoth's intake procedure and the negotiation begins with exceptional trust.
- **Cult first:** the Shrine papers reveal that the kobolds are alive in Vargoth's pens and that their possessions were taken for a frame. Showing the papers exposes sabotage before it starts.
- **Food convoy first:** Yiznak cooperates if he receives a direct allotment. If his food passes through Karrgan, later negotiation becomes partly a demand for political independence.

## Karrgan's hobgoblin–goblin bloc

**Leader:** [Karrgan](../characters/faction-leaders.md#hobgoblin-chief--karrgan) | **Meeting places:** [feast hall](../locations/caves/25-hobgoblin-feast-hall.md) or [Karrgan's quarters](../locations/caves/30-karrgans-quarters.md) | **Population:** [63 hobgoblins](../locations/caves/population-hobgoblins.md), plus the dependent goblin ward

**Expanded playable branches:** [Hobgoblin Negotiations scenario set](../scenarios/hobgoblin_negotiations/index.md)

### Campaign default used here

The eating of captives is **famine-driven but institutionalized**, not a sacred obligation. Karrgan has turned emergency food into procedure, status, and discipline because commanders turn recurring necessity into rules. A dependable food supply gives him a practical reason to end it, but he needs the change presented publicly as a successful command decision rather than moral submission.

### What Karrgan wants

- Recognition as the commander of the ravine's strongest organized force.
- Food security under terms that preserve discipline and authority.
- A defined military role: patrols, road security, convoy escort, or holding ground the Keep cannot garrison.
- Information about the three missing hobgoblins.
- Influence over the goblin ward and any future federation.

### Contact

Do not sneak into a command meeting. State the Duke's commission at the barred entrance, request safe conduct, accept counting and weapon control, and let the guards escort the party. Leontes's military vocabulary and bearing matter more than courtly charm. Karrgan notices whether the party has a spokesperson, whether companions interrupt one another, and who is authorized to promise food.

His opening challenge is administrative:

> “Name what you offer, what you require, who answers when your promise fails, and what happens when one of mine breaks it.”

A clear answer establishes Contact.

### Proof — solve the prisoners without humiliating him

The six prisoners in [area 24](../locations/caves/24-hobgoblin-prison-kitchen.md) make the negotiation concrete. Offer enough food to replace their expected value and a repeatable rule for future captives:

- prisoners are exchanged alive for food, goods, coin, or recognized bounties;
- captured road raiders are held for an agreed period and reported through Brundt or another messenger;
- killing and eating captives ends when the first delivery arrives;
- the release occurs publicly in the feast hall as Karrgan announcing a more profitable policy.

This is not purchasing six people as property. Payment is emergency leverage attached to a rule that ends the market in human meat. The distinction matters: there must be no reward for taking replacements.

Returning the three hobgoblins from Vargoth's pen is even stronger proof. It shows that the party protects Karrgan's people while Vargoth treats them as stock.

### The goblin question

Karrgan's four-person party secretly steals food from [goblin storage](../locations/caves/21-goblin-storage.md). A stable agreement cannot ignore this.

The goblin ward has also shared in human meat distributed through Karrgan's bloc during the famine, although it keeps no separate human pen. Direct food ends that conduct quickly; direct accounting matters because provisions routed only through Karrgan can preserve the same dependency that produced it.

Give the players three possible settlements:

1. **Karrgan remains overlord:** he receives the goblin allotment and accepts responsibility for them. Fastest, but strengthens the faction most able to dominate the federation.
2. **Direct goblin allotment:** goblin porters receive food and sign separately while remaining military allies. Best balance; Karrgan demands compensation in status or patrol command.
3. **Goblin independence:** expose the theft route and negotiate with the headman separately. Morally cleanest, politically most disruptive, and likely to require protection from retaliation.

Do not let the party accidentally make this decision by handing every sack to the largest creature present.

### Terms

A workable agreement includes:

- immediate food for release of the six prisoners;
- no new captive-taking for food or replacement prisoners;
- a ceasefire on the road and named consequences for violations;
- food wages for specific patrol or escort work rather than arms or unrestricted coin;
- separate accounting for the goblin share;
- no claim that Karrgan commands Yiznak, the orcs, or Maruk merely because he commands the largest formation.

### Failure and recovery

- **If the party calls him a savage:** he ends the audience rather than losing control before witnesses. Recovery requires a peer-commander frame and a materially better policy.
- **If they demand prisoner release without replacement food:** he asks who feeds his common room tomorrow. Showing actual provisions or arranging a short exchange answers him.
- **If they expose goblin theft publicly without protection:** Karrgan treats it as an attack on command. Recovery may require a mediated meeting with the goblin headman and another chief as witness.
- **If fighting starts:** Karrgan uses his prepared defense and does not surrender cheaply. The staff can create one turn to state terms, but the party must still offer a face-saving stand-down.

### Vargoth's sabotage against Karrgan

Vargoth wants Karrgan to believe the ducal envoys captured or killed the missing patrol while pretending friendship. A false scene uses a stolen hobgoblin token, ducal arrowheads, and a message alleging that Yiznak or the party informed on them.

Clues point outward:

- the token belongs to a hobgoblin currently alive in area 41;
- the message uses military words correctly but gets Karrgan's reporting formula wrong;
- bugbear cord or catnip contaminates the packet;
- the supposed ducal material matches the Shrine's framing cache;
- Karrgan's own scouts find no party tracks on the claimed route.

Karrgan's response is formal: detention at the threshold, presentation of the accusation, and a demand for an answer. This makes the frame an investigation scene rather than an unavoidable battle.

### How earlier choices change this meeting

- **Hobgoblins first:** this is the hardest version. Karrgan has no reason to believe the food bridge is real, and the missing patrol looks like ordinary wilderness loss. He may demand a small convoy, prisoner exchange, or joint patrol as proof.
- **Kobolds first:** he respects that the party can make terms but tests whether Yiznak's agreement makes Karrgan subordinate. Offer him a distinct military role without giving him sovereignty over the kobolds.
- **Bugbears first:** returning the three hobgoblins and showing the pen tally transforms the audience. Karrgan still bargains hard, but Vargoth's planned frame is dead and fighting the bugbears costs no trust.
- **Cult first:** Vesna's papers show that the missing hobgoblins are alive and that their tokens were intended for sabotage. Karrgan grants time to strike Vargoth or recover them before final terms.
- **Food convoy first:** food can release the six prisoners on the spot. The danger is that public distribution lets Karrgan claim command of all porters; settle the goblin allotment before the first sack moves.

## Vargoth's bugbear cell

**Leader:** [Vargoth “the Welcomer”](../characters/faction-leaders.md#bugbear-chief--vargoth-the-welcomer) | **Entry:** [welcome passage](../locations/caves/h-entry-passage.md) | **Population and captives:** [Cave H sheet](../locations/caves/population-bugbears.md)

**Expanded playable branches:** [Bugbear Negotiations — The Welcome That Breaks](../scenarios/bugbear_negotiations/index.md)

### The apparent negotiation

Vargoth offers exactly what the party wishes the other factions had offered:

- food and a place to sit;
- assurances that his people understand trade;
- information about the other caves;
- an offer to mediate disputes or guard a convoy;
- willingness to discuss prisoners as laborers who can supposedly be bought free.

He asks names, home communities, party roles, languages, affiliations, which chief they trust, who would pay ransom, where the wagons will travel, and who sleeps on watch. Every question is intake.

### The real interaction challenge

The players are not expected to guess “bugbears are evil.” They are expected to notice that this specific welcome does not match the facts.

Offer multiple clues before the trap closes:

- the guards ask about ransom before asking about trade;
- the lair has good food and imported goods despite claiming the same desperation as its neighbors;
- interior doors lock from the guard side;
- muffled chains or captive voices come from below;
- welcome phrases are repeated like a practiced script;
- a guard tries to separate an unarmored, small, or visibly wealthy character;
- the offered terms never include Vargoth releasing anyone without payment that would fund another capture.

Danica can read the intake behavior and records. Stelian can distinguish guarded movement from household traffic. Brynja can identify the Eagles as plated lead. Tavin can notice that Vargoth's provisions are inconsistent with his claimed hunger.

### When the trap closes

Vargoth prefers to attack after the party has eaten, separated, surrendered weapons, or revealed its escape plan. If challenged early, he laughs off one inconsistency and redirects; after a second, guards shift toward exits; after a third, he orders the capture.

The staff breaks the first living attack and allows questions, but Vargoth uses the conversation to reposition and resumes violence when he sees an advantage. He answers plausibly rather than honestly. Indefinite conversation may hold him, but it cannot free the pens while Demetrios remains occupied.

### What counts as resolution

- free both pens and return the missing kobolds and hobgoblins;
- rescue Skarn and preserve him as a witness;
- seize the stock tally, Eagles, trade dates, and route marks;
- prevent Vargoth from escaping through area 36's secret route if possible;
- distinguish Vargoth's adult collaborators from the young and from Skarn;
- arrange custody, dispersal, or guarded surrender for noncombatants rather than treating the whole species as the cell.

Killing Vargoth without finding prisoners or records proves only that the party fought bugbears. It does not increase trust. Returning missing residents and exposing the threat to other communities does.

### How earlier choices change Cave H

- **Bugbears first:** Vargoth is relaxed and the welcome is at its most convincing. The party has no corroborating evidence, but he has also had no opportunity to tailor a frame to their allies.
- **Kobolds first:** Vargoth knows which faction to target. One stolen kobold token may already be in a courier packet; the three prisoners become leverage.
- **Hobgoblins first:** Vargoth presents himself as a safer, less demanding alternative to Karrgan. He may offer false information about the missing patrol to draw the party deeper.
- **Kobolds and hobgoblins first:** Vargoth knows the party threatens the system. The welcome remains, but guards are ready, records are packed for removal, and the framing operation is active.
- **Cult first:** the party may arrive with Vargoth's name, exact prisoner counts, and his sabotage plan. Vargoth abandons patient intake once shown that evidence. If a scheduled Shrine courier has failed to return, he doubles the pen guard and prepares the secret escape, but does not remove the prisoners from play.
- **Food convoy first:** Vargoth uses the gathering to count residents, map the supply route, and choose captives. If excluded, he claims persecution; if included, he performs cooperation while preparing an abduction. Either version should expose him rather than reward him with a lasting share.

## The Shrine and the cult of Thanatos

**Leader:** [Vesna](../characters/the-hierophant.md) | **Reachable cultist:** [Mara Sokol](../characters/mara-sokol.md) | **Forces and evidence route:** [Shrine population sheet](../locations/caves/population-cult.md)

**Expanded dungeon and interaction guide:** [The Shrine of Evil Chaos scenario set](../scenarios/cult_shrine/index.md)

### There is no faction bargain

The Shrine's objective is famine, division, and death. Food, territory, recognition, and security cannot satisfy it. The party can communicate with living cultists, accept surrender, save individuals, gather intelligence, and decide who legitimizes the assault; it cannot negotiate a peace that leaves the operation intact.

That distinction should emerge from conduct, not alignment labels:

- cave leaders can name material needs and reciprocal terms;
- Vargoth can name terms but means to betray them;
- Vesna turns every concession into another means of producing conflict.

### The meaningful social interactions

#### Mara — surrender and a way back

In [area 54](../locations/caves/54-acolytes-chamber.md), show her hesitation openly. A credible offer of protection plus proof that the undead destroyed cave food can make her lower her mace. She can identify Vargoth's couriers and authenticate the Shrine records, but she does not know the wider patron.

#### Vesna — confrontation, not persuasion

Vesna sincerely believes the cult serves Traladaran liberation. Let players expose contradictions and force Vesna to name the human cost. This may affect Mara and other witnesses, but Vesna chooses suicide-ascension rather than capture. The conversation determines what the climax means, not whether Vesna signs terms.

#### The torturer — operational testimony

If captured after Vesna falls, the area 61 torturer can confirm that Vargoth supplied captives and intelligence while the Shrine supplied warnings and undead harassment. He is a corroborating witness, not a repentant guide.

#### Lysandra — a separate gamble

The medusa in area 64 is a prisoner, not a cult faction. Negotiating through the door, using the guest-room mirror, and creating real mutual safety can change her response. Do not let her outcome stand in for the cult's.

### Legitimacy changes the victory

Bringing Father Branislav or another recognized Church of Traladar voice makes the assault a cleansing of a corruption rather than a ducal attack on Traladaran religion. Irina can recognize liturgical contradictions; Demetrios can identify evil and open living dialogue but cannot substitute for local Traladaran legitimacy.

### Preserve redundant evidence

The party need not find every clue:

- [area 60](../locations/caves/60-shrine-guest-chamber.md): physical proof of bugbear guests;
- [area 61](../locations/caves/61-torture-chamber.md): captive questioning and sabotage planning;
- [area 59](../locations/caves/59-vesnas-chambers.md#command-papers): Vargoth named with exact prisoner counts;
- [area 63](../locations/caves/63-shrine-storeroom.md): framing packets;
- Mara: testimony explaining what the cult believed.

If Vargoth or another survivor warns the Shrine, remove or burn **one** clue set and increase alertness. Never erase the entire trail; order should change difficulty, not invalidate investigation.

### How earlier choices change the Shrine

- **Cult first:** the party lacks cave allies but finds the cleanest evidence against Vargoth. This route turns the rest of the campaign into exposing a local conspiracy and returning missing residents.
- **Kobolds first:** Yiznak can report food losses, odd night tracks, and missing people. His trust gives the party a victim's account, but his warriors do not storm the Shrine.
- **Hobgoblins first:** Karrgan can provide disciplined observations and a map of where patrols disappeared. He still says the Shrine is “a human problem” and will not spend his force on it.
- **Bugbears first:** the pen tally, Skarn, and matching fittings make Shrine clues immediately intelligible. If Vargoth escaped, the temple is alerted and one evidence cache may be burned.
- **Several factions first:** leaders may provide scouts, guides to the ravine, warning watches, and refuge after the assault while declining direct combat. Their restraint reinforces that they are political communities, not the party's summoned army.
- **Food convoy first:** the Shrine has already revealed its priorities by attacking or observing the food. Undead destroyed during that scene may reduce one defensive wave; surviving cultists are more alert.

## Supporting factions and individuals

These contacts do not replace the four core resolutions, but they can change how difficult those resolutions are.

### Maruk's gnolls — the neutral witness

Maruk wants fair terms and peace. A direct food-and-safe-passage agreement is enough. If contacted early, he can witness later exchanges without trying to dominate them. Discovering the secret Shrine route adjoining [area 50](../locations/caves/50-maruks-chamber.md) turns him from neutral witness into an injured party with useful access—but he still decides whether to risk his people.

### Gorrak and Ushka — security and the feud

Ushka is the easier first contact. The party can exploit the feud for fast support or mediate a limited nonaggression agreement. Orc recognition gives the proposed peace armed weight independent of Karrgan. Backing one chief exclusively makes the other suspicious of every later faction-wide term.

### The goblin ward — the overlooked dependency

The goblins are not merely Karrgan's extra troops. Their food is being skimmed through the secret route between areas 21 and 28, and hunger has led them to accept human meat distributed by the hobgoblin bloc. They hold no separate human prisoners. Speaking to their headman and delivering food directly ends the immediate conduct and exposes whether the party is building a federation or replacing one hierarchy with another.

### Brundt — messenger, not diplomat

Brundt can carry invitations, vouch that the party pays, and tell them which entrances are dangerous. He cannot negotiate abstract obligations and will accept a second employer's money without understanding that this creates a conflict. Use him to open doors, never to settle terms.

### Khaeros — witness and moral test

Khaeros is not a faction chief. His safe conduct and treatment at Riverfork nevertheless affect every later negotiation. If the Duke's envoys can recognize a feared minotaur as a free traveler, their promises of legal standing become credible. His testimony also helps separate Vargoth's local slave cell from the larger Black Eagle route.

## Five workable orders

No route is the canonical route. Use the one the players create.

### 1. Kobolds first — diplomatic route

1. Recognize Yiznak's engineering and promise a direct food channel.
2. Use the first agreement as proof when approaching Karrgan, while resisting his claim to command it.
3. Vargoth attempts to frame the party against whichever group is more vulnerable.
4. Expose Cave H, return the six missing residents, and convert provisional trust into firm terms.
5. Follow famine evidence to the Shrine and end the cause.

**Tone:** hope first, conspiracy later. **Risk:** the first agreement gives Vargoth time to tailor sabotage.

### 2. Hobgoblins first — political route

1. Meet Karrgan as a peer-commander and make food-for-prisoners the pilot agreement.
2. Approach Yiznak separately so the peace does not become Karrgan's empire.
3. Investigate the missing patrol and Vargoth's false evidence.
4. Destroy the bugbear intake cell and return the missing residents.
5. Assault the Shrine with proof that famine caused the behavior Karrgan agreed to stop.

**Tone:** hard bargaining first. **Risk:** early concessions may over-empower Karrgan.

### 3. Bugbears first — rescue route

1. Accept the welcome, discover the trap, and fight out of Cave H.
2. Free the captives and seize records.
3. Return kobolds and hobgoblins to their leaders; begin both negotiations with living proof.
4. Use matching bugbear evidence to interpret the Shrine.
5. Establish food terms after removing the faction that would sabotage them.

**Tone:** apparent confirmation of every monster story followed by the prisoners disproving it. **Risk:** without records or rescued witnesses, other factions see only an unprovoked bugbear fight.

### 4. Cult first — investigation route

1. Track undead through the northern woods and strike the Shrine.
2. Save Mara and collect the Vargoth correspondence, prisoner counts, and framing packets.
3. Warn Karrgan and Yiznak that their missing residents are alive.
4. Enter Cave H with the trap exposed, free the captives, and return them.
5. Negotiate food and political terms after both sources of sabotage are gone.

**Tone:** mystery first, diplomacy with answers afterward. **Risk:** the party faces the Shrine without cave intelligence or established refuges.

### 5. Food first — public route

1. Secure provisional invitations without attempting final treaties.
2. Run the convoy as the first shared act.
3. Let distribution expose the goblin question, Karrgan's ambitions, Yiznak's status, and Vargoth's bad faith.
4. Follow sabotage or undead back to Cave H or the Shrine in whichever order the evidence indicates.
5. Return to each leader for permanent bilateral terms.

**Tone:** the entire political problem becomes visible at once. **Risk:** both hostile operations have a single public target.

## If the false incident occurs

Do not resolve it with “the chief attacks.” Run a short hearing:

1. The injured faction presents the planted evidence.
2. The party may inspect it, account for its own movements, question witnesses, or ask for time.
3. Give at least three inconsistencies pointing toward bugbear handling or Shrine preparation.
4. Success exposes Vargoth and improves trust.
5. Failure suspends the agreement until the party produces the missing residents, Shrine papers, Skarn, or bugbear records.

The sabotage is interesting because the party can solve it and because prior choices change which clues they recognize. It should never function as a cutscene that nullifies successful play.

## End-state checklist

Peace is viable when the party has achieved most of the following:

- Yiznak has direct standing, useful paid work, and a food route.
- Karrgan has exchanged every prisoner, ended captive-taking for food, and accepted a bounded military role.
- The goblin allotment and status have been addressed explicitly.
- Vargoth's cell is dismantled, its captives freed, and its evidence preserved.
- The Shrine's famine operation is destroyed and its Traladaran disguise publicly answered.
- At least one food delivery or bilateral supply arrangement has occurred.
- Valerian has witnesses and results sufficient to authorize a continuing bridge.

Unfinished secondary negotiations do not invalidate the ending. The achievable victory is a functioning ceasefire and food bridge with named parties, not a finished constitution for the ravine.
