# The Bandit Camp — "The Other Side of the Road"

*Not in the module — B2 only suggests bandits as a post-caves complication. Placed here deep in the woods across the road from the caves: a small camp of twelve misfits who can't rejoin lawful society, suffering the same manufactured famine the caves suffer, just as frightened of the nights.*

**Type:** outside mission / faction (human) · **Where:** deep woods, across the road from the [Caves of Chaos](caves-of-chaos.md) · **Hook:** the Castellan's vague reports of "raids by humans and monsters" — *these are the humans,* and [Valerian](../characters/castellan-valerian.md) knows little about them

> **Role in the campaign:** The last outside mission and the thesis-mirror — **humans doing exactly what the caves are blamed for, labeled "bandits" instead of "monsters."** Also a buried conspiracy node (some are ex-Barony; some lost the minotaur) and the human integration case: where do the desperate go when nobody catches them in time?

---

## The core idea

These are not the road's primary problem. They're a **relatively small group — twelve at most** — and they raid because they're **starving, exactly like the caves.** The [cult's undead destroy their food sources at night too](the-iron-ring.md); their nights are scary for the same reason the caves' are. They're a **collection of misfits banded together to survive** — some cast off by the [Black Eagle Barony](the-borderland.md), some from elsewhere, none able to live in normal society for various reasons.

They run the campaign's thesis **in reverse.** The party spent the caves learning the "monsters" were hungry people. Here are *humans* preying on the same road for the same reason — and nobody calls them monsters. They're "men who went wrong." Same crime, opposite label, purely by species. **The bandits are where the party stops being able to believe the danger was ever about *what* the predators were, and has to face that it was always about *why* — and the why is the same for monsters and men.**

**Placement:** deep in the woods, across the road from the caves. Far enough that the two outcast communities don't *cooperate* — they don't even know how alike they are — but close enough that they share the same famine, the same fear, and the same road to prey on.

---

## How to run the camp (before the people)

- **Triage, not a kill-box.** Like the caves, the camp sorts: the desperate (reachable — the federation's logic works on them), the ex-Barony (reachable *and* valuable, carrying grudges and knowledge), and the genuinely vicious (the control group — some can't be saved, and the party shouldn't pretend otherwise). The party runs the same sorting they ran in the caves, on their own kind, and finds it comes out the same way.
- **Conspiracy is buried, not announced.** Some clues link the camp to the Baron, the Iron Ring, and Khaeros's escape. The party can clear the camp without understanding any of it and have the connection detonate *later* (when [Father Branislav](../characters/the-curate.md) — or another authority — reads the iron-ring mark, or [Khaeros](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md) sees a piece of Barony gear and goes still). Don't front-load it.
- **Keep the primary job clean.** First and most important, the bandits are *just desperate people on the wrong side of a label.* The Barony/minotaur threads are the bonus underneath. Don't let it become "the minotaur-handler dungeon."
- **They're scared at night.** Play them as people under siege by the dark, not as confident predators. Their raids are desperate, not bold. A party that observes before attacking should see frightened, hungry people — which is the whole point.

## The Leader — Aldric Venn, "the Captain"

*Formerly a sergeant-at-arms in the Black Eagle Barony's service. The man who lost the minotaur.*

**The short version:** Aldric was a competent, mid-level soldier in von Hendriks's forces — not a true believer, not Iron Ring, just a professional who took the Baron's coin because it was the work available to a hard man with no better options. He was put in charge of the overland leg of a smuggling job: move a captured minotaur from the coast inland to the Baron. **The minotaur escaped on his watch.** Men died. Aldric knew exactly what failure meant under the Baron and the Iron Ring that runs his operations — *Hunted*, made an example of — so he didn't go back. He ran, gathered the other castoffs the borderland produces, and has kept twelve people alive in the woods through a famine by being disciplined, fair, and ruthless in roughly equal measure.

**Why he works as the leader:**

- **He's the human [Khaeros](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md) — the mirror image.** Both are men the Baron's machine chewed up; both fled inland and went to ground in the wilderness on opposite sides of the same road; both are surviving on competence and dread. The difference: Khaeros was the *cargo*, Aldric was the *handler.* They are the two ends of the same crime, and *neither knows the other is a few miles away.* Aldric lost the minotaur; the minotaur is in the caves; the party has met both. (See *The Minotaur Collision*, below.)
- **He's reachable, but it costs him everything.** Aldric isn't vicious. He's a practical man doing wrong because the alternative was death and his people would starve. The federation's logic *can* reach him — offer his band food, amnesty, honest work, a way to stop running — and he'd take it, because he's tired and he's responsible for twelve people. **But** turning means facing the Baron's reach, testifying to what he did and saw, and carrying the guilt of the men who died when the minotaur broke loose. He's a man who could be redeemed and who isn't sure he deserves to be.
- **He has the knowledge.** Aldric is the second human intelligence source on the conspiracy (with [Skarn the rebel bugbear](../characters/skarn-the-rebel-bugbear.md) and [Brundt](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md) as the cave-side sources). He knows the Baron's overland smuggling corridor, the cove where shipments land, how the Iron Ring handles "acquisitions," and — the thing that breaks the Duke's denial — that the Black Eagle traffics in *thinking creatures.* He drove the cart. He's testimony with a cart-driver's certainty. See [The Iron Ring](the-iron-ring.md).

**His tension, the thing that makes him interesting:** Aldric keeps his band alive by being *good at the bad thing.* He's a fair leader of a foul enterprise — he rations honestly, he protects his people, he's killed travelers to feed them and hated it. He's the proof that *competence and decency don't save you from circumstance* — a good sergeant in a bad cause, who ran from a worse cause, and is now running a slightly-less-bad cause because there's nothing else. The party offering him a *way out* is offering him the first non-terrible option he's had in years. Whether he can trust it — whether a man who served the Black Eagle believes the Duke's mercy is real — is the negotiation.

**What he wants:** to keep his people alive, and, somewhere under that, to stop. To not be hunted. To put the cart and the dead men down. He won't say it. A sharp party will see it.

## The Twelve

*Aldric plus eleven. Sorted into the three groups the camp triages into. Each is a sketch — a name, a reason they can't go home, and a hook.*

### Ex-Barony (the conspiracy thread — carry the grudges and the knowledge)

**1. Aldric Venn — "the Captain."** *(above)* The leader. Lost the minotaur. Has the knowledge that breaks the Duke's denial.

**2. Hesk** — Aldric's second, and the other survivor of the minotaur job. Where Aldric carries the failure as guilt, Hesk carries it as *fear* — he's certain the Iron Ring will find them, and he's right to be. Twitchy, watchful, the first to suggest moving camp or killing witnesses. The most dangerous to a parley because he'd rather flee or silence the party than risk exposure. *Hook: if Hesk panics, he forces the violent version of the encounter; if he's reassured, he breaks first and talks fastest, because terror makes a man cooperative.*

**3. Mother Coll** — older woman, was a cook/quartermaster in the Barony before she was cashiered (saw something, or stole to survive a bad winter). The camp's *keeper* — she rations the food, tends the sick, holds the band together as much as Aldric does. She's the one who'd *advocate* for taking the federation's deal, because she's tired and she remembers what a kitchen with full stores felt like. *Hook: the moral center of the camp; if the party reaches her, she reaches Aldric.*

**4. Tomil** — young, was a stablehand/cart-driver on the minotaur job, barely more than a boy. Didn't choose any of this; followed Aldric because Aldric was the only one who didn't abandon him when it went wrong. *Hook: the clearest redemption case — a kid who fell into this. The party saving Tomil is the federation's logic at its most legible. Also: he can be made to talk easily, out of naivety, if separated from the others.*

### From elsewhere (the camouflage — mostly just desperate)

**5. Brennan** — a failed tenant farmer, lost his land to debt and a bad season, drifted to banditry when his family scattered. Not a killer by temperament; does the raids and hates himself. The purest "circumstance, not nature" case — three years ago he was an honest man. *Hook: the human [freed-prisoner-with-nowhere-to-go](the-prisoners.md), one stage further along. Resettle him and you've proven the thesis on a human.*

**6. The Sisters — Wren and Pell** — two sisters who fled a situation they won't describe (an abusive household, a forced betrothal, a debt sold along with them). Inseparable, competent, fierce in defense of each other and no one else. They don't care about the Baron or the Ring; they care about not being sent back wherever they came from. *Hook: their loyalty is purely to each other — they'll take any deal that guarantees they stay together and stay gone. A lever and a humanizing pair.*

**7. Garrow** — a deserter from some lawful militia or a caravan guard who ran from a fight and can't show his face among honest men again. Ashamed, quiet, capable. Wants redemption he doesn't believe he can have. *Hook: a chance at honest soldiering (caravan guard for the federation's trade route) is exactly the second chance he's been unable to ask for.*

**8. Old Pater** — too old to be doing this, a former petty criminal/con man who's run out of cities to be run out of. Survives on wit and the camp's tolerance. Knows a great deal of borderland gossip and rumor — including, possibly, things about the *caves* and the *cult* he's pieced together. *Hook: a minor information source in his own right, and a tonal note — the bandit who's just an old man with nowhere left to go.*

### The vicious (the control group — some can't be saved)

**9. Korsk** — genuinely violent, the man who'd rob the road in any economy. Joined for license, not survival. Chafes under Aldric's discipline and fairness — he'd rather the camp be crueler, take more, kill more. The internal threat to Aldric's authority. *Hook: the [human "Hero"](the-prisoners.md) equivalent — vicious by choice, not circumstance. If the camp fractures, Korsk is the fracture. He may force a confrontation Aldric was trying to avoid, or split off with the worst of the band.*

**10. Vash** — Korsk's follower, a younger man being *made* vicious by the company he keeps — not yet irredeemable but heading there fast. *Hook: the fork made flesh — Vash could go either way, and which way depends on whether Korsk or Aldric (or the party) gets to him. The campaign's "[almost-corrupted recruit](../characters/the-hierophant.md)" theme, in human form.*

**11. The Quiet One** — nobody knows much about them; keeps apart, kills efficiently, never explains. Possibly Iron Ring — an actual sworn member who *attached* to this band as cover, or who's watching it, rather than a castoff like the rest. *Hook: the deepest-in, the one who might carry the actual iron-ring sigil. If the camp has a true conspiracy connection beyond Aldric's lost-job knowledge, it's the Quiet One — a planted or hiding Ring member among genuine misfits. Optional: pull this thread if you want the camp actively tied to the Ring rather than just sediment from it.*

**12. (open slot)** — left deliberately empty for a player connection, a recurring NPC, or whoever the table needs. A bandit who recognizes a PC, a former associate of a freed prisoner, a face from the Keep. Fill it at the table.

## The Minotaur Collision

The campaign's sharpest coincidence, to deploy at whatever heat you want:

**Aldric (and Hesk and Tomil) lost [Khaeros](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md).** The minotaur escaped *their* cart on the overland leg. They got the blame, fled the Baron, and have been surviving across the road ever since. **The men who lost the minotaur and the minotaur himself are a few miles apart, on opposite sides of the same road, and neither knows it.**

- **Cold version:** the party clears the camp, and only *afterward* — from documents or a captured bandit — realizes these were Khaeros's lost handlers. Evidence connects the camp to the Baron *and* to the minotaur they've already met. The party understands they've been standing at both ends of one crime.
- **Hot version:** the bandits *know* there's an escaped minotaur loose in the caves. Some still fear him; some might want to *recapture* him to buy back their lives with the Baron or the Ring. The camp becomes an *active threat to Khaeros* — the one loose end of his escape, the men with the most reason to want him taken. **Befriending Khaeros and clearing the bandits become connected protective acts:** you can't fully secure the minotaur while the men who lost him are still out there, desperate.

Either way, the camp **closes Khaeros's backstory into the present tense.** The cart had a driver. The driver couldn't go home. He found other people who couldn't go home. *That's a bandit camp — and it's also, exactly, the [Caves of Chaos](caves-of-chaos.md).*

## Clues to seed (physical, partial)

- **Barony gear** — equipment, a badge, livery, a brand that *some* bandits carry (the ex-Barony ones) and others don't, visually sorting the camp.
- **The iron-ring sigil** — on one or two of the deepest-in (the Quiet One; maybe Hesk). The same mark that's in the temple and the bugbear slave-station, so the [three nodes start to rhyme](the-iron-ring.md).
- **Orders / correspondence** referencing a shipment, an "acquisition," "the beast," or a failed delivery — manifest-level evidence tying the camp to Khaeros's route and the Baron's corridor.
- **A spoken grudge** — Aldric, Hesk, or Mother Coll, parleyed with or captured, *talks*, because they hate the man who'd have them killed for one failure. The willing witness.

As with every evidence-cache in the campaign: the party can carry these out without understanding them, and let the connection land later.

## What the camp proves (the thesis, one last time)

- **The road's danger was never a species problem.** Humans prey on the road for the same reason the caves do — hunger and opportunity. The "monster raids" and the "bandit raids" are the same phenomenon with different faces. You can't fix the road by clearing the caves, because the caves were never the only thing on it.
- **The famine is everyone's.** The bandits starve under the same manufactured scarcity, fear the same undead nights. Thanatos's harvest doesn't check species either. The cult is bleeding *humans* on this side of the road too — which means the bandits are *also* an applicant pool for the borderland's organized evil (the Iron Ring recruits exactly these men: brutal, unattached, desperate). Leave them unhelped and the Ring gets them, the way the famine gets the caves.
- **Displacement curdles in stages.** The [freed prisoners with nowhere to go](the-prisoners.md) and the bandits are the *same population at two stages* — displacement that hasn't yet curdled, and displacement that already has. A freed prisoner the federation fails to resettle *becomes* a bandit. A bandit reached in time *becomes* a resettled man. The cure is the same: catch the desperate before the organized evil does.
- **From the victim's side, the predator's species never mattered.** To a merchant on the road, a bandit's blade and a hobgoblin's are the same threat. The Guild doesn't distinguish. It was always just the road being dangerous, for the oldest reason there is.

---

*Entirely campaign invention — B2 only suggests bandits exist. Built as the human mirror of the caves: same famine, same fear, same outcast logic, with a buried thread to the Baron, the Iron Ring, and the minotaur's escape.*
