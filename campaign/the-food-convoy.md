# The Food Convoy — Emergency Relief at the Ravine

*An optional playable scenario in which the Keep puts an enormous amount of food on wagons, takes it to the Caves of Chaos, and asks enemies to stand together long enough to receive it.*

**Use:** before the Shrine as a dangerous proof of concept, after the Shrine as the public payoff of the peace, or not at all · **Primary locations:** [Riverfork's warehouse](../locations/keep/05-common-warehouse.md), the road, and the [Ravine Floor](../locations/caves/ravine.md) · **Key NPCs:** [Valerian](../characters/castellan-valerian.md), [Mihail Radu](../locations/keep/16-guild-house.md), [Brundt](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md), and the [cave chiefs](../characters/faction-leaders.md)

> **What this scenario accomplishes:** it interrupts the immediate famine, demonstrates that the Keep and caves can cooperate, and creates the first piece of a food bridge. It does **not** create a permanent market, settle the factions' politics, eliminate the cult, or guarantee that anyone remains peaceful after the food is gone.

---

## When to use it

The convoy is never required. It becomes possible when the party has enough of the following:

- peaceful contact with several cave leaders;
- evidence that hunger is driving at least some raids;
- Valerian's grudging confidence or direct ducal authority;
- Brundt or another trusted broker able to carry invitations;
- a defensible plan for distribution rather than “open the wagons and hope.”

Its meaning changes with its timing:

| Timing | What the convoy becomes |
|---|---|
| **Before the Shrine is found** | A vulnerable relief effort and a possible way to discover that someone is deliberately destroying food. |
| **After the Shrine is found but before it falls** | A public challenge to the cult's famine strategy; undead or cult agents have strong reason to sabotage it. |
| **After the Shrine falls** | A victory scene and emergency bridge while hunting, stores, and ordinary trade recover. |
| **Never attempted** | No failure of the campaign. Use smaller faction deliveries, cave envoys at the Keep, food-for-patrol wages, or a neutral market instead. |

## What is on the wagons

The default is **six heavy wagons** carrying roughly one week of emergency food for the cooperating communities, counted in human-sized ration equivalents rather than exact biological arithmetic. Scale the number of wagons to the factions participating.

Use durable, divisible foods the caves can actually store and cook:

- meal, barley, and oats;
- dried peas and beans;
- hard bread and hard cheese;
- salt pork and dried fish;
- onions, roots, salt, and cooking oil;
- spare sacks, cord, and a small reserve held back for counting errors.

This is diverted and purchased from regular ducal and private traffic; Riverfork itself is not stripped or placed on starvation rations. [Mihail Radu](../locations/keep/16-guild-house.md) arranges ownership and manifests. **Vasile Neagu** supplies carts, **Julia Neagu** reads the hauling terms, **Stoyan Neagu** drives, and **Sorina Petrov** keeps the distribution ledger. [Captain Bohdan](../characters/captain-bohdan.md) can command a restrained escort of about twelve soldiers while riders watch the road outside the ravine.

The characters are not merely wagon guards in their own scenario. Their jobs are to secure invitations, design the peace, inspect the cargo, read the factions, and prevent one bad moment from becoming a battle.

## Getting authorization

Valerian does not object because Riverfork lacks food. He objects because sending six wagons into hostile ground risks teamsters, soldiers, draft animals, and the credibility of ducal authority.

He wants:

1. named leaders who have accepted the delivery;
2. a temporary cessation of attacks on the road and convoy;
3. a neutral distribution site on the ravine floor;
4. a plan for recovering the wagons and animals;
5. someone accountable if a faction seizes more than its share.

Mihail asks different questions: who owns the food, who pays the carters, whether this is charity or the opening of a Guild-controlled market, and whether future deliveries will owe dues. He can approve one emergency experiment locally. He will not concede a permanent trade regime without reporting to Anton Radu.

The party can frame the delivery to Valerian as **advance payroll**: food in return for a ceasefire, safe road, convoy protection, the return of prisoners, or later patrol work. It can also simply be emergency relief under the Duke's commission. The table should decide which promise the food represents.

## Invitations and the peace of the wagons

Brundt is the natural messenger, but the party should visit any leader whose trust is uncertain. Each invited community receives a marked tally-stick showing its assigned load and arrival time. No one must trust a human clerk's writing.

Suggested terms:

- The wagons enter in daylight and leave before dusk.
- Everyone may come out to witness the delivery, including families and young, but only named porters approach the carts.
- Weapons may be carried but remain sheathed or lowered inside the marked wagon ground.
- Each chief is responsible for their own people and porters.
- No faction crosses another cave's path or carries food through another faction's entrance without permission.
- Violence ends the delivery; it does not automatically give the remaining food to whichever side wins.

If trust is high, the ravine fills with residents who have previously existed only as room populations: children, elders, cooks, wounded guards, laborers, and armed adults trying not to look frightened. This is the scene's most important image. The garrison sees communities rather than silhouettes at cave mouths, while the cave residents see soldiers standing beside food without taking prisoners.

It is also a frightening intelligence exchange. Everyone can estimate everyone else's numbers and dependents. Chiefs who are unwilling to expose their households send porters instead; do not force the mass gathering merely because it would be picturesque.

## Who receives food

The party is deciding which groups it recognizes as communities, not merely dividing sacks.

| Group | Likely behavior at the wagons |
|---|---|
| **Yiznak's kobolds** | Arrive early with many small baskets and visible young. Yiznak notices immediately whether his people are served as participants or leftovers. |
| **Gorrak's orcs** | Come armed and suspicious of poison. Gorrak refuses any order that visibly places Ushka over him. |
| **Ushka's orcs** | Watch the process before committing. Ushka accepts a rotating or drawn distribution order if it prevents Gorrak claiming precedence. |
| **The goblin ward** | Waits to see whether its allotment goes directly to goblin porters or through Karrgan. That choice either recognizes goblin autonomy or strengthens hobgoblin control. |
| **Karrgan's hobgoblins** | Arrive in disciplined columns. Karrgan offers to impose order on everyone and expects control over the goblin share unless stopped diplomatically. |
| **Maruk's gnolls** | The easiest participants. Maruk will take a late turn if his people's sealed allotment is visibly protected. |
| **Brundt** | Serves as broker, enthusiastic food inspector, and a porter strong enough to move a disputed sack without drawing a weapon. |
| **Khaeros** | Watches the treatment of dependents and prisoners more closely than the speeches. He accepts food, trade, or wages but does not need charity. |
| **Vargoth's bugbear cell** | Not a good-faith participant. If still active, it uses the gathering to count rivals, identify valuable captives, and locate the supply route. |
| **The Shrine** | Receives nothing. Its project is famine, not survival. Any representative is reconnaissance or sabotage. |

The lizardfolk are self-sufficient in their own territory and are not part of the caves' emergency ration count. They may attend as recognized traders or observers if the party has opened that relationship.

## The distribution problem

Do not make the players calculate hundreds of meals at the table. The Guild has pre-marked faction loads based on the party's best population estimates, with a ten-percent reserve for errors.

The live decisions are political:

- **Equal factions or equal mouths?** Equal wagon shares privilege small groups; per-capita shares require leaders to reveal population.
- **Need or status?** Serving the visibly starving first is humane but publicly identifies weakness.
- **Who receives the goblins' food?** Direct delivery limits Karrgan; delivery through him formalizes his rule.
- **What buys the hobgoblin share?** If their prisoner-eating is subsistence, food can be exchanged for all six captives immediately. If it is cultural, the wagon reveals rather than solves the disagreement.
- **What happens to the reserve?** Giving it to the strongest faction rewards pressure. Dividing it too early leaves no answer to a genuine counting error.

A defensible default is one agreed ration-equivalent per resident, adjusted openly for size, with each community responsible for internal distribution. Draw lots for order, keep the reserve sealed until the end, and deliver the goblin allotment directly unless the goblins themselves request otherwise.

## Running the gathering

1. **Arrival:** Bohdan halts the escort before it resembles a battle line. The party and Brundt bring the first cart onto the ravine floor.
2. **Proof:** Open sacks at random. Let cave representatives smell, taste, or inspect them. A cleric may bless or purify suspect food, but trust should not depend entirely on magic.
3. **Terms:** Repeat the wagon peace in every needed language. The Concordant Staff can stop a living escalation and open dialogue; it does nothing to undead attackers.
4. **First allotment:** The party's choice of first recipient announces the political order they are building. Drawing lots avoids precedence but does not avoid the goblin question.
5. **Distribution:** Porters move marked loads while the party watches for fear, tampering, missing sacks, and chiefs using hunger to establish dominance.
6. **The reserve:** Resolve shortages, disputed household counts, and any prisoner exchange before opening it.
7. **Departure:** Obtain each chief's mark in Sorina's ledger. Agree what happens next before the empty wagons leave.

## Choose one major complication

The politics are sufficient conflict by themselves. Add at most one major complication unless this scenario is replacing another set piece.

### Undead attack — use before the Shrine falls

Tracks or a scout warn that undead are converging on the food. If the party misses the warning, they attack the reserve cart or draft animals near dusk. Use a manageable patrol drawn from the Shrine's controlled undead rather than the entire temple force.

The staff cannot stop them. Cave warriors and human soldiers must decide, in public, whether to defend one another. Capturing or following the route back toward the Shrine turns the relief effort into a strong clue to the manufactured famine.

### Ariston's betrayal — use if he remains embedded

[Father Ariston](../characters/father-ariston-vale.md) offers religious reassurance and volunteers Corin and Sera around the wagons. His preferred result is not merely burned food; it is humans and cave residents each believing the other betrayed the truce.

His attendants can cut a harness, plant a slow fire in the reserve, or place evidence pointing toward one of the orc camps. Ariston then urges frightened soldiers to interpret confusion as attack. Alert guards, examination of the cart, inconsistencies in his account, or someone watching the food rather than the crowd can expose him.

### Vargoth exploits the gathering — use before the bugbear cell falls

Vargoth feigns participation, gathers intelligence, and looks for an isolated teamster, clerk, or young cave resident to seize after the convoy separates. He may provoke a dispute over exclusion and attack only once the factions blame one another.

Food cannot purchase his reform. The useful outcome is exposing the difference between hungry neighbors and an organized slaving operation.

### No villain at all — use for a political scene

An axle breaks, one faction's count is badly wrong, or frightened porters reach for the same sack. Nothing is secretly causing it. The challenge is whether the new peace survives an ordinary mistake without inventing an enemy.

## Ways characters can matter

- **Languages** let the party state identical terms rather than allowing a convenient mistranslation to become policy.
- **Tracking and scouting** can find undead approach routes, hidden bugbear observers, or tampered wagon tracks.
- **Thief skills and close inspection** can detect cut harness, false seals, missing sacks, or a slow-burning sabotage device.
- **Fighters and dwarves** can lay out a defensible but nonthreatening wagon ground, organize porters, and repair a failed cart.
- **Clerical magic** can reassure participants about food, treat crush injuries, and interrupt panic. Turning matters if undead arrive.
- **The Concordant Staff** can break a living attack long enough for everyone to hear what actually happened. It cannot pacify the undead.
- **Reputation** matters more than a single reaction roll. Chiefs remember which promises the party has already kept.

## Outcomes

### Strong success

- Every recognized community receives its marked share.
- No faction captures the process or another faction's allotment.
- Prisoners are released if the hobgoblin issue is truly subsistence-driven.
- The carts, teamsters, and escort return safely.
- The chiefs agree to a short ceasefire and a follow-up arrangement.

Road raids driven purely by hunger stop for roughly the life of the delivery. Valerian now has the result he needs to authorize a recurring bridge, and Mihail has evidence that a future market could be profitable.

### Partial success

Most food arrives, but one faction is excluded, humiliated, or strengthened over the others. The immediate famine eases while the political imbalance becomes the next problem. This is often more useful than a perfect ending.

### Failure

The food is seized, burned, poisoned, or abandoned after violence. Failure does not prove peace impossible. It identifies who could not tolerate the experiment and leaves smaller bilateral deliveries as a safer alternative.

## What comes after

One convoy buys days, not a settlement. Before the food is eaten, the party needs at least the beginning of one of these:

- scheduled smaller deliveries paid for in coin or trade goods;
- food wages for road patrols, escorts, quarrying, engineering, or other agreed work;
- cave representatives admitted to buy at Riverfork under escort;
- a neutral periodic market outside either side's walls;
- restored hunting and stores after the Shrine's undead stop destroying them;
- Guild contracts connecting cave products and labor to Karameikan buyers.

The convoy answers **“Can we feed one another without fighting?”** It does not answer **“What political and economic relationship will exist next year?”** That larger question remains the work of the food bridge and the peace.
