# The Minotaur at the Keep — Hiring Khaeros

*The payoff of winning [Khaeros](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md): he wants out of the cave and into a life — as a **soldier.** The party has to convince a frontier commander to take a resident, armed minotaur onto the watch. Unlike [the ogre](the-ogre-goes-to-the-keep.md), this one will take the job.*

**Follows:** [First Contact — The Minotaur](first-contact-the-minotaur.md) · **Plays at:** the Keep · **Key NPCs:** [Khaeros](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md), [Castellan Valerian](../characters/castellan-valerian.md), [Captain Bohdan](../characters/captain-bohdan.md)

> **The point of this scene:** Brundt came to *shop* and went home. Khaeros wants to **stay.** He's asking for a post on the guard — pay, a place, work he chose. It's the [freeman deal](the-keep-arrival-and-hub.md) made concrete, and the hardest version of it: not "let a monster buy bread for an afternoon," but "let a minotaur stand armed on your wall, indefinitely." The party has to sell Valerian on it — and Valerian moves on *results,* not sentiment.

---

## The ask (what Khaeros wants — and why it's different)

Once he's reached (he ate), Khaeros doesn't want an escort to the market. He wants a **job and a place.** Specifically:

- **A soldier's post.** He's a trained fighter (the empire made him one before it made him cargo), and unlike Brundt he will *hold a line, take orders, stand a watch.* Soldiering is his trade. The difference from his whole prior life is the only one that matters to him: **this is fighting he can quit.** The war-thrall, finally paid — and free to walk away. That freedom *is* the dignity; it's the exact inverse of the chain.
- **Honest wages, not the hoard.** He has passage money — a chest of it — and he *won't spend it.* The slave-fare stays untouched, a monument: *the proof he stayed free.* He'd rather earn a clean guard's wage than draw down the money that came out of bondage. (So the job isn't really about the coin. It's about being a *person with a role.*)
- **He speaks their language.** Khaeros speaks **Thyatian** and carries himself with a civilized register — he doesn't need [Demetrios's staff](../characters/father-demetrios-kallistos.md) to be understood at the Keep the way the cave-dwellers do. He can stand in front of Valerian and *make his own case,* smart and measured. That alone moves him from "monster the party vouches for" toward "applicant."
- **The open horizon:** does he earn his passage and go home, or stay forever? *Out of this campaign's scope* — like the trade relations, it's a generational question. Tonight he just wants in.

---

## Why this is a harder sell than the ogre

| | Brundt | Khaeros |
|---|---|---|
| Wanted | a day's shopping, then home | a *post*, to stay, armed |
| Combat | won't soldier (guide/broker only) | **a trained soldier who will** |
| Speech | bad Common | fluent **Thyatian**, civilized |
| The risk to Valerian | a vouched, escorted afternoon | a resident minotaur on the wall, indefinitely |

Brundt's visit was reversible — he left at dusk. Khaeros is asking Valerian to take *permanent* responsibility for an armed, immensely dangerous creature living among frightened, grieving people. The upside is real, though, and it's the party's lever: a **frontier keep is always short of good soldiers**, and Khaeros is a disciplined HD-6 fighter who *wants honest work.* To a commander counting spears on a dangerous border, that's not charity — it's a windfall he has every reason to distrust.

---

## The negotiation with Valerian

This is another **persuade-by-results** room (see [Valerian](../characters/castellan-valerian.md)), harder than the ogre's. His objections are, again, the *reasonable* ones:

- *"A minotaur. With a weapon. Living inside my walls. Tell me why that ends any way but badly."*
- *"My men buried friends killed out of those caves. Now I'm to pay one of them a soldier's wage and arm him?"*
- *"What happens the first time a drunk in the tavern calls him what he is? Who dies?"*
- *"You vouched for the ogre for an afternoon. This is forever. Are you vouching for* forever*?"*

**What wins the room:**

- **Let Khaeros speak for himself.** His single biggest advantage over Brundt: he can stand there, in Thyatian, disciplined and articulate, and *be* the argument. A commander who expected a beast and meets a soldier has already half-lost the objection.
- **Frame it as garrison strength, not mercy.** Don't argue Khaeros *deserves* it (Valerian doesn't care). Argue the Keep is undermanned, the border is bleeding, and here is a trained fighter who'll hold a post for wages. That's the soldier's logic he respects.
- **Offer a trial, not a tenure.** Valerian won't grant "forever" on day one, and shouldn't. **Probation** is the natural landing: a watch under supervision, a patrol, a proving period. Khaeros — who understands earning his place — will accept it without offense; a free soldier expects to be tested.
- **Take responsibility, but share it.** The party vouches, but the smart move is to make Khaeros *Valerian's* asset, under *Valerian's* command — so the Castellan owns the win if it works, and isn't just doing the party a favor.

**The result:** hired, almost certainly **on trial,** and placed under **[Captain Bohdan](../characters/captain-bohdan.md)** — which is its own scene (below). The freeman rung of the [citizenship ladder](the-keep-arrival-and-hub.md), realized in one wary handshake.

## Khaeros and Bohdan — the two big soldiers

Putting Khaeros on the watch puts him under **[Captain Bohdan](../characters/captain-bohdan.md)**, and the pairing is a gift:

- Bohdan is the **biggest human** in the Keep, built on a *presence* — and Khaeros dwarfs him, the way Brundt did. The captain meets, again, the limit of size as authority.
- But Bohdan already *warmed to a monster once* (Brundt's market walk), and he, of anyone, knows what it is to be **read as a body before a person.** He's the likeliest in the whole garrison to take the minotaur as a *soldier* rather than a threat.
- Two trained fighters, both defined their whole lives by their bodies, sizing each other up — start it as wary, competitive friction (a sparring match, a contest of strength), and let it resolve into **professional respect.** Soldiers recognize soldiers. Bohdan vouching for Khaeros to the wary garrison is the human side of the same vouching the party did — and the fastest way Khaeros becomes *one of the watch* instead of *the minotaur they tolerate.*

## The labyrinth (a personal-quest seed)

*Minotaurs run labyrinths.* It's worth holding as Khaeros's own thread, undeveloped on purpose:

- Maybe his **way home** is a maze — the corridor back to Thyatis, the tangle of the Iron Ring's routes, a thing only a creature with a minotaur's instinct for paths can thread.
- Maybe there's a **literal labyrinth** somewhere in the borderland or the deeper Mystaran world — an old ruin, a Thyatian relic, something that *calls* to him — that becomes his personal quest if the campaign ever reaches for one.
- Or it's purely **metaphor**: Khaeros, the minotaur, has spent years lost in the maze of *whether he's a person or a monster, whether to go or stay* — and the campaign is him finding the thread out.

Pick the register you want when you want it. For now it's a resonance to keep in your pocket: the minotaur and the maze, his to solve.

## DM notes (player challenges & opportunities)

- **The hardest "yes" the Keep can give.** Brundt's afternoon was a demo; hiring Khaeros is the Keep *committing.* If the party pulls it off, they've moved Valerian from *permitting* an experiment to *staffing* his garrison with the federation's logic — a major step on the [political track](../characters/castellan-valerian.md).
- **Khaeros is his own best advocate — use that.** The scene rewards the party for *getting out of his way:* the more they let the articulate, disciplined minotaur make his own case, the better it goes. A party that talks *over* him reduces him to an asset again — exactly the thing he's escaping.
- **The garrison and town are the live problem.** Valerian's "yes" isn't the end; the grieving, frightened people are. Seed friction — a soldier who won't serve beside him, a tavern incident waiting to happen — and let Khaeros's *restraint* (and Bohdan's backing) earn his place over time. This is where the trial period pays off as drama.
- **Fighting he can quit is the emotional core — don't lose it.** Every beat should remember: this is a former slave choosing to take up arms *for wages he can refuse.* The day he could walk away and stays anyway is the day he's free. Let that be the quiet triumph under the negotiation.
