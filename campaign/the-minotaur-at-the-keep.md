# Khaeros at the Keep — Safe Conduct

*The payoff of winning [Khaeros](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md): the Duchy proves that he is free not by finding a use for him, but by allowing him to choose where he goes next.*

**Follows:** [First Contact — The Minotaur](first-contact-the-minotaur.md) · **Plays at:** Riverfork Keep · **Key NPCs:** [Khaeros](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md), [Castellan Valerian](../characters/castellan-valerian.md), [Captain Bohdan](../characters/captain-bohdan.md), and [Mihail Radu](../locations/keep/16-guild-house.md)

> **The point of this scene:** Brundt came to shop and went home. Khaeros comes to ask whether “home” is still a choice. His first request is not a wage, a barracks bed, or permission to settle. It is: **“If I choose to go, will your soldiers let me pass?”**

---

## What Khaeros asks for

Khaeros has enough treasure to pay for travel. Money is not his obstacle. He lacks orientation, recognized legal status, and any reason to trust the people who would carry him.

He therefore asks Valerian for three things:

- **A map and an honest explanation of where he is.** He wants the roads, borders, ports, and route to Thyatis explained without someone choosing the route for him.
- **Recognition as a free person.** He is not escaped property, an ownerless beast, or cargo awaiting a claimant.
- **Safe passage through Karameikos.** Soldiers, toll keepers, and patrols must not attack or detain him merely for being a minotaur traveling armed.

This is a harder and cleaner test than hiring him. If the Keep only accepts Khaeros when his strength becomes useful, it has offered a kinder form of ownership. If it gives him the means to leave, any later decision to stay is real.

## The Writ of Peace and Passage

Valerian can issue a provisional military safe-conduct covering Riverfork, its patrol roads, and Khaeros's journey to the next ducal authority. He forwards Khaeros's testimony and a request for confirmation to Duke Stefan. The Duke's ratified writ can protect Khaeros throughout Karameikos, though it cannot bind officials once he crosses into Thyatis.

Use wording like:

> **Khaeros of Riverfork is recognized as a free person under the Duke's peace. He may travel armed upon the roads of Karameikos. He is neither property nor outlaw and shall not be detained without lawful cause. Aid rendered to him under this writ is service to the Grand Duchy.**

The document is written in Thyatian and Traladaran, bears Valerian's seal, describes Khaeros without reducing him to the description, and remains in **Khaeros's possession**. A sealed badge or lead token matching the writ lets a patrol recognize it quickly.

### The bureaucratic trap

Someone may initially draft language granting permission to “transport one minotaur.” Khaeros recognizes the insult immediately. A player who catches it before he must object has understood the whole problem. The corrected writ names **Khaeros**, calls him a **person**, and authorizes **his travel** rather than somebody else's custody of him.

## The three honest choices

Valerian and the Keep can place three offers before him. None is a false ending.

### 1. Guard a Guild caravan — the likely first choice

[Mihail Radu](../locations/keep/16-guild-house.md) can offer Khaeros a written contract guarding the next Merchants' Guild caravan to Specularum. Khaeros travels openly and under his own power rather than inside a wagon or ship's hold.

The contract must provide:

- ordinary wages appropriate to an exceptionally capable guard;
- food and camp space, not confinement;
- Khaeros's right to retain his writ and personal property;
- clear authority during an attack but no ownership outside that duty;
- an end date at Specularum and an explicit right to refuse another contract.

This arrangement gives Khaeros what his treasure cannot buy: recognized companions, accountable witnesses, a route to the sea, and a commercial organization whose reputation depends on delivering him safely. His presence also makes the caravan formidable security. Mihail may see profit before justice, but a sound contract does not require pure motives.

### 2. Take a probationary garrison post

Valerian may offer a trial place under [Captain Bohdan](../characters/captain-bohdan.md). This remains attractive: Khaeros is a trained soldier, speaks Thyatian, and may want fighting he can quit rather than fighting imposed on him.

It is now an **option**, not the scene's prescribed result. He may accept immediately, return to it after seeing the Duchy, or decline without losing his writ. If he does enlist, the terms are wages, a defined trial, ordinary military discipline, and the same right to resign that belongs to any free soldier.

### 3. Return to Thyatis

Khaeros can use his own wealth to buy passage. The Guild can arrange introductions to a reputable captain, and Duke Stefan's Thyatian connections can eventually provide a letter of introduction on the far shore.

He will not accept transport as freight. A captain who demands that he be chained, locked below deck, or entered on a manifest as cargo ends the negotiation. Khaeros may instead buy passenger terms, insist on deck access, charter a vessel, or continue overland as far as possible before choosing a ship.

If he leaves Karameikos, that is a successful ending. The peace did not fail because the person it freed chose another home.

## Running the scene

1. **Khaeros enters under escort but speaks for himself.** He is articulate in Thyatian and does not need the party to translate his wishes.
2. **He gives Valerian evidence.** His route and captivity turn scattered Black Eagle incidents into the beginning of an official investigation.
3. **Valerian expects the question to be employment.** Khaeros instead asks whether the army will let him leave.
4. **Draft the writ.** Let the players shape or inspect its terms. Use the bureaucratic trap if the table benefits from one sharp test.
5. **Present the offers.** Bohdan can speak for the garrison; Mihail speaks for the Guild. Neither speaks *for Khaeros*.
6. **Khaeros chooses.** Unless play has given him a strong reason otherwise, he accepts one caravan contract to Specularum before making a lifetime decision.

## The likely immediate result

Khaeros leaves Riverfork with the next guarded Guild caravan. He walks beside it with his spear, his writ, and as much of his wealth as he chooses to carry. He is a contracted guard—not a passenger under restraint and not cargo.

At Specularum he can see the sea, investigate passage to Thyatis, report his captivity to higher ducal officials, and judge Karameikos beyond one fortress. He does not have to decide his entire life during this campaign.

The most likely long-term course is that he remains in Karameikos for now as a well-paid independent caravan guard. The Duchy is culturally familiar—Thyatian language, law, military custom, and commerce—while Stefan's new policy offers a legal identity that his captors denied him. Riverfork and the caves can remain his home base without becoming another enclosure.

## Long-term outcomes

- **Caravan freeman:** Khaeros works selected Guild routes, accumulates contacts, and periodically returns to Riverfork. He can become the dependable security link between the Keep, Specularum, and the caves' emerging trade.
- **Bicultural resident:** He keeps and improves his cavern, works intermittent contracts, and remains free to travel. The passage money may eventually become a home, a business stake, or an actual fare.
- **Riverfork soldier:** After seeing more of the Duchy, he chooses Bohdan's offer and takes a paid post he remains free to resign.
- **Return to Thyatis:** He selects the captain and terms himself and goes home. Whether he later returns is his decision.

Do not make him the party's permanent heavy. His strength can serve a caravan, a garrison, or nobody at all. The campaign has succeeded when those are genuinely his choices.

## Khaeros and Bohdan

Bohdan still matters even if Khaeros chooses the caravan. He is the first soldier who must treat the writ as real, the officer who briefs the gate and patrols, and the professional fighter most likely to recognize another professional fighter.

If Khaeros joins the garrison later, their wary sizing-up can become professional respect. If he does not, Bohdan's uncomplicated salute as the caravan departs may be the stronger scene: the captain is not releasing a prisoner or dismissing a subordinate. He is acknowledging a free traveler.

## DM notes

- **Freedom is the right to leave.** Keep this as the scene's emotional core.
- **The writ creates precedent.** Valerian's local order is practical; Stefan's ratification makes Khaeros the first unmistakable test of whether the caves policy has legal substance.
- **Paper does not erase prejudice.** Frightened civilians, suspicious patrols, and hostile officials remain possible, but they must now decide whether to defy the Duke's peace.
- **The Guild is useful and interested.** Mihail gains exceptional security and political access to the caves' new trade. Khaeros gains a route and institutional witnesses. Let both understand the bargain.
- **His wealth creates agency.** Do not force him to preserve the hoard as a symbol. He may keep it, spend it, invest it, or use it to buy unusually strict passage terms.
