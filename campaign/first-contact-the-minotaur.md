# First Contact — The Minotaur

*[Brundt](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md) brokers the party's meeting with [Khaeros](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md). The hardest negotiation in the caves, approached the only way it can survive: behind the one creature the minotaur trusts. Boxed text is read-aloud; everything else is DM guidance.*

**Part of:** [beat 4 — Walking the Caves](caves-of-chaos.md) · **Requires:** [Brundt](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md) as intermediary (see [his briefing](brundts-briefing.md)) · **Key NPCs:** [Khaeros](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md), [Brundt](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md)

> **Trigger:** The party asks Brundt to take them to Khaeros, after [the briefing](brundts-briefing.md). Do NOT let them approach the minotaur cold — that's a fight, and a deadly one (Khaeros attacks first, and means it; near-TPK at level 3). Brundt is the airlock. He goes in front.
>
> **The point of this scene:** Khaeros's first reaction to peace is not gratitude or hope. It's **disbelief that curdles into suspicion**, because the good news *indicts his whole survival strategy.* The party doesn't win him with arguments. They win him by being the first beings in his life to treat his leaving as *his own to decide* — and the "yes," when it comes, is not a word. It's that he eats.

---

## Before the door — what Brundt does

Brundt doesn't announce the party. He goes in **first, alone or nearly**, the way you approach a dangerous animal you happen to be friends with. He's done this before; Khaeros won't gut Brundt on sight. The party waits while the ogre clears the way — and *how* Brundt does it is the whole reason this works:

He brings **proof, not words.** Specifically:
- **His own transformation** — Brundt is clean, barbered, dressed in his Keep clothes ([the glow-up](the-ogre-goes-to-the-keep.md)). He *shows* Khaeros what the humans did: let him in, took his coin, sent him home better. He doesn't argue the humans are safe. He stands there *as the evidence* that they are.
- **A meal from the Keep** — a whole roasted pig, an apple in its mouth, aromatics. Brundt's idea of abundance: a thing so large it announces there's *more where it came from.* (He'd get the choice a little wrong — bring what *he* loved at the Keep — and the wrongness is the kindness. See [khaeros-the-minotaur.md](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md).)

This is diplomacy in the only currency Brundt trusts — *tangible proof delivered by a trusted peer* — and it's the most persuasive thing in the cave. The most convincing argument for the Duke's whole project is Brundt's haircut.

---

## Beat 1 — The Cave, and the Stillness

> *Brundt leads you back through the shunned caverns, past where the other factions' territory ends, to a cave mouth most of the ravine pretends isn't there. Inside, in the dim, you make out the shapes first — bones and skulls, but arranged, ordered, set into patterns along the walls. Not a slaughter. A* decoration*. Someone* lives *here, and has made it theirs.*
>
> *And then the shape at the back of the cave stands, and keeps standing, and the bones forgotten, every eye in the room goes to the bull-headed giant rising out of the dark. Chain mail. A spear already in his hand, already leveled. He does not speak. He does not charge. He has gone utterly still — the stillness of something that has decided, in the half-second since you appeared, exactly how it will kill each of you and in what order.*

**DM notes:** Khaeros is at threat-level the instant strangers appear. Spear up, but not yet thrown — because **Brundt is here**, and Brundt is the only reason there isn't blood already. Play the stillness, not rage. This isn't a beast working itself up; it's an intelligence that has *already done the math* and is waiting to see if it has to act. The decorated bones are the tell the party should clock: this is a *person's* home, not a monster's den.

Let the danger be real. If the party does something stupid — draws, advances, threatens — Khaeros will act, and he hits hard (spear for 4–9, then gore-and-bite). Brundt may physically interpose. The scene can collapse into combat here if the party blows it. It's *supposed* to be a knife's edge.

---

## Beat 2 — Brundt Vouches

> *Brundt steps between you and the spear — slow, unbothered, the way only someone who's done it before would dare. He sets the roasted pig down on the cave floor between himself and the minotaur, and straightens, spreading his clean-dressed arms wide so the bull-man can see all of him.*
>
> **"Khaeros. Me. Brundt."** *He says it simply.* **"Look at me. Look what they did."** *He turns, showing the clothes, the clean of him.* **"They let me in. Their wall. Their food. I came back better. These ones"**—*a thumb at the party*—**"paid for it. They want to talk. Just talk. I brought you good meat to prove it's not a trick."**

**DM notes:** Brundt's pitch is *concrete and personal* — *look at me, look what they did, I came back better.* He's not making an argument about peace; he's presenting himself as the *outcome.* And critically, Khaeros **knows Brundt** — knows him well enough to read him. Which sets up the hinge of the whole scene: Khaeros can tell the difference between his friend-under-duress and his friend-just-being-himself, and Brundt is *unmistakably* the latter. Comfortable. Cleaner. *Himself, but better.* You cannot fake that to someone who's known you for years.

---

## Beat 3 — The Disbelief (the heart of the scene)

This is the most important beat, and it moves in *stages*, fast, across one conversation. Khaeros does not soften steadily. He *resists*, and the resistance is the character.

**Stage one — it's a trap.** His first read isn't "the humans are kind." It's *"this is the most sophisticated trap I've ever seen, and they got Brundt."*

> *The spear does not lower. When the minotaur finally speaks, the voice is deep, accented, rusty from disuse — and cold.* **"They dressed you up. Fed you. Sent you to me smiling."** *His eyes move over Brundt's clean clothes like they're a wound.* **"That is how it is done, Brundt. That is exactly how it is done. They make it look safe. They make it look* kind*. And then—"** *the spear shifts, a fraction, toward the party* **"—the chains."**

**DM notes:** This is *rational*, given everything Khaeros knows. He's seen the [bugbears' welcome signs](../characters/faction-leaders.md#bugbear-chief--vargoth-the-welcomer). He knows the grammar of *lure-then-take.* To him, Brundt's transformation looks like **grooming** — bait dressed up to draw the next victim in. Let the party feel that their best evidence (the happy, transformed ogre) reads, to a traumatized exile, as the *trap itself.*

**Stage two — but he can't make the trap-reading hold.** Because Brundt isn't *acting* baited. He's just *Brundt*, comfortable, complaining the pig's gone cold, scratching at his collar — being himself, with better stuff.

> *But Brundt only shrugs, and sits down heavily by the pig, and tears off a leg, and eats it like a man who has nowhere better to be. "S'good cold, even," he says, mouth full, to no one. And you watch the minotaur watch him — and watch the certainty in those eyes flicker, because whatever this is, the ogre eating contentedly on his cave floor is not a creature performing a part.*

**DM notes:** *Brundt's ease is the crack.* Not the party's words — the ogre's body. You can't fake ease to someone who's watched you be uneasy for years. Khaeros knows Brundt; he can see his friend isn't under duress. That's what opens the door a finger's width.

**Stage three — *they let YOU in?*** The line, with the weight on *you*.

> *The spear-point drops — not all the way, but it drops. And the minotaur says, quieter, and the cold has cracked into something rawer underneath:* **"...They let* you *in. An ogre."** *A long breath.* **"Through the wall. Into the town. And let you* leave."**

**DM notes:** This is the gut-punch, and the party may not fully grasp why it lands so hard. Khaeros is an *ogre* — a creature even lower in the human bestiary than a minotaur in some reckonings. If the wall opened for *Brundt*, then it was never about *worthiness*, never about being too monstrous. Which means the cage Khaeros has kept himself in for years *had no lock* — he could, perhaps, have walked to that wall any time. The disbelief here is part envy, part self-recognition, part the dawning horror that his whole survival strategy might have been built on a fear that, for the ogre at least, *resolved.* The good news indicts the waiting. Let it be heavy.

---

## Beat 4 — Cautious Admittance

> *For a long moment the minotaur stands torn — spear half-lowered, the math half-abandoned, his whole stance the picture of a creature whose certainties have just developed a crack and who does not know what holds him up if they fail. Then, slowly, he lowers the spear's point to the cave floor. Not away. Just down.*
>
> **"...Talk, then,"** *he says, and the word costs him.* **"Carefully. And know that if this is a trap, ogre or no ogre, I will open three of you before the last one reaches the door."**

**DM notes — this is exactly the tone you want:** he doesn't admit them because he's persuaded. He admits them because **Brundt's existence broke his model of the world, and he has to find out what replaces it.** The listening is *not hope.* It's a creature whose foundational belief ("humans take you or kill you, the wall is closed") just developed a crack, who has to find out — carefully, ready to kill if it's a trick — whether the crack goes all the way down. He admits them the way you approach a wall you always thought solid and just heard echo *hollow.*

The threat at the end is real and he means it. Keep the knife's edge even now. He is *listening*, not *trusting.*

---

## Beat 5 — The Negotiation, and the Question He Can't Help Asking

Now the party gets to actually talk. **Words won't win him** — *terms are what slavers offer,* and he knows it. What reaches him is something else, and you should watch for the party to find it (or fail to):

**What does NOT work:**
- *"The Duke is kind / nicer."* Nothing. The Duke is a human power; so is the Baron; under one crown he sees no difference.
- *Promises of safety.* Safety is exactly what the lure promises. The more they reassure, the more it sounds like bait.
- *Treating him as a creature to be recruited, used, or managed.* He's spent his life being *used for his strength.* He'll smell it.

**What DOES work — and this is the key the party has to find:**

Somewhere in the conversation, Khaeros will ask Brundt — not the party, *Brundt* — a question he can't help asking. It gives away the thirty years:

> **"Brundt. When they took you in."** *A pause; this is hard for him.* **"Did they ask where you were from? Did anyone ask if you* wanted *to leave?"**

**DM notes — this is the whole negotiation in one line.** Brundt won't understand the question — he has nowhere he's trying to go, nobody ever needed to ask him such a thing, so he'll blink at it. But the question underneath is: *is there a version of this where my leaving is treated as mine to decide?* Khaeros isn't asking whether the humans are safe. He's asking whether anyone will treat him as a person with a *where* — a home he came from, a choice about returning — rather than a *what*, a monster or an asset.

**If the party catches this** — if they answer *that* question, treat Thyatis as a real place he came from and might go back to, treat his leaving as his own to decide, offer *orientation* (where he is, the way home) rather than *terms* — they reach him. That's the move. Not "we'll protect you." But "you're far from home, we know the way, and whether you go or stay is yours."

(And the cruelty waiting underneath, for later: the way home leads back to an empire that would call him a monster, and the federation can offer him what Thyatis won't — to be a *person* here. But that's a choice for a later scene. See [khaeros-the-minotaur.md](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md). Tonight, just reaching him is enough.)

---

## Beat 6 — The Yes (which is not a word)

Khaeros will not say "I trust you." He may not say yes at all in words. The turn is **physical**, and it's the scene's climax:

Through the whole encounter, he has *stood* — spear near, ready. The moment it turns is small. Either:

- **He sits.** The math stops. He lets his guard down a degree no threat could make him lower.
- **Or — the bigger one — he eats.** He takes meat from the Keep's pig. *Taking offered food, eating it,* is the enormous concession: it's literally taking the human world *into his body* on his friend's word. For a creature who may have been starved or fed-as-property once, eating offered food is not nothing. It's the floor being replaced.

> *The minotaur looks at the pig on his cave floor — the Keep's food, in his home, brought by one of his own kind. He looks at Brundt, eating without fear. He looks at you. And then, slowly, this creature who decided in half a second how to kill you, who has not lowered his guard for any living thing in years, lowers himself to the floor, and reaches out, and takes a piece of the meat, and eats.*
>
> *Nobody has to say anything. That's the yes.*

**DM notes:** If you want the "yes" with zero dialogue, this is it. **Khaeros eats.** Everything before it is the disbelief; the eating is the floor being replaced. Don't oversell it. Let the table understand what it cost. A creature that "attacks first" just chose, for the first time in a long time, *not to* — and instead to share a meal. That's the whole campaign's thesis enacted in one gesture: a nature is not a scar, and someone took the risk of finding out.

---

## If it goes wrong

The scene is a genuine knife's edge and can break:

- **If the party approaches cold (no Brundt):** it's a deadly fight. Khaeros attacks first and hits like a truck. Don't soften this — the party was *told* to let Brundt go first.
- **If the party draws, advances, or threatens during the parley:** Khaeros acts on his stated promise. Brundt may interpose, possibly taking a hit, which is its own gut-punch (the trusted friend bleeding to protect the meeting).
- **If the party reaches for "terms" — bribes, deals, "work for us":** he hears *slaver*, and the door closes. He won't necessarily attack, but the chance is gone for now, and he goes cold. They may get a second chance later, but they've confirmed his worst read.
- **If the party gets it right:** Khaeros eats, and the hardest case in the caves becomes the federation's most loaded ally — and, eventually, its [living evidence against the Baron](the-iron-ring.md).

## Quick beat-sheet

1. **The cave** — decorated bones (a *person* lives here); Khaeros stands, spear up, gone *still* (the math done, waiting).
2. **Brundt vouches** — sets down the pig, shows his clean clothes: *"Look what they did. I came back better."* Khaeros *knows* Brundt — can read that he's genuine.
3. **The disbelief** (the heart): *it's a trap → but Brundt's ease cracks it → "they let YOU in?"* (the cage had no lock).
4. **Cautious admittance** — spear lowered, not away: *"Talk, then. Carefully."* Listening, not trusting. The crack, not the conversion.
5. **The negotiation** — terms fail; the question that wins is *"did anyone ask if you wanted to leave?"* Reach him as a person with a *where*, offer orientation not safety.
6. **The yes** — not a word. **He sits. He eats.** The floor replaced. A creature that attacks first chooses, this once, not to.
