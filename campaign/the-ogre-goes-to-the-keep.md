# The Ogre Goes to the Keep

*The payoff to a "yes" at [first contact](first-contact-the-ogre.md). [Brundt's](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md) sincerity test, run in full — and the campaign's whole thesis demonstrated through a shopping trip. Boxed text is read-aloud; everything else is DM guidance.*

**Follows:** [beat 3 — First Contact](first-contact-the-ogre.md) (on a "yes") · **Plays at:** the Keep ([beat 2 location](the-keep-arrival-and-hub.md)) · **Key NPCs:** [Brundt](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md), [Castellan Valerian](../characters/castellan-valerian.md), [Captain Bohdan](../characters/captain-bohdan.md)

> **Sequence:** Travel → Entry at the gate → The Castellan's briefing → Escort through the Keep → He spends the 250 → The Eagles → Cleaned up → The wagon home.
>
> **The point of the whole scene:** Brundt doesn't buy a weapon. He buys the materials of a *settled life* — and when his supposed gold turns out to be Black Eagle Barony scrip, he doesn't take it out on the merchants. By the end, the most persuasive argument for the Duke's entire project isn't a speech. It's an ogre with a haircut, pulling his own wagon home.
>
> **Note on timing:** the party has already met Valerian and worked from the Keep ([beat 2](the-keep-arrival-and-hub.md)), so this isn't a first meeting — it's the trust they built there getting stress-tested by a nine-foot ogre at the gate. This is the "Brundt test, in his face" beat from [Valerian's profile](../characters/castellan-valerian.md).

---

## Part 1 — Travel

The road from the ravine to the Keep. Brundt walks it on his own two feet, carrying his own coin (the party's 250gp advance, plus a sack of his own gold he's proud of — see *The Fake Gold*).

> *Brundt walks like a man who has never had anywhere to be. He stops to look at things — a milestone, a fence, a field under cultivation — with frank, slow curiosity, the way you'd look at the inside of a house you'd only ever seen from the road. Once, watching smoke rise from a distant farmstead chimney, he stands still long enough that you have to call him on.*

**DM notes:** This is quiet character work. Brundt has lived his whole life in the ravine; the *ordinary* world is exotic to him. He's not casing the place — he's *seeing* it. Play his curiosity as guileless. If the party makes conversation, he's easy company: blunt, literal, interested in how things cost and how they're made. He may ask what farmers eat, who owns the fields, whether the Keep's food is "the good kind." Everything routes back to the concrete.

**Tension to seed lightly:** travelers on the road who see an ogre walking *with* an armed party react with alarm. A cart pulls off. A rider spurs ahead — possibly to warn the Keep. This is the first ripple of the thing the whole scene tests: *how does the human world react to a monster walking into it?* The party may have to manage it. Brundt notices the fear and doesn't comment, but he notices.

## Part 2 — Entry at the Gate

The guards have **standing instructions: the party works for the Duke.** But nobody briefed them on the *ogre.*

> *The gate-wardens see you coming and you watch the moment land — the easy nod for an approaching party curdling into something else as they count one more figure than expected, and register its size. Spears come down. A voice, tight: "Hold. HOLD. What in the Immortals' names is that?"*

**DM notes:** Play the gate as a *real* obstacle that the party's authority *resolves but does not erase.* The guards know to admit the party; they emphatically did not expect a nine-foot ogre, and their instinct is correct alarm. This is a roleplay/negotiation beat, not a fight. The party has to vouch — invoke the Duke's commission, explain the peace project, take responsibility for him. Let it be a little tense. A guard captain or sergeant is summoned. Word goes up to the Castellan *before* anyone passes.

**Brundt's behavior here is the first proof:** he does *nothing* threatening. He stands still, hands visible, and lets the humans argue about him. He's been the object of fear his whole life; he knows exactly how to be still while people decide whether he's allowed to exist near them. If anything, his *calm* unsettles the guards more than rage would — they're braced for a monster and getting a patient one.

> *Brundt, for his part, simply waits. He does not bristle, does not loom. He stands with the weary patience of someone who has been looked at this way many times, and folds his huge hands in front of him, and says nothing — which somehow rattles the wardens more than a roar would have.*

**Outcome:** They're admitted, conditionally, pending the Castellan. The ogre crosses the threshold into the Keep — possibly the first humanoid to do so peacefully in the place's memory. Mark the moment.

## Part 3 — The Castellan's Briefing

The party is called up to brief **[Castellan Valerian](../characters/castellan-valerian.md)** — the Duke's man who commands the Keep — while Brundt is held (courteously, watchfully) below or in a guarded yard.

**Valerian's position:** He has orders to support the party, and a working relationship with them by now — but he did *not* sign up for an ogre inside his walls, and it's a problem he didn't ask for. He's a practical frontier commander — not a villain, not a bigot for its own sake, but a man responsible for the safety of a fortress full of frightened people on a dangerous border. His questions are the *reasonable* ones, and the party has to answer them:

- *You brought a monster through my gate. Explain.*
- *The Duke said open relations. He didn't say march one into my keep. What is this?*
- *Is it dangerous? Can you control it? What happens when it's drunk in my tavern and someone says the wrong thing?*
- *My people raid those caves and bury their dead from those caves. How do I tell a man whose brother was killed on that road that there's an ogre buying bread in the market?*

**DM notes:** This is the *Duke's-hall argument in miniature*, run early and at the level of one practical commander — and it's pure [Valerian](../characters/castellan-valerian.md), who **moves on results, not rhetoric.** The party can't win it with sentiment ("he's actually nice"). They win it the way the whole campaign wins these rooms — by reframing:

- **It's a demonstration, not a risk.** If the federation is ever going to work, *someone* has to be the first, and better a controlled first contact, vouched for, under the party's responsibility, than a surprise later.
- **It's the Duke's own project.** Valerian's orders to support the party *are* the answer; this is what supporting it looks like.
- **The ogre is proof of concept.** If one ogre can spend coin in the market and walk out without blood, Valerian has just watched the borderland's whole problem get cheaper to solve than war — and that's an argument a soldier counting lives and supplies can actually hear.

Valerian, won over (or at least *permitting*), sets **terms**: the ogre is escorted at all times by **[Captain Bohdan](../characters/captain-bohdan.md)** and a detail. He doesn't go where he pleases. He does his business and he leaves. The party is responsible for anything that goes wrong. *Fair, and the party should take the deal.*

**This is a load-bearing beat in Valerian's arc.** He's the man who'd say "why not just wipe them out" if pushed, and he keeps that in reserve. Today he *permits* the demonstration — and watches it, closely, to learn whether he was a fool to. What Brundt does in the market (Parts 5–6) is the result that moves him. If it goes well, this is the day the Castellan's skepticism takes its first real crack.

## Part 4 — The Escort

Brundt is walked through the Keep by **[Captain Bohdan](../characters/captain-bohdan.md)** and three conspicuously large rank-and-file soldiers: **[Titus Maro, Marcus Varro, and Ilie Dobrev](../locations/keep/18-inner-gatehouse.md#brundts-escort-detail)**. They are just big, steady men selected to reassure the Keep, not special operatives. This is the heart of the scene — and it should be, in the best way, a little bit *wonderful* and a little bit *funny.*

> *Word runs ahead of you faster than you walk. Shutters crack open and slam shut. A mother pulls a child off the street by the collar. Conversations stop dead and pick back up in whispers. And through it all moves Brundt — escorted, watched, hands where everyone can see them — gawking at the Keep the way the Keep is gawking at him.*
>
> *He stares at a glazed window. He stops at a baker's door and breathes in like a man at a shrine. He watches a blacksmith's hammer fall and asks, to no one in particular, how much a thing like that costs to make.*

**DM notes:** [Captain Bohdan](../characters/captain-bohdan.md) — "the Wall," the biggest man in the Keep — is your secondary character for this stretch. Start him wary, professional, hand near his sword, *trying to be a presence next to an ogre* (and failing, in front of his own men). Over the course of the walk, let his posture *change.* Because what he's escorting isn't a monster straining at a leash; it's a creature *delighted,* overwhelmed, polite, paying for things — and Bohdan, of all people, knows what it is to be read as a *size* before a man. He came expecting to manage a threat and finds himself, against his will, almost a *tour guide.* That arc — wary handler to grudging guide — is the human side of the same thesis the party's been living. Don't force it; let his lines soften a half-step at a time. By the end he might be the one telling Brundt which stall has the better cheese. See [his profile](../characters/captain-bohdan.md).

**The market reacts in a spread:** some shopkeepers refuse him outright (fear, grief, principle — let at least one slam a door, because not everyone comes around and the scene is truer for it). Some serve him out of nervous courtesy. And a few — the bold, the broke, the curious — serve him *gladly,* because his coin is as good as anyone's and a story about the day an ogre bought your wares is worth telling for years. The market is the Keep's whole range of responses to the federation, compressed into one afternoon of commerce.

## Part 5 — He Spends the 250

What Brundt buys is the entire characterization. **He does not buy a weapon. He does not buy armor. He does not get drunk and throw his weight around.** Given access to a market for the first time in his life, what he reaches for is the materials of a *decent, settled life:*

Run it roughly in this order — each purchase a small beat:

1. **A bath and a barber.** First thing. He wants to be *clean,* and presentable. (Comedy and pathos both: an ogre easing into a bath sized for him by terrified, then fascinated, bathhouse staff; a barber working up the nerve.)
2. **Clothing.** Decent clothes that fit — which someone has to *make* or improvise, another small event. He fusses with the unfamiliar feel of a real shirt.
3. **Furniture.** A bench. A table. And — the line that lands — **a bed. A mattress.** He has never owned a bed. He buys one.
4. **Provisions and comforts** — good food, the *good kind*, drink, small things that please him.
5. **A wagon** — to haul it all home. (He'll pull it himself. He's strong. See Part 7.)

> *You watch a nine-foot ogre run a thumb along a bolt of cloth with something like reverence, and ask the price of a mattress, and count out your coin to pay for a bed he intends to sleep in. He is not arming himself. He is not casing the place. He is, with total seriousness, the way a man does it who has wanted to for a very long time, simply* furnishing a life.

**DM notes — say this out loud to yourself before you run it:** *The Keep didn't civilize him. It finally sold him the things he already wanted.* He was never the obstacle. The gate was. The second the exclusion lifts, he self-civilizes faster than any sermon could manage — because the disposition was always there, and all he lacked was the *market.* This is the Duke's entire thesis, demonstrated without a word of argument, by a shopping list. Let the party *watch* it. Don't explain it to them. The good ones will feel it.

The 250gp goes on these things. He spends it freely, happily, like a man on the best day he's had in years — because he is.

## Part 6 — The Eagles

Then he reaches for his *own* sack — the gold he's been proud of, hauling around, maybe the down payment on something bigger, a finer thing he's had his eye on.

> *When the first sack runs low, Brundt reaches for his own — a fat purse he's carried with quiet pride all day, gold he's saved, gold he means to spend on something good. He counts a coin into the merchant's palm. The merchant frowns. Turns it over. Scratches the surface with a thumbnail — and beneath the gold wash, a dull grey gleam. Lead. He points to the little eagle stamped on one face and the warning worked around its edge. “Eagles,” he says, in the voice you use near a large animal. “Barony scrip. Not gold. Not legal coin here.”*

**The drama — and the load-bearing beat of the whole scene:** Brundt's gold, the sack he was proud of, is **Eagles: the Black Eagle Barony's gold-washed lead scrip.** The tokens can be spent through the Barony's own system, but are not Karameikan legal tender and the lead itself is worth less than a copper. Somebody paid him in Eagles as though they were gold, long ago, and he never knew. He's been carrying another ruler's promise around like treasure.

Eagles are familiar this close to the Barony. Duke Stefan knows they exist, traders occasionally receive them innocently, and the [Loan Bank](../locations/keep/11-loan-bank.md#eagles--black-eagle-barony-scrip) is vigilant about catching them in mixed coin. A single Eagle is not proof of wrongdoing. Passing one as gold is fraud; finding a whole pay sack can also point toward employment or trade connected to the Black Eagle Barony. Locals sometimes call worn examples **“black eagles”** after the dark lead showing through their rubbed-away wash.

**He gets angry.** Let the table feel the danger spike — a nine-foot ogre, just humiliated, in a market full of people who already feared him, holding fistfuls of fake money. The guards tense. Hands move to weapons. *This* is the moment everyone braced for at the gate. This is where it could all go wrong.

**And he doesn't.**

> *The anger comes off him like heat off a forge — his hands close, his shoulders bunch, and every guard in the square sets their feet. The merchant goes white.*
>
> *And then Brundt lets out a long, slow breath. He looks at the fake coin in his palm, and at the merchant who didn't make it, and at his own fist. And the fight goes out of him into something older and heavier. "Not yours," he says, quiet, to the terrified merchant. "Somebody did me. Not you." He sets the false coins down on the counter, gentle, like they might break. "Sorry. For the scare. Sorry." And he turns away, and the square breathes again.*

**DM notes — this is the proof the Duke is right, and you should let it be the emotional center of the session:**

Brundt has *every excuse the stories say a monster needs.* He's been cheated, humiliated, frightened, surrounded — and he does the one thing that defines a person rather than a beast: **he distinguishes the merchant in front of him from whoever actually wronged him.** He knows the difference between the man across the counter and the man who did him. That is the exact faculty the [cult lacks](../characters/the-hierophant.md). That is the faculty the entire federation runs on. The dumb, venal ogre has it — and he uses it *while angry,* under genuine grievance, which is the only version that counts.

If you ever need to convince a skeptic at the Keep — Valerian, the captain, a grieving widow — *why the Duke is right to bet on these people,* you don't make a speech. You tell them what the ogre did when he found the fake coins. He had every reason the stories demand, and he behaved better than the men who cheated him.

**Practical note:** there's no damage. No one is hurt. The party may want to help — cover the loss, find a trader willing to redeem the Eagles, or investigate who passed a Barony pay sack to an ogre as gold (and whether it connects to the [Iron Ring](the-iron-ring.md)). All of that is good. But the *beat* is complete the moment Brundt sets the coins down and apologizes. Let it breathe before you move on.

## Part 7 — Cleaned Up, and the Wagon Home

> *By the time you leave, the ogre at your side is not the one you brought. Bathed, barbered, dressed in clothes that nearly fit, he stands a little differently — not prouder, exactly, but* settled*, like a man who has remembered something about himself. Behind him, roped and loaded, sits a wagon stacked with the makings of a home: a mattress, a table, sacks of good food, the small comforts of a life he was never before allowed to buy.*
>
> *He takes up the wagon's traces himself — no horse, no ox, just Brundt — and leans into the weight, and the whole loaded cart rolls forward behind him as if it weighed nothing. He is, after all, strong. He's just never had anything worth hauling before.*

**DM notes — the image to land the scene on:**

Brundt returns to the caves transformed, and **he's bringing the evidence home on a cart he pulls himself.** He doesn't come back as the swing-vote mercenary who left. He comes back as the **first resident of the caves to have lived, even briefly, as a citizen** — clean, dressed, fed, with furniture and stories of a town that took his money and let him sleep in a bed.

The party didn't hire a guide. **They funded a demonstration,** and it's about to roll through the courtyard where every faction can see it. Every cave-dweller who's been told the Keep is the wall that locks them out now has to reckon with the ogre who got *in,* and came back *better off.* That's worth more than the 250gp it cost. It's the federation's whole pitch, made visible, pulling its own weight down the road.

**The shadow to hold for later (don't play it now):** Brundt coming home *enviably* better off is the first *visible inequality* the federation has produced. Some factions will want what he got. Some will resent that the human's first gift went to the freelancer who was never loyal to anyone. The [hobgoblins under Karrgan](../characters/faction-leaders.md#hobgoblin-chief--karrgan), who've *paid* this ogre for years, may have a pointed question about why the creature they kept fed came back wearing the Duke's prosperity. The bed on the cart is a wonderful thing — and the first thing anyone in the ravine has to *count.* You can't change who has what and expect everyone to feel the same. File it. It comes due later, in the [distributive politics of the federation](../narrative/progression.md).

## Quick beat-sheet

1. **Travel** — Brundt sees the ordinary world like an exhibit; the road fears him; he notices and says nothing.
2. **Gate** — guards know the party, not the ogre; real alarm; the party vouches; his *calm* unsettles them more than rage. Admitted, conditionally.
3. **Castellan** — the Duke's-hall argument in miniature with Valerian; win it by reframing risk as demonstration; he permits it on terms (escort, responsibility). He's watching to see if he's a fool.
4. **Escort** — [Captain Bohdan](../characters/captain-bohdan.md), wary handler → grudging guide; the market reacts across the full spread (refusal, courtesy, delight).
5. **Spends the 250** — bath, barber, clothes, furniture, **a bed**, provisions, a wagon. He furnishes a *life,* not an arsenal. The thesis, shown not told.
6. **The Eagles** — his supposed gold is Black Eagle Barony scrip; he's humiliated, *angry,* surrounded — and he distinguishes the merchant from whoever wronged him, apologizes, sets the coins down. The proof the Duke is right. No damage.
7. **Wagon home** — bathed, dressed, settled, pulling his own loaded cart back to the ravine: the proof of concept made visible, and the federation's first visible inequality, rolling into the courtyard.
