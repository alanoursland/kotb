# Brundt's Briefing — The Ogre Tells What He Knows

*A beat that can happen at various times. After [first contact](first-contact-the-ogre.md), once [Brundt](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md) is hired (or trust is established), the party can ask him about the caves. He's the best source they have and an imperfect one. He knows the ravine the way a tradesman knows his customers — who pays, who's strong, who's trouble — and he knows nothing the way a tradesman doesn't: their inner lives, their secrets, the things they hide even from each other.*

**Timing:** any time after [beat 3](first-contact-the-ogre.md), recurring · **Key NPC:** [Brundt](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md) · **Frames:** [beat 4 — Walking the Caves](caves-of-chaos.md)

> **The point of this scene:** give the party real, useful intelligence — and let them *over-trust it.* Brundt's gaps and his amorality are not flaws in the briefing; they're the briefing. What he leaves out (the [cult](the-iron-ring.md), the [bugbears'](the-iron-ring.md) true business) and what he can't see (that any of it is *wrong*) is exactly what the party will walk into unprepared. He's a map with the dangerous parts blank.

---

## How Brundt knows what he knows

He's been **for hire to every faction in the ravine for years.** That's the whole basis of his knowledge: he's worked *for* most of them, fought *against* some of them, been paid by all of them. So he knows:

- **Who's strong, who's weak, who pays well, who reneges.** Tradesman's knowledge. Reliable.
- **Where everyone lives and roughly how many.** He's walked the ravine a thousand times.
- **Who feuds with whom.** He's been hired by both sides of the same quarrel.
- **The surface behavior of each group.** What they do where he can see it.

And he does **not** know:

- **Anything any faction keeps secret.** He's a contractor, not a confidant. Nobody tells the hired muscle their plans.
- **What the bugbears actually do with the people they take.** He knows they take slaves. He doesn't know about the [Iron Ring](the-iron-ring.md), the pipeline, or where the slaves *go.* To him it's just "bugbears keep slaves, like always."
- **The cult, essentially at all.** He won't take jobs near the back of the shunned caverns — *"bad ground, nobody pays enough"* — and he's incurious about why. He doesn't connect the famine to anything. He doesn't know what's down there. The thing the whole campaign turns on is the one thing his briefing is blankest about.

And — the crucial framing — he has **no ethical problem with any of it.** Brundt isn't withholding judgment to be diplomatic. He genuinely doesn't have the judgment. Slaving, cannibalism, raiding — to him these are just *things the neighbors do,* facts of the ravine like weather. He'll report them in the same flat, untroubled tone he'd use for "the kobolds dig" or "the orcs are jumpy." **The horror, when the party feels it, is theirs — not his.** He's not a bad creature. He simply doesn't sort the world into right and wrong; he sorts it into *who pays and who's trouble.* Let that come through. It's characterization *and* it's why his briefing is dangerous: he'll tell you the bugbears enslave people with the exact same shrug he uses for everything else, and move on, because to him it isn't the headline it is to you.

---

## Running the briefing

Let the party ask. Don't dump it. Brundt answers what he's asked, in his concrete, transactional way, and the *gaps show* when the party asks about things he doesn't track. Below is what he says about each group — accurate where he's accurate, blank where he's blank, untroubled throughout.

### The Kobolds → [Yiznak](../characters/faction-leaders.md#kobold-chief--yiznak-the-deepwright)

> **"Little scaly ones. Lots of 'em — most bodies in the ravine, fewest worth worrying about. They dig. Set traps. Live where nobody else'll go, down in the wet and the dark. Poor. Nobody respects 'em."** *He shrugs.* **"They know every hole in this place, though. Every back way. You want to know how to get somewhere quiet, you ask a kobold — if one'll talk to you."**

*Accurate.* Useful intel: the kobolds are the ravine's engineers and know its geography. Brundt's tone is dismissive (everyone underrates them) but the *information* is good. He has no idea they might be kin to the [marsh lizardfolk](the-lizardfolk-of-the-marsh.md) — that's not the kind of thing he'd ever think about.

### The Orcs → [Gorrak & Ushka](../characters/faction-leaders.md#orc-chiefs--gorrak--ushka-feuding)

> **"Two lots of 'em, and they hate each other more'n they hate anybody. Don't ask me why — old grudge. Both jumpy. Both armed, good gear, some of it magic. They put heads on sticks out front."** *He says this with mild approval.* **"Smart. Keeps people off. I've fought for 'em, fought against 'em. They pay, they don't cheat much. Just don't walk up sudden — they kill first and look after."**

*Accurate.* Good intel: two feuding orc groups, well-armed, paranoid, deterrence-minded, *rational* (Brundt respects the heads-on-pikes as strategy, which it is). The warning — *don't approach suddenly* — is real and worth heeding.

### The Goblins / Hobgoblins → [Karrgan](../characters/faction-leaders.md#hobgoblin-chief--karrgan)

> **"Goblins down one way — but the ones that run things are the big disciplined sort. Hobgoblins. They've got order. Lines, ranks, all that. Best-organized lot in the ravine, and they know it."** *A pause; this part he says as plainly as the rest:* **"They eat people. Keep some penned for it. Always have."** *He moves on without weight.* **"They've paid me regular, the goblins. When they get hit, they toss me the sack and I come running. Reliable money."**

*Accurate, and here's the tone test.* The [cannibalism](captivity-and-hunger.md) lands as a **flat aside** — "they eat people, keep some penned, always have" — delivered with no more gravity than "they pay regular." Brundt doesn't flag it as horror because to him it isn't. The party supplies the horror. He does *not* know whether it's subsistence or ritual (that's an interior question he's never asked); he just knows it's a thing that happens. Useful intel: hobgoblins are the organized military faction and Brundt's most regular employer.

### The Bugbears — *the dangerous blank* → [Vargoth](../characters/faction-leaders.md#bugbear-chief--vargoth-the-welcomer)

> **"Strong, the bugbears. Stronger'n they look, and they look plenty. They keep slaves — take travelers, keep some, that's their way."** *He says it like a weather report.* **"Put signs up. Welcome-like. People walk in, and, well."** *A shrug.* **"I don't work for 'em much. They don't pay so good, and—"** *he considers, then can't articulate it* **"—I dunno. Don't like the feel. But strong. Wouldn't want 'em against me."**

*This is the critical entry, because what Brundt knows is a fraction of the truth.* He knows: bugbears are strong, they enslave, they use the welcome-sign lure. He does **not** know: that they're [Iron Ring](the-iron-ring.md), that the slaves feed a pipeline to the Black Eagle, that they're a *node in a conspiracy* rather than just brutal locals. To Brundt it's "bugbears keep slaves, like always" — a static fact, not an operation.

The one *flicker* worth playing: Brundt doesn't like them and can't say why. That's his honest instinct catching something his mind can't name — the same faint unease [the Curate has about the Priest](../characters/the-curate.md). He won't pursue it. But a sharp party might note that the amoral ogre who's untroubled by cannibalism is *uneasy* about the bugbears specifically, and wonder what his gut is catching that his words can't.

### The Gnolls → [Maruk](../characters/faction-leaders.md#gnoll-chief--maruk)

> **"Gnolls. Big, strong, keep to themselves mostly. Honestly?"** *He thinks about it, which is rare.* **"Less trouble than most. Don't bother nobody much. I don't have a lot to tell you about 'em, which — round here, that's a good word for a neighbor."**

*Accurate, and quietly the most important thing he says.* Brundt has *nothing bad to report* about the gnolls — and his having nothing to say is the tell. The party braced for the worst will note that the cleanest sheet in the ravine, per the ogre who knows everyone, belongs to one of the scariest-looking groups. Let that inversion sit.

### The Minotaur ([Khaeros](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md))

> *Here his tone changes — the only one that does.* **"There's the bull-man, back in the shunned caves. Khaeros."** *He says the name carefully, like it's owed respect.* **"Keeps to himself. Got coin, got good steel. Attacks first if you don't know him — I mean that, don't go in there green."** *A beat.* **"We talk, him and me. Sometimes. Two of us are the only ones in here who came from somewhere else, I figure. He's not like the rest. I'll take you to him, if you want. He'd not gut me on sight, at least."**

*This is the bridge.* Brundt *knows Khaeros* — socially, the two outsiders — and he's the airlock to the hardest case. He knows Khaeros is dangerous, wealthy, an outsider, and that he himself is trusted enough to make an introduction. **He may know fragments of Khaeros's history** (that he came from far off, that something was done to him) — how much you reveal here is your call, but Brundt is the natural source for the *first thread* of the minotaur's past, because he's the one who *asked.* (See [khaeros-the-minotaur.md](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md).) If the party wants to meet Khaeros, Brundt offering to walk them in is the start of it → **[First Contact — The Minotaur](first-contact-the-minotaur.md)**.

### The back of the shunned caverns — *the blank he won't fill* → [the cult](the-iron-ring.md)

If the party asks what else is back there, past Khaeros, in the deep shunned caves:

> *He shifts, uneasy in a way he wasn't for any of the rest.* **"Bad ground that way. I don't go. Nobody pays enough to go."** *Pressed:* **"Cold. Wrong-feeling. Dead things, I heard. I don't ask and I don't go. That's how I'm still here to talk to you."** *And that's all he'll give. He genuinely doesn't know more — he's never wanted to.*

*This is the most important gap in the whole briefing.* The cult — the thing the campaign turns on, the [manufactured famine](the-iron-ring.md), the [Hierophant](../characters/the-hierophant.md), the Thanatos shrine — is the one place Brundt has *deliberately stayed ignorant of.* His survival instinct (don't go where it's wrong and nobody pays) has kept him alive and kept him blind. He can point the party at the bad ground. He cannot tell them what's on it. **The briefing's biggest blank is the campaign's biggest threat** — and the party, having gotten so much good intel from Brundt, may not realize how little he's told them about the thing that matters most.

### The famine — *the connection he doesn't make*

If the party asks why the caves raid, or about food:

> **"Hungry, mostly. Bad land out here, nothing grows, game's gone thin lately—"** *he frowns, briefly, at "lately," but it passes* **"—so they take from the road. Always been some of that. More now."** *He doesn't know why "more now." It hasn't occurred to him to wonder.*

*He has the symptom and not the cause.* He's noticed the game's gone thin "lately" and the raiding's worse "more now" — but he doesn't connect it to the bad ground at the back, to the dead things he's heard about, to anything. The [famine is manufactured](the-iron-ring.md) by the cult's undead destroying food at night; Brundt has the *data points* (thinning game, worsening raids, dead things in the deep caves) and never assembles them, because assembling them would mean thinking about the bad ground, and he survives by not doing that. A *party* might assemble what Brundt won't.

## What the party should walk away with

**Real, valuable intelligence:** populations, geography, who's armed, who feuds, who's organized, who's reliable, where the kobolds' back-ways are, that the gnolls are clean, that the orcs are jumpy, that the hobgoblins run things. This is genuinely useful and the party is right to value it.

**A false sense of completeness.** Brundt is so forthcoming and so obviously knowledgeable that the party may take his briefing as *the whole picture.* It isn't. He's handed them an accurate map of the ravine's *surface* with two critical regions blanked out — the bugbears' true business and the cult entirely — and a famine whose cause he's never traced. The danger isn't that he lies. He never lies. The danger is that **his honesty about what he knows disguises how much he doesn't.**

**The tone lesson.** The party watches an intelligent creature describe slavery and cannibalism without a flicker of judgment — and that's data about Brundt, and about the ravine. He's not evil. He simply doesn't sort the world morally; the *neighbors do what they do,* and he reports it flat. If the party expects their guide to share their horror, they'll misread him. He's a tool and a friend, not a conscience. The conscience is theirs to bring.

## DM notes

- **Don't let Brundt editorialize.** His value and his danger both come from the flatness. The moment he says "isn't it terrible what the bugbears do," he stops being Brundt. He doesn't think it's terrible. He thinks it's Tuesday.
- **The two blanks are deliberate — protect them.** If a player asks point-blank "is there a cult?" or "where do the bugbear slaves go?", Brundt's honest answer is *"don't know, don't go there, never asked."* He cannot give them the cult or the pipeline because he genuinely doesn't have them. Resist the urge to have him know more than he should; his ignorance is load-bearing.
- **His unease about the bugbears is the one crack.** Play it once, lightly — the amoral ogre who shrugs at cannibalism is *bothered* by the bugbears and can't say why. It's the only place his gut outruns his knowledge, and it's a breadcrumb: something about the bugbears is wrong on a level even Brundt can feel.
- **This briefing pairs with experience.** What Brundt tells them is the frame; what they *find* in the caves fills it in — and the gaps between his account and the reality (the bugbears are worse than he knows; there's a cult he never mentioned; the famine has a cause) are where the campaign's real shape emerges. The briefing is good precisely because it's *incomplete in instructive ways.*
