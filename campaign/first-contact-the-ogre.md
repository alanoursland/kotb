# First Contact — The Ogre at the Ravine

*Run-at-the-table scene for the party's first meeting with [Brundt](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md). Boxed text is read-aloud; everything else is DM guidance. Adapt freely — the beats matter more than the words.*

**Spine beat:** [3 — First Contact](../narrative/progression.md) · **Type:** social (or a dangerous fight, if they blow it) · **Key NPC:** [Brundt "Who-Comes-When-Paid"](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md)

> **Trigger:** the party enters the lower ravine **announcing trade** rather than creeping in to fight. A trade posture brings Brundt out. A war party gets a war. See *If they come in hot*, at the end.

> **Why him first:** Brundt is the only resident whose disposition is *transactional, not territorial.* Every other faction defends a *home;* he runs a one-man toll booth whose cave opens right onto the lower ravine. He's the one creature whose door is functionally already open — so a party that arrives wanting to *deal* draws out the one resident who deals. The shape of the whole scene is the **menace-to-toll-booth flip,** and the campaign's thesis lands in it without a word of exposition: *the monster is a merchant.*

---

## Beat 1 — The Menace

> *The ravine narrows here, the cave mouths watching you from the rock like empty sockets. Before you've gone twenty paces, stone grinds on stone — and something unfolds out of the dark of the nearest cave.*
>
> *It stands nine feet if it stands an inch. Grey hide stretched over a frame like a loaded cart, a slab of a face, small eyes that find you and hold. One hand rests on a club the size of a man's leg. It does not charge. It does not roar. It just looks at you, the way a toll-keeper looks at a road — measuring what's coming, and what it's worth.*

**DM notes:** Let the table feel the threat. Hands go to weapons; that's correct, that's the fight they were promised. Hold the silence a beat longer than is comfortable. Then break it with the thing they don't expect: not violence — *appraisal.*

---

## Beat 2 — The Flip

> *It sniffs once, and its gaze drops to your packs, your belts, your hands — cataloguing. When it speaks, the voice is a low gravel, slow, and entirely unbothered.*
>
> **"You came loud. Loud means trade, or loud means stupid."** *A pause, while it decides which.* **"You got coin? Food? Something I can hold?"**

**DM notes:** This is the pivot the whole campaign turns on — the monster reveals himself as a *merchant.* He's not threatening them into paying; he's **quoting** them. He wants something he can *see and feel* — coin, food, drink. He is genuinely, transactionally interested. Play him bored-but-attentive, like a man who's done this a hundred times and will do it a hundred more.

If a player brandishes a weapon or postures: he doesn't flinch. **"Or that. That works too. Costs you more, though."** He is not afraid of them and sees no reason to pretend otherwise.

---

## Beat 3 — The Toll (and the hard rule)

If the party offers payment to pass:

> *It takes the coin — or the wineskin, or whatever you've offered — and weighs it in a palm that could close around your skull. Whatever it's worth, it's apparently enough. The ogre steps aside, not far, just enough.*
>
> **"Go on, then. Today you're paid up."** *Those small eyes track you as you pass.* **"You come back through and I don't know you again. Pay again, or don't come back. That's how it works."**

**DM notes:** The **first lesson of the cave market**, in one line: *the deal buys this passage, not a relationship.* (Module: 20+ gp of visible value = 90% he lets them by; but catch them again and he'll try to kill them, whatever they offer.) Pay every time, renegotiate every time, a coin is not a friendship. Let it register — it's the rule the whole federation runs on, learned here for the price of a wineskin.

**But the better path opens if the party *talks* instead of just paying past.** Most groups try to bribe deeper. A group that *engages* him — asks who he is, what he wants, whether he'd deal — finds a creature nobody has ever offered anything but per-job coin. Go to Beat 4.

---

## Beat 4 — The Name

When they ask who he is:

> *The question seems to catch it slightly off-guard — not the words, but that anyone asked. It considers you a moment longer.*
>
> **"Brundt."** *Just that. Then, as if remembering the rest is owed:* **"The caves call me other things. To you—"** *a short grunt, almost a shrug* **"—Brundt's enough."**

**DM notes:** Among the factions he's *Brundt Who-Comes-When-Paid* — a name that's also a job description. To the humans, in this moment, just **Brundt.** He drops the mercenary tag without noticing, the instant he's talking to someone who might treat him as a person instead of a service. **Don't flag it.** It's there for anyone listening.

---

## Beat 5 — The Pitch and His Test

If the party explains the real offer — hiring him, or opening trade and peace with the caves:

> *You watch the idea travel across that slab of a face — slowly, but it travels. Standing pay. Not a sack of coin when the goblins are bleeding, not a fight that might kill him, but* steady*. Something in the small eyes sharpens.*
>
> **"Steady work."** *He says it like tasting it.* **"Huh."**

He's interested — but he doesn't just say yes. **He tests them**, in the only currency he trusts:

> *Then he leans down, close enough that you can smell old smoke and older meat, and asks the thing he actually wants to know.*
>
> **"You got good food where you come from? The keep. The wall. They got a place a man buys a meal?"** *He straightens, and something almost like a challenge enters his voice.* **"Take me there. Let me spend my coin inside your wall, sit where your people sit, eat what they eat. My money good in there?"** *A beat.* **"Then maybe we talk steady."**

**DM notes — this is the whole scene's payload:**

- He's not asking "can I trust you." That's an abstraction he can't hold. He's asking **"will the gate open for *me,* will my coin spend, can I eat at your table"** — a sincerity test rendered into something he can taste.
- **The test runs both directions.** He thinks he's testing the humans. The request puts the **[Keep](the-keep-arrival-and-hub.md)** on trial: can the Duke's project actually let a monster walk in and buy bread? And it puts the **party** on trial — are they willing to vouch for an ogre, in front of frightened humans, on day one? ([Castellan Valerian](../characters/castellan-valerian.md) is the man who has to watch it happen.)
- **The hidden layer:** Brundt has been paid in counterfeit before — a sack of gold-washed lead worth a copper a coin, still sitting in his hoard. So underneath "is your food good" is **"is your money real, or is the economy you're offering me another fake sack."** He's been burned. He's checking the coins from the other side this time. You don't need to say this aloud — but if a player is perceptive about *why* the question matters so much to him, it's there to be found.

---

## Where it leaves the party

First contact ends on a choice that *is* the campaign in miniature: **do they take an ogre to the Keep, and can they get him through the gate?** Brundt thinks he's running a test. He's become the test *of them.* Everything that follows — [his glow-up at the Keep](../characters/brundt-the-ogre.md) (the bath, the bed, the cart), the front-man role, and the intelligence he carries about [Khaeros](../characters/khaeros-the-minotaur.md) and the [dark thread under the caves](the-iron-ring.md) — unspools from whether the party says yes to walking a nine-foot monster up to a human wall and vouching for him.

The shape in one line: **they brace for a monster, meet a merchant, learn the rules of his market, get his real name, and walk away holding his sincerity test — which is really the Keep's, and really their own.**

---

## If they come in hot

If the party enters weapons-out, no trade posture, treating the ravine as a dungeon to clear: **Brundt fights, and he's dangerous.** Use his B2 stats. Remember his hidden cache under the bones in the south of his cave — **6 magic arrows +1, a potion of invisibility, a cleric scroll (cure light wounds, hold person)** — and that he's smart enough to use terrain and to flee-and-return if the fight turns against him.

If they kill him, **don't fudge it.** They've slain the one creature who would have opened the whole ravine, and the easy path is gone. The caves don't *owe* the party first contact — it's earned by approaching as dealers rather than exterminators. Losing Brundt makes the campaign harder, and that's a fair and instructive consequence, not a punishment. Every other faction becomes a cold approach with no trusted intermediary, and the party will feel the absence of the airlock for a long time. *(This is the reason for [open decision #4 — the ogre fallback](../narrative/progression.md): decide your backup contact in advance.)*

---

## Quick beat-sheet (for at-a-glance running)

1. **Menace** — nine feet of ogre unfolds from the cave, *measures* them, doesn't charge.
2. **Flip** — "Loud means trade, or loud means stupid. You got coin?" The monster is a merchant.
3. **Toll** — pays for *this* passage only; catch-them-again-and-he-kills-them. The market's first rule.
4. **Name** — "Brundt." Drops the mercenary tag. Don't flag it.
5. **Pitch & Test** — "steady work" lands; he asks to be taken to the Keep to spend his coin. The test of them, of the Keep, of whether his money's real.
6. **The choice** — do they take an ogre through the gate? The campaign cracks open from here.
