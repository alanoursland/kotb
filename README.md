# The Caves of Chaos, Reconsidered

*A campaign reference for B2: **The Keep on the Borderlands**, relocated to Mystara's Grand Duchy of Karameikos and rebuilt around diplomacy, political intrigue, dangerous exploration, and the economics of a frontier.*

**System:** Basic-era D&D (B/X/BECMI assumptions) · **Starting party:** approximately 3rd level · **Setting:** Karameikos, Mystara · **Original foundation:** B2, *The Keep on the Borderlands*

## Players: start here

Karameikos is a young country still deciding what it is.

A generation ago, the Thyatian noble Stefan Karameikos exchanged his ancestral estates for authority over wild Traladara. He now rules from the port of Specularum as Grand Duke, trying to turn Thyatian settlers and administrators and the native Traladaran population into one realm. The roads, laws, churches, guilds, noble families, and military posts all participate in that project—and they do not agree about what the new country should become.

The campaign begins when Duke Stefan summons your party and sends you west to **Riverfork Keep**, an isolated fortified post in the river country north of Blight Swamp. Travelers have been attacked near a ravine known as the Caves of Chaos. The Duke needs the road made safe, but he is not asking for a routine extermination. He wants you to discover who lives there, whether they have leaders, what they need, and whether relations can be opened.

If they will speak, you have authority to negotiate. If they will not, you have authority to use force.

That contradiction is your commission.

### What kind of campaign is this?

Expect:

- old-school exploration and dangerous Basic D&D combat;
- negotiations where food, safety, status, and trade matter as much as Charisma;
- political tension between Thyatian authority and Traladaran history;
- a well-supplied frontier Keep that serves as home base, market, rest stop, and military headquarters;
- people whose motives cannot be read reliably from ancestry, appearance, title, or alignment label;
- decisions with consequences that may become clear only after you return home.

Talking is not a trick or a failure to engage with the adventure. It is one of its primary forms of play. Conversely, not every opponent can be persuaded, and extending trust can carry real risk.

Characters fit best if they can accept the Duke's commission, work together in public, and remain curious long enough to learn what is happening. They may be loyal ducal agents, skeptical Traladaran locals, Thyatian settlers, guild affiliates, clergy, mercenaries, or ordinary adventurers. They do not need to agree with Stefan—or with one another—but they do need a reason to take the mission seriously.

### Player content notice

This campaign can involve hunger, prejudice, colonial rule, religious conflict, captivity, slavery, cannibalism, torture, political corruption, and harm to civilians. The campaign examines those subjects rather than treating them as decorative villainy. The group should establish lines, veils, and the desired level of detail before play.

## Dungeon Masters

This repository is a working, table-facing reconstruction of B2 rather than a replacement rulebook or a polished standalone module. The original room descriptions are preserved in [reference/README.md](reference/README.md); the other files interpret, connect, name, and reorganize that material for this campaign.

The central change is simple: take the social behavior in B2 seriously. The Caves contain populations, leaders, dependents, alliances, trades, grudges, and a working balance of power. Treat them as frontier polities before treating them as encounter areas. The adventure then becomes an attempt to build and feed a peace while discovering who benefits from the violence.

### The campaign's hidden structure

The campaign braids two visible tracks over one hidden engine:

- **At the Keep**, the party must earn Valerian's confidence and turn a military supply line into a politically acceptable food bridge.
- **In the Caves**, the party must identify leaders, negotiate across incompatible interests, and discover why the settlements are starving.
- **Underneath both**, a Thanatos cult manufactures the famine while unknowingly serving an Iron Ring operation tied to the Black Eagle Barony.

The conspiracy is not a single-clue reveal. Evidence is distributed among the Shrine, the bugbear slave station, Khaeros, Skarn, Mara, the bandit camp, and the route itself. Let the party carry pieces they cannot initially interpret. Father Branislav and the Church of Traladar supply the religious and political legitimacy needed to understand what the state church cannot.

Keep the moral controls intact. Most cave populations are people under pressure, but some individuals remain murderous, some institutions cannot be made acceptable by better prices, and the Shrine is genuinely irreducible. If every creature is secretly good, the campaign becomes as flat as the alignment reading it is meant to replace.

### Running it in about eight hours

The repository includes a more generous [16-hour weekend schedule](campaign/weekend-cut-schedule.md). For a single fast eight-hour run, use it as a menu rather than a timetable.

Protect these beats:

- the Duke's contradictory commission;
- Brundt as first contact and proof that negotiation works;
- one substantial cave negotiation, preferably Khaeros or Karrgan;
- the bugbear confrontation and Skarn as the control against species-level judgment;
- Mara's visible chance to surrender;
- Vesna's suicide-ascension and the iron-ring evidence;
- one final conversation with Valerian that converts demonstrated peace into a food bridge.

Montage the Keep's shopping and minor cave factions. Cut the lizardfolk necklace mission, bandit camp, mad hermit, medusa, and most room-by-room exploration unless the players move unusually quickly. Abstract the Shrine's undead into waves or a timed pressure rather than resolving all 85 individually.

A workable eight-hour allocation is:

| Segment | Time |
|---|---:|
| Commission and arrival at the Keep | 45 minutes |
| Brundt and the Keep proof-of-concept | 75 minutes |
| Selected cave contacts and investigation | 3 hours |
| Shrine assault and aftermath | 2 hours |
| Buying the peace and horizon ending | 45 minutes |
| Breaks, rules lookup, and overrun | 15 minutes |
| **Total** | **8 hours** |

Use a visible clock. When a segment expires, summarize the unresolved portion and move to the next protected beat. Reaching the ending with unexplored caves is better than ending halfway through a complete dungeon crawl.

### Important campaign decisions still open

Before running, review the open decisions at the end of [Narrative Progression](narrative/progression.md#open-decisions-yours-to-make--flagged-seams). The most important is whether the hobgoblins' cannibalism is a response to scarcity or a cultural institution; that choice changes whether trade can solve their hardest problem.

Several names and setting interpretations are intentionally campaign-specific, and some profiles still mark provisional details. The [Rules section](rules/index.md) is currently empty. Use B/X or your preferred Basic-compatible rules for anything not specified here.

## Design and source notes

- B2 supplies the Keep, wilderness encounters, Caves, populations, treasures, and many behavioral details.
- Mystara's Karameikos supplies the political geography, Thyatian–Traladaran tension, churches, Black Eagle Barony, Iron Ring, and wider setting.
- This campaign supplies the diplomatic commission, manufactured-famine structure, named population, political economy, expanded character motives, and connective tissue between published elements.
- Canon and invention are identified in individual files where the distinction matters.

This is an evolving campaign notebook. Follow the links, use what survives contact with your table, and change anything that does not.
