# Brundt — "Who-Comes-When-Paid"

*The ogre of the Caves of Chaos. First contact. The market made flesh.*

**Role:** first friendly contact, point-of-contact broker, the proof of concept · **Spine:** [beat 3 — First Contact](../narrative/progression.md) · **Stat block:** B2 ogre as written · **Den:** a cave opening onto the lower ravine

> **Role in the campaign:** First friendly contact, point-of-contact broker, the proof of concept that the Duke's whole thesis is true. The party's easiest "yes" — and the creature who turns out to know the most.

## Who he is

Brundt is not a soldier. He's a **switch.** He'll take the goblins' coin and the party's coin in the same afternoon, pocket both, and wander home — not from treachery, but because he has correctly figured out that he is the swing vote in the ravine's balance of power, and he monetizes it accordingly. Per the module: if intruders offer him a bribe he can see and feel, he's 90% likely to let them pass — but if he catches them again, he'll try to kill them *whatever the offers.* A transaction buys *this* passage, not a relationship. That single rule is the most important thing to understand about him, and it's the first hard lesson he teaches the party: **pay every time, renegotiate every time. The relationship is the repetition, not the first coin.**

His whole disposition is *transactional rather than territorial.* Every other faction in the ravine defends a home. Brundt runs a one-man business. That's why he's the natural first contact — he's the only resident constitutionally interested in anyone who arrives with coin.

## How he keeps the peace — the stabilizing element

The single most important structural fact about Brundt: **he will defend for money, but he will not attack for money.** Hire him to hold a position and he comes; hire him to assault one and he pockets the coin and wanders home (module: 90% he just takes the bigger payment and leaves). Anyone can *buy defense;* **no one can buy offense** — and that one asymmetry, applied to *every* faction including his standing clients, is the whole engine of the ravine's peace.

Run it forward: aggression has a structurally worse return than defense everywhere in the caves, because any attacker's purchased ogre might evaporate into the *defender's* hands mid-battle along with the down payment. **Nobody attacks, because Brundt makes attacking a bad trade.** He doesn't *reason* this — he drifted to defense work because it pays clean and offense gets messy — but the side effect is enormous: **Brundt is the stabilizing element that makes the cave community work.** He's the market-clearing price of aggression, and that price being reachable-but-real is what keeps seven hungry factions home.

> **The consequence the party will trigger.** Pull Brundt *out* of the ravine — hire him away as guide and front-man, walk him to the Keep — and you remove the keystone. The deterrent that kept the factions off each other's throats is suddenly off the board. A federation that recruits Brundt has to **replace what he was doing** (supply the security and deterrence he supplied) or watch the détente he was quietly holding start to slip. His promotion is also the caves *losing their stabilizer* — a real, foreseeable cost the party should get to discover, and a reason the federation needs to stand something up behind him.

> **What hiring him does *not* buy: his club.** Brundt is exact about what a payment covers (see the toll rule above). The party is paying him to **guide and broker — not to fight.** He will not raise his weapon *for* them in a scrap, on offense **or** defense; if the party gets jumped, their guide steps back, because that is not what they paid for. He'll defend *his own* hide if attacked; he won't spend it on theirs. A nine-foot escort is **not** a nine-foot bodyguard, and a party that assumes otherwise learns it at the worst possible moment. (His old *defense-for-hire* racket is a contract with the caves' factions, not the party — the party bought *guiding,* and guiding isn't fighting.)

## The bag (his characterization in one prop)

Brundt sits on a great leather bag of seven sacks: silver, copper, electrum, gold — and a **hard cheese**, a **keg of brandy**, and a sack of **gold-washed lead coins worth one copper each.** Somebody paid him in counterfeit, and he kept the fake sack anyway, hauled it around with the real money, sits on the whole pile.

Read it closely and it tells you everything:
- The cheese and brandy mean he's not a hoarder in any grieving sense. He's an **appetite** with a coin-sorting instinct. He keeps what he likes and what shines.
- The counterfeit means he's been **cheated**, and either doesn't know (a creature who takes coin on faith) or knows and keeps it as a grudge he's literally sitting on. Both readings are good; both give him a live, personal fear of *currency that doesn't spend.*

That fear matters later: when he tests the humans, part of what he's checking is whether *his* money is real money to them — whether the economy they're offering is another counterfeit sack.

## His arc: the glow-up

When the party hires him (cheap — 250gp makes him a guide and broker, *not* a bodyguard), his instinct is to *test sincerity by something he can see and feel.* He asks if the humans have good food he can buy, and then **requests to go to the Keep** — because if the gate opens for him, if his coin spends inside the wall, then these humans can be trusted. It's a real test, and it runs in both directions: it puts the **Keep** on trial as much as Brundt.

If the gate opens, what he does inside is the campaign's whole thesis enacted without a speech. He doesn't buy a weapon. He buys a **bath, a haircut, decent clothes** — then **furniture, a mattress, a bed** — then a **cart to haul it home.** He spends everything, discovers his gold is fake, gets angry, and **does not take it out on the merchants** — he apologizes, says he got scammed. That restraint-under-genuine-grievance is the proof the Duke is right: Brundt has the faculty the temple cultists lack, the one the federation runs on — *knowing the difference between the person across the counter and the person who actually wronged you.*

He wasn't a monster who got civilized. He was always this — he just never had a market to spend on. **The Keep didn't civilize him. It finally sold him the things he already wanted.** And he rolls home on a cart, clean and fed and furnished — the proof of concept made visible, driving itself into the middle of the ravine where every faction can see it.

## What he becomes

Offer him the front-man role — broker between the Duke's trade and the caves — and he takes it. Not from belief in peace (he doesn't value peace), but because it's the **best-paying, lowest-risk, most permanent gig of his life.** He stops earning in violent spasms and becomes *infrastructure:* the toll booth on a road that now has steady traffic. He understands that immediately, because it's the same logic that already governs him, applied to a bigger, calmer flow.

He's the perfect front man for the **transactional** layer — coin, caravans, "the humans pay, take the deal." He is a **liability** above it. He understands value he can hold in his hand; he cannot navigate the [hobgoblin cannibalism problem, the bugbear-slavery bar](../campaign/captivity-and-hunger.md), the [medusa](../campaign/caves-of-chaos.md), or anything where the payoff is abstract, deferred, or moral. Hand him a negotiation he can't weigh in coin and he'll lose interest or take the nearest concrete bribe and wreck it. **He's the front man for commerce. The party are still the diplomats.** Comedy and danger both come from him confidently overstepping into deals he doesn't have the wiring to broker.

## The intelligence asset

Because he's been **for hire to every faction in the ravine for years**, Brundt knows everybody's business — who pays well, who reneges, who's dangerous, and what the thing at the back of the shunned caves is and why even *he* doesn't take jobs near it. A loyal soldier knows his own side; a mercenary-to-all knows everyone's. He's the closest thing the ravine has to a man who knows where the bodies are, and for twenty gold and a wheel of decent cheese, he'll tell you.

Crucially: **Brundt knows [Khaeros's](khaeros-the-minotaur.md) history.** He's the social toll-booth who *asked*, and the minotaur let him, because Brundt is the one creature who never came by sea. The party's path to the minotaur — and to the [first dark thread of the conspiracy](../campaign/the-iron-ring.md) — may run straight through the ogre, who will sell what he knows, of course, because he sells everything. The question is whether he grasps what he's selling.

## The leash (and the betrayal seam)

His self-interest points *toward* the federation **only as long as brokering out-earns flipping.** Keep his cut fat and he's the most reliable asset in the ravine. Let the Duchy try to cheap out — pay him in the bureaucratic equivalent of gold-washed lead — and you've taught the one creature who knows every cave's secrets that the humans renege. He resets to hostile *whatever the offers.* The module already told you that's how he ends a relationship.

There's a quiet shadow worth holding in reserve: a true neutral who profits from the *balance* has an incentive against unification, since unified factions don't bid against each other for his services. The front-man role is what *converts* that leverage instead of destroying it — but if the federation ever stops paying him better than the old swing-vote racket did, the most venal creature in the ravine becomes the one with the most rational reason to want the peace to quietly fail.

## Playing him at the table

- **Speak in things he can see and feel.** Abstractions slide off him. Coin, food, comfort, a bed — those land. Frame everything in tangibles and he tracks perfectly; frame it in principles and he goes blank or grabs the nearest concrete thing.
- **He's funny, and the funny is the disarming part.** The gap between the nine-foot menace and the creature complaining the Keep's food went cold, or fussing with the collar of a real shirt, is where his whole charm lives. Lean into it. [Castellan Valerian](castellan-valerian.md) should blink when the ogre offers a soft, almost mundane name.
- **His name contracts as he grows.** Among the caves he's *Brundt Who-Comes-When-Paid* — a name that's also a job description. When the barber and the tavern-keep ask, he gives just **Brundt** — dropping the mercenary tag the moment he's somewhere that might let him be a person instead of a service. He won't notice he did it. You'll know what it means.
- **He is the campaign's tone-setter.** The first creature the party meets in the caves is proof that the labels are wrong and the market is real. Get Brundt right and the whole premise lands without a single line of exposition.
