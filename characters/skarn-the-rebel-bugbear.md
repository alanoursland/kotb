# Skarn — The Rebel Bugbear

*The inch of daylight. The living proof that "bugbear" isn't a moral category — the cell is the Iron Ring, not the species. He won't be bound, and he won't bind.*

**Role:** the anti-essentialism safety valve; insider witness; the wedge into the bugbear fight · **Stat block:** B2 rebel bugbear (slave pen 41) · **Found:** chained among the captives of his own people

> **Role in the campaign:** Small entry, disproportionate weight. He's the structural guarantee that the bugbear storyline — the one *living* faction the party must destroy — stays a story about **allegiance and choice**, not about a monstrous race. Drop him and that storyline slides into the exact essentialism the whole campaign exists to refute. Keep him central and it holds.

## What the module gives you (B2 as written)

In **slave pen 41**, among the captives (3 hobgoblins, 2 gnolls, the berserk human "Hero," and him): **AC 7, HD 3+1, hp 14, Save F3, ML 9.** The text singles him out as an exception — the other pen-41 slaves flee if freed, but *"the big bugbear hates his fellows, and will take arms and fight against them or any of the other inhabitants of the whole area; he will continue to do so for as long as the party stays there."*

Straight from the module: **a bugbear, enslaved by his own kind, who will fight his own people and everyone else alongside the party for the duration of their stay.** Everything below just asks *why.*

---

## Why he refused (the soul)

The Iron Ring's own rules require members to swear a **Holy Oath**, and breaking ranks makes you **Hunted by the Iron Ring.** So Skarn is a bugbear who **refused or broke that oath** and is being punished exactly as the organization promises: chained in his own people's pen.

His refusal runs two ways, and they're the same refusal:

- **He won't be bound.** He would not swear himself — would not be *owned* by the oath, would not trade his autonomy for the Ring's support. A proud creature who refuses the collar in principle, even a collar offered as membership.
- **He won't bind.** He thinks his people are **wrong** — the slaving is wrong. He would not help fit collars onto others.

Put together: **a creature who hated bondage in both directions, inside an organization that traffics in nothing else.** The slavers who demand oaths caged the one who wouldn't swear; the Ring built to prevent defectors manufactured its purest enemy by its own central mechanism. That's not soft — it's *hard and principled.* His conscience and his pride are one thing, and it keeps him a character with a spine, not a sentimental convert.

---

## His single most important function

**He proves the species isn't the Iron Ring — *this cell* is.** The campaign introduces the bugbears as the one living faction that can't be integrated: unredeemable not by nature but by **freely chosen allegiance, eyes open.** That's a dangerous note — it sits one inch from "the bad monsters are just bad." Skarn is the inch of daylight. A bugbear *could* refuse. He did. He got chained for it. With him present, the bugbear storyline stays about *choice and allegiance;* without him, it collapses into monster-logic.

> **Make the safety valve redundant — DM note.** One defector can read as "the exception that proves the rule." A *pattern* proves the cell is the Ring, not the species. Keep **more than one** non-Iron-Ring bugbear visible: other bugbear captives in the pens, bugbears elsewhere on the borderland who never took the oath, rumor of a clan that refused. The line holds best when Skarn isn't a freak but the *nearest* example of a real distinction. See [The Iron Ring](../campaign/the-iron-ring.md) and the [bugbear roster entry](../campaign/caves-of-chaos.md).

## Free him and you get a defector, not a pet

Consistent with the campaign's anti-sentimental streak (freed slaves scatter, owe you nothing): Skarn doesn't fight for the party out of love or gratitude. He fights because he and the party **share an enemy** — bugbear slavers — and because he believes his people are wrong. That's **interest and conviction, not affection** — the federation's actual recruitment model in miniature. Treat him as an ally with his own reasons, never a rescued follower.

## The inside witness

The head cult-cleric is built to **suicide-ascend** rather than be interrogated, so the conspiracy can't be extracted from that direction. Skarn is the counterweight: a creature who was *inside* the bugbear Iron Ring operation. He can tell the party what the bugbears actually **are**, who buys the slaves, where the carts go — the [evidence trail](../campaign/the-iron-ring.md) made willing to talk, because he *wants* the slavers stopped. The bugbears built their own witness and put him in a cage.

## The wedge into the fight you can't win locally

The bugbears are the cave the party **can't** solve at the local level — can't afford to buy the slaves, can't trade their way in, have to fight (see [Captivity & Hunger](../campaign/captivity-and-hunger.md)). Skarn is the handle on that fight: the faction itself handed the party an **insider who wants the slavers gone as badly as they do.** Free him and you haven't freed a slave — you've recruited the **bugbear resistance.**

## Player challenges & opportunities (a design lens)

- **A combat multiplier the party earns by *thinking like the campaign.*** The bugbear fight is the hard "no." A party that frees Skarn first turns a brutal assault into an inside job — he knows the lair, fights at their side, and names the targets. The reward for treating a "monster" as a potential ally is *mechanical*, not just moral.
- **He makes the players argue with themselves.** Right after the campaign has taught them "the labels are wrong, give the caves a chance," the bugbears are the faction they have to *kill* — and Skarn is the voice in the room insisting that's still correct *and* that it isn't about being a bugbear. A great source of table tension: the exception that sharpens the rule instead of dissolving it.
- **A witness with a motive to talk.** Use him to deliver conspiracy truth the suicided cleric can't — but make the party *earn* his candor (free him, prove they mean to end the slavers, not just loot the pen). Information as a relationship.
- **The cost of using him: the Hunt.** He's *Hunted by the Iron Ring* for breaking ranks. Keeping him alive and visible can draw the Ring's attention the same way exposing Khaeros can — a defector and an escaped slave in the same ravine is two reasons for a Black Eagle reprisal. Stakes, if you want them.

## What he becomes (fate)

- **Recurring ally, conditionally (recommended).** His conscience makes him more than a one-fight defector — he could become a genuine voice in the federation: the bugbear who proves the federation's whole premise from inside the "unredeemable" faction. But keep it *earned and conditional* — he allies with people who share his enemy and respects those who don't try to own him. Try to command him like a hireling and you've become the thing he refused to swear to.
- **One-fight defector who vanishes (the leaner option).** He fights while the party stays (as the module says), then walks — Hunted, with his own road to run. A clean, hard ending that honors the anti-sentimental streak. Either works; the conscience version is the richer one.

## Running him at the table

- **Hard, plain, few words.** He's not warm and he's not grateful. He's *right*, and he knows it, and he's furious about what his people became. Let his disgust at the slavers carry the moral weight — he says the thing the party might be too polite to.
- **He bristles at being handled.** The fastest way to lose him is to treat him as a tool or a follower. He didn't refuse one oath to take another.
- **Keep him on the line of his thesis:** *the slaving is wrong, and being a bugbear has nothing to do with it.* That single sentence is his whole function — make sure it gets said.

---

*Everything above is interpretation layered on the B2 rebel bugbear as written — pen 41, the stats, the "hates his fellows / fights alongside you" exception. Nothing contradicts the module; it just asks why he hates them, and finds the answer the campaign keeps finding.*
