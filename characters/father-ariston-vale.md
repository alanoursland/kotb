# Father Ariston Vale — Priest of the Open Lantern

*The warm, trusted priest who is exactly what people wrongly fear Father Branislav might be: a witting servant of Thanatos hiding behind unfamiliar theology, genuine miracles, and impeccable kindness.*

**Role:** friendly infiltrator, intelligence-gatherer, and expedition saboteur · **Public faith:** Fellowship of the Open Lantern · **Claimed Immortal:** Seren, the Kindly Wayfarer · **True allegiance:** Thanatos · **Where:** [area 7b of the Keep](../locations/keep/07-private-apartments.md) · **Stat block:** B2 visiting Priest

---

## Quick reference

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who people meet** | A robust, silver-haired foreign priest who keeps a good fire, pours good wine, listens without interruption, and speaks plainly against evil. |
| **What he says he wants** | To spread a young western faith through hospitality, healing, and travel; to learn about Karameikos; to help decent people oppose the Caves of Chaos. |
| **What he actually wants** | To learn who might build a durable peace in the borderland, accompany them into danger, and make their attempt fail at the moment failure will cause the most fear and resentment. |
| **Voice** | Warm, attentive, lightly amused. He repeats a person's own words back in a kinder form. He almost never contradicts; he asks the question that lets someone justify what they already want to do. |
| **Immediate use** | A friendly source of drink, theology, sympathy, healing, and an apparently powerful cleric willing to join an expedition. |
| **The tell** | His faith speaks beautifully about ending pain and laying down burdens, but never clearly about rebuilding what evil has broken. |
| **If exposed** | He does not rant or confess. He tries to make the accuser look frightened and intolerant, escapes if he can, and abandons both name and religion afterward. |

## The identity he presents

Ariston says he comes from **Saragón**, near Ciudad Matacán on the far-western Savage Coast. His route east supposedly took him through Slagovich and then along years of caravan roads to Karameikos. The distance does most of the work for him: few people at the Keep know enough about Saragón to challenge a detail, and any oddity can be explained as translation, regional custom, or the novelty of his faith.

He calls himself a priest of the **Fellowship of the Open Lantern**, a new and deliberately missionary religion with no temples in Karameikos. Its priests travel instead of settling, keeping an open door wherever they lodge and gathering small circles of listeners rather than permanent congregations.

The Fellowship worships **Seren, the Kindly Wayfarer**, whom Ariston describes as a Saragóner physician and philosopher who attained Immortality within living memory. Seren supposedly crossed battle lines to treat the wounded, reconciled feuding families, and taught people to lay down hatreds they could no longer heal. The faith's youth explains why Branislav has never heard of it; Seren's recent ascent explains why no ancient religious text mentions the Immortal.

None of it is true.

**Ariston Vale is an adopted name.** His real birthplace and birth name are unknown, and need never be established. His only meaningful homeland is the network of roads on which he has practiced this deception. The divine power behind his spells is **Thanatos**.

## What people know before meeting him

- He comes to the Keep regularly but is not permanently assigned there.
- He is generous with ale and wine and welcomes visitors into comfortable rooms.
- He discusses theology with anyone interested but does not press unwilling listeners.
- He is outspoken in his hatred of evil.
- He has traveled farther than anyone else presently at the Keep.
- Father Branislav's three acolytes admire him. Branislav himself does not.
- His two assistants, Corin and Sera, are under a religious vow of silence.

Almost everyone speaks well of him. The silence of his acolytes makes people uncomfortable, but Ariston answers that discomfort so gracefully that questioning it begins to feel rude.

## The Fellowship of the Open Lantern

### Public doctrine

Ariston teaches three principles:

1. **Every stranger is owed shelter and a hearing.**
2. **No traveler should carry a burden alone.**
3. **What cannot be healed must eventually be released without hatred.**

These are compassionate teachings when heard once. Ariston corrupts them through application, not wording. A duty becomes a burden. A difficult ally becomes someone who cannot be healed. A failed negotiation becomes proof that further effort should be released. Death becomes the final end of pain.

He almost never urges violence directly. He encourages people to stop preserving things: a promise, an alliance, a prisoner, a settlement, or hope itself.

### Symbol and customs

The Fellowship's sign is an **open silver lantern above a road**. Ariston wears it openly. The gold chain beneath it is real and valuable; its bloodstone is concealed against his chest.

Its chief private rite is the **Laying Down of Burdens**. A visitor tells the priest whom they fear, whom they resent, what they regret, which duty exhausts them, and where they expect to travel next. Ariston presents this as confidential pastoral care. It gives him an excellent map of grievances, vulnerabilities, plans, and routes.

Corin and Sera are supposedly observing the **Discipline of Listening**, a year-and-a-day silence required before they can preach. Followers say that a person must learn to hear another's burdens before offering guidance.

### Useful sayings

- “Set it down for tonight. It will still be there tomorrow if it truly belongs to you.”
- “Seren asks first where the pain may end.”
- “A closed door judges the traveler before hearing the knock.”
- “Not every broken thing is made kinder by being kept.”
- “The last mercy is release.”

The first three sound wise. The last two are closest to what he really believes.

## How to play him

- **Give the other person the conversation.** Ariston asks names, remembers details, and follows up later. He speaks less than whoever visits him.
- **Make his kindness materially useful.** He offers warmth, wine, discreet counsel, and help on the road. If his charm has no cost to him, it will feel fake; let him spend coin and effort maintaining it.
- **Validate the worst reasonable interpretation.** He never invents a ludicrous fear. He takes an existing doubt and supplies the calm, compassionate argument for surrendering to it.
- **Oppose obvious cruelty.** He is genuinely convincing when condemning torture, slavery, or slaughter. Thanatos does not require him to approve every evil; destroying trust between people is more valuable than defending any particular villain.
- **Never leer at his own deception.** He believes despair is clarity and dissolution is mercy. His serenity is not entirely an act.
- **Do not make foreignness the clue.** Saragón and the new religion explain why he seems unfamiliar; they are camouflage, not evidence of guilt.

## What he wants from the party

Ariston first determines whether the player characters are merely another armed expedition or whether they might create something lasting. He is most interested in:

- who makes the group's decisions;
- who argues for negotiation;
- who fears betrayal;
- which cave factions they consider redeemable;
- whether Branislav, Demetrios, Valerian, or the Merchants' Guild supports them;
- where and when the next expedition will go;
- what single failure would discredit the entire project.

If they intend only to raid the caves, he may help them sincerely enough to preserve his cover. If they begin building trust, trade, rescue arrangements, or a federation, he offers to accompany them.

His purpose is not to save the Shrine, the Iron Ring, Bargle, or the Black Eagle Baron. He is a separate servant of Thanatos and may happily let any of them fall. He attacks **the possibility that something durable might replace them**.

## The betrayal

Use the module's instruction: Ariston betrays the expedition during a crucial encounter with monsters. For this campaign, the strongest moment is not an ordinary fight. It is a negotiation, hostage release, evacuation, or first joint operation whose failure can be blamed on the caves.

Good choices:

- Cast *silence, 15' radius* when one sentence could prevent violence.
- Use *hold person* on the party member visibly restraining an ally from attacking.
- Cast reversed *light* to blind the person carrying a truce sign, opening a sudden misunderstanding.
- Use *cause light wounds* while hidden in the confusion, making it appear that a nearby cave-dweller struck first.
- Have Corin or Sera block a retreat, destroy evidence, or kill the one witness who understands what happened.

Ariston's ideal result is not simply dead adventurers. It is survivors returning to the Keep convinced that peace was naive, while the caves receive an equally convincing story of human treachery.

Do not preserve him with plot armor. If the party exposes or kills him early, reward them. Thanatos loses this hand, and Branislav's judgment is vindicated.

## Corin and Sera

Both acolytes are witting assistants, not deceived converts. Their silence keeps them from contradicting Ariston's invented history, reveals nothing about their accents, and lets him interpret every gesture on their behalf.

**Corin** is the visible shield: broad-shouldered, shaved-headed, serene, and usually positioned where he can intercept a sudden approach. His eyes move whenever Ariston appears most relaxed. He handles animals, heavy baggage, and physical intimidation that can still be mistaken for quiet competence.

**Sera** is the precaution: strong-featured, dark-haired, and outwardly tranquil. She watches food preparation, locks, windows, and escape routes. She remembers what guests touch and notices anyone searching the chambers. If Ariston's cover fails, she destroys papers and secures the retreat while Corin holds the door.

They communicate through ordinary hand signs disguised as the habits of a silent order. When alone, they speak normally.

## Branislav's suspicion

Father Branislav has no proof that Seren is fictitious and does not claim that every unknown western Immortal must be false. What troubles him is the shape of Ariston's theology.

- Ariston gives thanks for **the ending of pain**, not the restoration of life.
- He calls obligations burdens more often than he calls them duties.
- His stories end with departure, surrender, disappearance, or death; there is never a dawn after the lantern.
- He can describe Seren's acts but gives slippery answers about teachers, witnesses, dates, and the Fellowship's first congregation.
- His supposed novices admire him, obey him, and defend him, but never display affection for Seren independently of him.
- Branislav recognizes the faint spiritual cold of Entropy without being able to demonstrate its source.

If pressed by someone he trusts, Branislav says:

> “His lantern illuminates the road, but he never speaks of sunrise.”

Ariston treats Branislav with faultless respect. He never attacks the old priest openly. Instead, he tells others that Branislav's long struggle against darkness may have made him see corruption everywhere.

## If questioned about Seren

Ariston has practiced answers to the obvious challenges.

| Question | Ariston's answer |
|---|---|
| Why has no one heard of Seren? | Seren ascended only within living memory, and the Fellowship has not yet traveled this far east. |
| Where are Seren's temples? | The first followers were travelers and healers. “A lantern belongs beside the road, not locked in a sanctuary.” |
| Why do established priests not recognize the faith? | Mortal institutions are always slower than an Immortal's call. He expresses sympathy rather than contempt. |
| Can Ariston prove his power? | Yes. His clerical magic is genuine, although Thanatos supplies it. |
| Where are the scriptures? | He carries selected copied “Letters of Seren,” written in a western hand and translated by him. They are fabricated devotional commonplaces, not overtly evil texts. |
| Why are the acolytes silent? | The Discipline of Listening prevents novices from mistaking eloquence for wisdom. |
| Why does he travel armed and armored? | A road-priest who opposes evil must survive long enough to shelter others. |

## Stats and possessions — B/X

Use the visiting Priest from B2 area 7b as written. Treat him as a **3rd-level cleric** if a level is required; the module provides his combat details but does not state one explicitly.

- **AC -1:** *plate mail +1*, *shield +1*, Dexterity 15.
- **hp 18.**
- **Weapon:** *mace +1* (1d6+1).
- **Prepared magic:** reversed *cure light wounds* (*cause light wounds*, 2–7 damage on a successful touch attack) and *light*, used to hinder or harm.
- **Scroll:** *hold person* and *silence, 15' radius*.
- **On his person:** 4d6 sp and a 100-gp gold chain set with a 500-gp bloodstone.
- **Hidden in his chair:** 30 pp, 30 gp, 30 ep, 30 sp, 30 cp, and a jeweled clasp worth 300 gp. This is his fund for bribery, subversion, or escape.

**Corin and Sera:** Cleric 1; AC 4 (chain mail and shield); hp 7 each; mace (1d6); ML 7; 4d6 sp and a 100-gp gold chain each.

Their comfortable suite, open hospitality, and conspicuous generosity are operational expenses. Ariston understands that trust is cheaper than force.

## If his cover breaks

Ariston's first defense is social. He asks for evidence, welcomes examination, and gently frames the accusation as fear of an unfamiliar foreign religion. If direct proof appears, he uses silence, darkness, confusion, bribery, and his acolytes to escape. He does not fight to the death unless escape is impossible.

If he gets away, “Father Ariston Vale” and the Open Lantern disappear. A similarly attentive traveler may surface later under another name, another young Immortal, and another doctrine fitted to local fears. If captured, he protects Thanatos's wider purposes by insisting he is merely a fraud who invented Seren for money and influence. Even his confession is a smaller lie designed to hide the larger truth.
