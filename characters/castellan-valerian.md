# Castellan Valerian — Commander of the Keep

*The good soldier who'd rather do it the old way — and salutes and complies when the answer is no. The loyal opposition. The gate on the whole political track.*

**Role:** the secular power of the Keep; the man whose buy-in the peace requires; the in-fiction skeptic who pressure-tests the party's idealism · **Stat block:** B2 Castellan (area 26), high-level Fighter · **Heritage:** Thyatian · **Spine:** [beats 2 & 7](../narrative/progression.md)

---

## Quick reference

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | A career Thyatian soldier who commands the Keep and is charged with keeping the borderland road safe. A genuinely good man. |
| **Where he stands** | He thinks the Duke's peace-with-monsters plan is naive and risky. His instinct is the clean solution: muster the garrison, raid the caves, end the raids by force. |
| **What he does anyway** | Argues his case up the chain — then **complies.** Disagreement *and* obedience, both sincere. He's the loyal opposition, never the saboteur. |
| **What he won't say** | He likely knows more about the **Black Eagle Baron** than he admits. He looks — but he won't speak, for political survival. |
| **How to move him** | **Results, not rhetoric.** You don't talk him into the peace; you *show* him it works. |

## Who he is

Valerian is a soldier, and everything about him follows from that. He's spent a career holding lines and burying men who held them with him, and he measures every plan by one question: *how many of my people does it get killed?* By that measure, the Duke's commission is a bad plan. Diplomacy with raiders is slow, uncertain, and stakes innocent lives — travelers on his road, merchants, his own garrison — on the goodwill of creatures that have been *killing* those people. The raid is fast, certain, and within his competence. From his chair, "open relations with the monsters" sounds like rewarding the killers, and "feed the caves" sounds like turning a dependable military supply line into an open-ended obligation to former enemies.

He is **not bloodthirsty and not a brute.** He's risk-averse about the lives he's responsible for, and skeptical of an unproven theory dreamed up far from the frontier. That skepticism is *reasonable* — and that's what makes him valuable. He says out loud the strongest case against the entire campaign, and a party that can't answer him may not have earned the peace yet.

And then — this is the whole man — **he obeys.** He'll argue hard, and when the Duke's word stands, he salutes and gives the party real support. His loyalty is to the office and the realm, not to any faction or his own preference. He won't undermine a mission he disagrees with; he'll enable it and hope to be proven wrong, or proven right. Either teaches him something.

## The loyal opposition (how he cooperates)

Valerian gives the party what the Duke's sanction entitles them to — quarters, the Keep's intelligence, access, a base — *while* making his disagreement plain. Like the Duke, he's also **testing them.** If these envoys prove competent and the raids actually fall, he can be won. If they're naive and people die on his road, he'll feel vindicated and press harder for the assault. He is persuadable, but only by **outcomes**: the raids dropping, [Brundt](brundt-the-ogre.md) rolling home from the Keep clean and fed, a tangible reason to believe.

> **He's the human-side control, the way [Skarn](skarn-the-rebel-bugbear.md) is the monster-side one.** Skarn insists the bugbears must be fought *and* that it isn't about being a bugbear; Valerian insists the raids must be stopped *and* doubts that talking will do it. Both keep the campaign honest from opposite directions. Don't let the party hand-wave past him.

## What he knows about the Baron (and won't say)

A military man on the southwestern frontier has *seen things* — smuggling on the roads, the Baron's men where they shouldn't be, the lawlessness bleeding out of [Halag](../campaign/the-borderland.md). Valerian has suspicions about the **Black Eagle Baron.** But he keeps his mouth shut, and his reasons are sound: it's political, it's above his station, the Baron is the Duke's *cousin*, and a Castellan who accuses a Baron without ironclad proof ends his career — or his life.

This makes him a **gated, secondary evidence source.** He won't volunteer a word — but bring him proof, or earn his real confidence, and he can **corroborate**: confirm the corridor, point at the cove, validate what [Khaeros](khaeros-the-minotaur.md) and the [iron-ring clues](../campaign/the-iron-ring.md) only imply. He is the Duke's blind spot one rung down, inverted: the Duke *won't look*; Valerian *does* look and **won't speak.** A different silence, born of duty and self-preservation rather than denial — and a silence the party can break with trust.

## Valerian and the Curate — the two powers of the Keep

The Keep runs on two people: Valerian (secular, martial, Thyatian, state-aligned) and **[Father Branislav](the-curate.md)** (sacred, old-faith, Traladaran, distrusted). They co-hold the only keys to the chapel's hidden treasury — an established working relationship and a mutual check. They needn't *like* each other; between them they hold the Keep's sword and its conscience, and the party's political game in [beats 2 and 7](../narrative/progression.md) is navigating both. Win one without the other and the peace is half-built.

His right hand at the wall is **[Captain Bohdan, "the Wall"](captain-bohdan.md)** — the garrison's biggest man, who runs the gate and the watch and carries out Valerian's orders where the spears meet the road. Bohdan is also the likelier of the two to *warm* to the federation first (he walks Brundt through the market and comes out changed) — which makes the captain a quiet lever on his own commander.

## Player challenges & opportunities (a design lens)

- **A persuade-by-results NPC, not a persuade-by-speech one.** No Charisma check unlocks Valerian. His buy-in is *earned in the ravine* — fewer raids, the proof-of-concept ogre, Khaeros as living evidence. This is a feature: it **welds the two tracks together.** Success in the caves is what moves the Keep, so the political win is downstream of the diplomatic one.
- **He's the skeptic who pressure-tests the players.** He articulates the best argument against the whole premise — *"these raiders killed my people; why are we feeding them?"* If the table can't answer him, that's information, not an obstacle. Let him be the honest friction that makes the eventual peace *mean* something.
- **A gated path to the conspiracy, with teeth.** Earning his testimony is a real objective — a fourth, independent line on the Baron (with Khaeros, the rings, Skarn). And getting a Castellan to speak against a Baron is itself a fraught political act with consequences the party sets in motion.
- **The supply-line sandbox.** The Keep is regularly and adequately supplied, but feeding the caves changes who the ducal convoys are for; *he* has to authorize, account for, and defend that change. That's a live source of complications — a resentful sergeant, merchant objections, convoy capacity, sabotage, and soldiers asking why former raiders receive ducal grain — that the party's choices can worsen or ease.
- **The muscle for the climax.** Won over, Valerian's garrison is the military force that can take the Shrine and back the [Duke–Baron confrontation](../campaign/the-iron-ring.md). His buy-in gates whether the party has *real troops* — pair it with the Curate's "holy contingent" and the composition of that force becomes the set-piece.
- **The bigger ask: hiring the minotaur.** Where Brundt is a day-trip, [Khaeros](khaeros-the-minotaur.md) wants a *permanent post on the guard* — a resident armed minotaur, which is the hardest "yes" the Keep can give. It's another persuade-by-results room (garrison strength over sentiment; a trial, not a tenure), and winning it moves Valerian from *permitting* an experiment to *staffing his garrison* with the federation's logic. → **[The Minotaur at the Keep](../campaign/the-minotaur-at-the-keep.md)**
- **The Brundt test, in his face.** When the ogre comes to the Keep for a bath and a bed, *Valerian is the man who has to permit it and watch it.* His reaction — disgust, grudging curiosity, a hairline crack in the skepticism — is a live barometer of where he's at, and what Brundt does with the fake coins is the *result* that moves him. A great scene that does real character work. → **[The Ogre Goes to the Keep](../campaign/the-ogre-goes-to-the-keep.md)**

## Running him at the table

- **Blunt, professional, respectful.** He says what he thinks plainly and then does his duty. Never a cartoon hawk; a competent man with a defensible position.
- **He argues, then salutes.** Voice the dissent strongly — and then *comply.* He does not sabotage; the institution working as designed is his whole role. If you want opposition that *fights* the party, that's the [visiting Priest](the-curate.md), not Valerian.
- **Frame things as a soldier hears them.** Safety, supply, defensible ground, holding what you can't garrison — he engages. Reframe tribute as **payroll for patrol work** (hiring auxiliaries to hold ground he can't) and you're speaking his language; that's the move most likely to win him to the food bridge.
- **He won't talk Baron easily.** Hold it close. Make the party earn every word, and let the cost of his speaking be real.
- **He is exactly what he appears.** In a campaign of hidden identities and mistaken labels, Valerian is the one major figure with nothing to uncover. His only secret he keeps from *duty,* not deceit. Let that steadiness read as its own kind of integrity.

## Name & identity

**Castellan Valerian** — a Thyatian officer's name. No hidden self, no second name, no mask: he's the campaign's fixed point, the straight edge everything crooked is measured against. (Swap the name if you like; the role is what matters.)
