# Lucan Vael — the Mad Hermit

*The third study in intelligence gone solitary. One of the Duke's own kind — **ill, not evil.** The case with no upside, and therefore the purest test of whether the federation is about more than utility.*

**Role:** the federation's care-category diagnostic; the human cost of the cycle made a face · **Stat block:** B2 Mad Hermit + mountain lion · **Where:** deep in the borderland woods

> **What he asks:** every other creature in this campaign can be sorted by *ally or threat.* Lucan is neither — he produces nothing, reciprocates nothing, and is occasionally dangerous without ever being evil. He forces the question the whole federation rests on: **is there a place for someone you can't use and shouldn't kill?** A society that has no answer for Lucan has no answer for anyone who stops being useful.

## Who he was, and how he broke

Lucan Vael was exactly the man the Duke's project is *for*: a settler who took the Duke's dream at its word and built a homestead on the wild frontier, betting his family's future on the promise that the borderland could be made safe. He had a wife, a daughter — **Anneke** — a few fields clawed out of the margin, a life.

Then the raids came. The hungry caves, starved by [the manufactured famine](../campaign/the-iron-ring.md) and driven to the road, hit the outlying steadings — and the Vael place was one of them. Lucan survived; his family did not. He came home to ash and silence, and something in him came loose that no cleric has set right since. He walked into the deep woods and did not walk out. A mountain lion took up with him, or he with it — the one bond left to a man who can no longer hold a human one — and now he lairs in the wild, raving, dangerous in his episodes, harmless in his grief, keening a dead girl's name at the trees.

**He is the human cost of the cycle the party is trying to break.** And he indicts every easy answer:

- You can't cleanly *side with the caves* — their raid killed this innocent man and his child.
- You can't cleanly *exterminate the caves* — they were starving victims of the cult, and wiping them out just makes more Lucans on the other side of the ledger.
- The only answer that honors Lucan is to **stop the engine** — the famine, the cult, the thing upstream — not to pick a side to blame. He's where the campaign's "follow it to the root" becomes personal: everyone here, human and monster, is downstream of Thanatos.

## The three solitaries (and the four corners)

Lucan completes the set the caves are built around — three minds isolated by three different things:

- **[Khaeros](khaeros-the-minotaur.md)** — isolated by *monstrosity* (everyone attacks him on sight).
- **[Lysandra](lysandra-the-medusa.md)** — isolated by a *curse* that makes proximity lethal.
- **Lucan** — isolated by *madness*, from the inside.

And against the prisoners' human "Hero," he completes the label/behavior matrix the whole campaign decorrelates:

| | **Behaves well / innocent** | **Behaves dangerously / harmful** |
|---|---|---|
| **Human** | **Lucan** (ill, not evil) | [**"Sir Davric"**](../campaign/the-prisoners.md) (the murderer) |
| **"Monster"** | [**Khaeros**](khaeros-the-minotaur.md) (good, traumatized) | [**Lysandra**](lysandra-the-medusa.md) (dangerous, wronged) |

All four cells filled. Label predicts nothing; only behavior, and behavior is made by circumstance.

## The diagnostic — why he's the hardest case

Every integration in the ravine has a *payoff.* Kobold engineers, hobgoblin discipline, gnoll labor, the medusa's potion, even Brundt's market sense — the federation can be argued for on **utility.** Lucan blows that argument up, because there is **no utility here at all.** He can't work, can't fight (reliably or safely), can't reciprocate, can't even reliably know you're there. He is pure cost.

So he's the test the federation can't fake its way through. Sparing a *useful* kobold proves nothing about your values; it's just good business. Finding a place for **Lucan** — a man who offers the society *nothing* and occasionally endangers it — is the only thing that proves the federation is about more than profit. And he's **human, the Duke's own kind,** so the party can't retreat into "well, he's just a monster." The question is asked in a mirror.

> If the campaign's thesis is "look at who got pushed to the bottom," Lucan is the bottom of the bottom: not exploited, just *discarded* — too broken to be worth anything to anyone. What a society does with him is what it actually believes.

## Possible solutions

There is no clean win here, and that's the point. Options, roughly best-to-worst for *honoring* him:

- **Care, not cure (the truest answer).** Place him in sanctuary — the **[Church of Traladar](the-curate.md)** under Branislav, a monastery, a holy house. Wild Traladar (gothic) has a real tradition of hermits and holy fools; a protected, dignified place where productivity is *not required* is dignity without utility. This is the federation discovering it has a **welfare/charity category** — the third option beyond ally-or-exterminate. The campaign's quiet moral capstone.
- **Reach him through the lion.** Don't charge the man who attacks strangers; approach through the one bond he has left. Patience, no threat, the cat as the channel. He can perhaps be *calmed* and led to safety even if never healed. (Killing the lion, conversely, is the fastest way to lose him entirely.)
- **Lucid intervals and the keepsake.** He surfaces, sometimes — a flicker of Lucan in the madness. A party that learns his story might recover something of his family (a token from the ruined steading, the truth of what happened, a proper burial for his dead) and give him not sanity but **peace.** Sometimes rest is the only mercy on offer, and it's a real one.
- **Cure (and the question it raises).** High enough clergy might restore a broken mind in theory — but ask whether it's *kind.* Heal Lucan and he wakes to the full, fresh weight of Anneke's death with nothing dulled. **Cure may be crueler than care.** Let the party wrestle with that; there's no obviously right answer.
- **The failure mode: extermination.** The [Castellan's](castellan-valerian.md) instinct — and B2's default — is that the mad thing in the woods is a wilderness threat to put down. It's the *easy* read, and choosing otherwise for a useless, occasionally-dangerous human is the single clearest sign the party has internalized what the campaign teaches. If they put him down without a second thought, that's information too.

## Player challenges & opportunities (a design lens)

- **The test that has no reward.** Make sure the party gets *nothing* material for helping Lucan — no XP-worthy foe defeated, no treasure that matters, no useful ally gained. That barrenness is the encounter's whole power: it asks whether they'll spend effort on someone who can never pay it back. Don't bail them out with a prize.
- **He makes the caves harder and truer.** Meeting Lucan *after* (or during) brokering with the caves complicates the peace honestly — the party is making terms with people whose raid did *this.* Don't resolve the discomfort. The mature answer (stop the engine, not the people) is something they have to reach, not be handed.
- **A gut-check disguised as a wandering monster.** B2 files him as a hostile wilderness encounter. Let the party meet him that way — and let the keened name, the protective lion, the flicker of Lucan be the cracks through which they might see he's a person, *if they're paying the attention the campaign rewards.*
- **A use for the clergy you've assembled.** Branislav, Demetrios, a monastery — Lucan is where the campaign's "contingent of holy people" does something other than fight a cult: it provides *care.* A nice inversion of what clergy are usually *for* at the table.

---

## Running him at the table

- **Frightening first, human underneath.** He's genuinely dangerous in his episodes — play the threat straight (the lion, the raving, the unpredictability). The humanity is something the party has to *find,* not something he offers.
- **The name in the noise.** Among the gibberish, one word comes clear and keeps coming: *Anneke.* Let an attentive player catch it and pull the thread.
- **Lucid flickers, not lucid conversations.** He doesn't explain himself. He surfaces for a sentence, a look, a moment of terrible clarity, then goes back under. Less is more.
- **Don't cure him on stage for a clean ending.** Whatever the party does, let it cost something and leave something unhealed. He's a wound the campaign doesn't get to close cheaply.

---

## Name & identity

**Lucan Vael** — a settler's name, the Duke's own kind. The locals call him only "the mad hermit"; the name and the household behind it (Vael, a steading that no longer exists) are gone with everything else, surfacing only when he answers, rarely, to *Lucan.* Like the cast's other lost-name beats, his is the proof that the broken thing in the woods was a *person* — a man with a farm, a wife, and a daughter named Anneke. Restore the name, even just by using it, and you've done him a kindness the world stopped offering long ago.

*Built on B2's Mad Hermit (and his mountain lion) as written — a hostile wilderness encounter. The backstory, the name, and his role as the federation's care-diagnostic are this campaign's; nothing contradicts the module, which never says *why* he's mad.*
