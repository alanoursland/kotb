# Mara Sokol — the Newest Recruit

*A Traladaran patriot who followed Vesna toward the same door—but has been inside only eleven days, not long enough to stop seeing the threshold. The one cultist who can still choose not to cross it.*

**Role:** Vesna's reachable counterfactual, surviving witness, and playable mercy · **Heritage:** Traladaran · **From:** Luln · **Class/Level:** Cleric 1 · **Where:** Shrine room 54, among the four acolytes · **Spine:** [beat 6](../narrative/progression.md)

---

## Quick reference

| | |
|---|---|
| **Who** | A 22-year-old former chapel novice from Luln, newly recruited into Vesna's liberation cult after her younger brother disappeared on the western road. |
| **What she wants** | To make somebody answer for the people who disappear while officials explain why nothing can be done. |
| **Why she joined** | Vesna named the injustice accurately, treated her anger as courage rather than sin, and offered action when the Church and the Duke offered patience. |
| **Why she can still leave** | She has served in the Shrine for only eleven days. She has helped the cult, but has not killed, sacrificed anyone, received a spell from Thanatos, or completed the rite that binds her to him. |
| **What stops her** | Shame. Leaving means admitting that her brother's memory led her into something monstrous—and facing punishment from both the cult and the Duchy. |

| **Voice** | Direct, intelligent, and raw. She is not meek and does not ask to be told the Duke is good. When frightened, she becomes precise. |
| **Opening in combat** | She hangs back and does not attack in the first round unless attacked. Another acolyte shouts her name and orders her forward, making the hesitation visible. |

## Who she was

Mara grew up in **Luln**, close enough to the Black Eagle Barony that refugees, disappearances, and official helplessness were ordinary facts of life. Her mother mended sacks and heavy clothes; her father burned charcoal when there was work. Mara learned her letters at a small Church of Traladar chapel and stayed as a novice, keeping accounts, carrying food to displaced families, sitting with the sick, and preparing the dead for burial.

She was good at the work but increasingly furious at its limits. The chapel could feed a refugee for one night. It could not stop the men who made refugees. It could record a missing name. It could not make a magistrate search for that person.

Her younger brother **Deyan** disappeared while carrying two baskets of charcoal along the western road. His mule returned with a cut trace. No body was found. A local official recorded *robbery or wilderness loss* and advised the family not to risk another person searching dangerous country.

Mara did not lose faith in Halav, Petra, or Zirchev. She lost faith in people who invoked them while doing nothing.

## How Vesna found her

Vesna met Mara while she was asking questions other people had stopped answering. They knew which grievances were real because they shared them. They did not tell Mara to calm herself, forgive, or wait for lawful authority. They said:

> “They call patience a virtue when it is your family doing the waiting.”

Vesna presented the cult first as a Traladaran resistance network: people willing to protect villages, punish collaborators, and make the Thyatian administration fear the cost of indifference. They never promised Mara comfort. They promised that what happened to Deyan would matter.

That was enough.

Mara carried messages for several weeks before being brought to the Shrine. She has been inside for **eleven days**. The speed matters: everything is still strange, still difficult to excuse, and still compared in her mind with the chapel she left.

## What she has done

Mara is reachable, not innocent.

- She copied patrol times and caravan observations supplied by sympathizers.
- She carried food, lamp oil, and grave wrappings into the Shrine.
- She stood watch while undead left through the caverns at night.
- She marked two cave storehouses on a rough map after being told they held goods stolen from Traladaran settlers.
- She cleaned rust-colored stains from the temple floor and chose not to ask what had happened there.
- She has worn the iron ring on its cord and repeated the cult's liberation oaths.
- If the Shrine is attacked, she initially takes her place beside the other acolytes.

She has **not** killed anyone, animated or commanded undead, assisted at a sacrifice, tortured a prisoner, or received divine magic from Thanatos. Vesna has scheduled her **First Severing** for the next temple rite. Mara has only recently learned that this initiation requires her to take part in a captive's sacrifice and speak Thanatos's name as her patron.

She has been telling herself that a weapon need not be good if it is aimed at something worse. She no longer believes that sentence, but she has not yet found the courage to say so aloud.

## Deyan — the truth Mara does not know

The Iron Ring took Deyan. He passed through the bugbear slave-station and was sent west toward the Black Eagle Barony. Whether he is alive is deliberately unresolved.

Vesna does not know this. The cult did not target Mara through secret knowledge of her brother; it simply recruits from the suffering its own masters create. That is worse. The machine took Deyan, ignored Mara's grief, and then offered her a place inside itself under the banner of resistance.

If you want the connection to become physical evidence, put **“D. Sokol”** or a charcoal-burner's mark beside one entry on a bugbear tally or slave manifest. Do not require this clue to save Mara. It is a later revelation, not the key to her humanity.

## The encounter in room 54

Mara is one of the four 1st-level acolytes in B2 room 54. The other three have passed their initiations and fight normally. One of them wears the room's *amulet of protection from good* and commands the group.

### Make the opening visible

On the first round, the senior acolyte shouts:

> “Mara! Shut the door and take your place!”

Mara moves toward the door or raises her mace defensively, but **does not attack unless someone attacks her first**. Describe the oversized robe, uncertain grip, and the flash of an old wooden Traladaran token when her iron ring swings aside.

This is the party's invitation. Do not hide it behind a perception check.

### What reaches her

Any one of these, paired with a credible offer of protection or surrender, can make her lower the mace:

- A priest of the Church of Traladar names the Shrine as a corruption and addresses her as one of the faithful rather than a traitor.
- The party explains or shows that the cult's undead are destroying food and causing the famine.
- A cave-dweller whom the party has befriended identifies one of the marked storehouses as their own food, not stolen human goods.
- The party has spared another enemy or protected a prisoner when killing would have been easier.
- Someone asks what her First Severing requires and gives her time to answer.
- The party simply offers surrender after she visibly refuses an order to strike.

No Charisma roll is required when the players give her both **a contradiction she can recognize** and **a way to survive choosing differently**. If they offer only argument, make a reaction or morale roll. If they offer only threats, she assumes capture means execution and fights or flees.

### What drives her deeper

- Calling Traladarans ignorant, conquered, or naturally disloyal.
- Insisting the Duke's good intentions erase the grievance.
- Killing a surrendering cultist.
- Demanding that she prove herself by immediately attacking Vesna.
- Promising unconditional pardon when the speaker plainly lacks authority to grant it.

Mara can abandon the cult without suddenly loving the Duchy. Saving her means giving her a better action, not winning an argument about history.

## If the party is moving fast

Run the entire mercy beat in three moments:

1. The senior acolyte orders, “Mara, strike!” She freezes.
2. A player offers surrender or names the famine. Mara drops the mace and says, “They told us those stores were stolen.”
3. After Vesna's death, Mara says, “They did not know who sent the orders. They thought the west was funding us because it believed in Traladara.”

That is enough to preserve her function in a compressed Shrine.

## Her relationship with Vesna

Mara admires Vesna. They were kind to her, remembered Deyan's name, and never mocked her anger. They are also the person who led her here.

She cannot talk Vesna down. Vesna sees Mara's hesitation as the ordinary terror before commitment and tries to carry her across it. During the climax, they may command Mara by name, forgive her fear, and promise that the First Severing will make everything clear. That tenderness should make the scene worse, not make Vesna redeemable.

If Mara defects, Vesna is not enraged. They are sorrowful:

> “You were almost free.”

Mara's answer, if she can manage one:

> “No. I was almost gone.”

When Vesna suicide-ascends, Mara is horrified and grief-stricken. She does not celebrate the party's victory. She has lost the person who exploited her and the person who first made her feel heard; both are true.

## What she can testify to

Mara supplies facts, not the solution to the conspiracy.

| She knows | She does not know |
|---|---|
| The Shrine deliberately sent undead to destroy cave food stores and game. | That the famine ultimately serves the Black Eagle Baron. |
| Vesna described the destruction as denying supplies to future ducal auxiliaries and provoking resistance to Thyatian expansion. | That Bargle is the intelligence behind the operation. |
| Orders, coin, equipment, and iron-ring pendants arrived from “friends beyond the southern fens,” sometimes through the bugbears. | The name or structure of the Iron Ring. |
| Vesna sincerely believed those patrons supported Traladaran liberation. | Who those patrons really were. |
| The cult's inner rites invoke Thanatos and teach that Entropy can be turned against an occupying order. | Thanatos's larger purpose, or how completely Vesna had been deceived. |
| Which undead used which exits, which stores were marked, and when the next night raids were planned. | Where every courier went after leaving the ravine. |
| Vesna intended to die rather than be captured and called that death an ascent. | Exactly what Vesna would become or how to prevent the return. |

Her testimony proves that the famine was intentional and that Vesna was not receiving orders from any recognized Traladaran authority. It does **not** by itself prove the Black Eagle connection. The rings, bugbear evidence, Khaeros, Skarn, and the route through the southern fens must still be assembled.

## After the Shrine

Mara does not expect forgiveness. She expects a rope, a cell, or quiet revenge from surviving cultists. Her first request is that someone warn her mother without saying Deyan's name in public.

The strongest outcome is **custody under [Father Branislav](the-curate.md)** rather than immediate military imprisonment. Branislav does not declare her innocent. He hears her confession, verifies what he can, and makes her help repair what she helped damage: identify targeted stores, recover bodies, name routes, and testify before people who suffered from the famine.

That is restoration rather than absolution. It also gives the party a concrete political problem: convince Valerian that a valuable witness and defecting young cultist is more useful alive than made into an example.

If she survives the campaign, Mara can become a lay organizer in the food bridge—counting sacks, identifying vulnerable settlements, and doing the unglamorous work that would have kept her from Vesna. Do not make her cheerful quickly. Let usefulness return before peace does.

## Running Mara at the table

- **Angry does not mean irrational.** The grievance that recruited her remains true after the cult's lie is exposed.
- **Let her notice specifics.** She was a copyist and novice: names, dates, routes, liturgical differences, quantities of food.
- **She does not beg.** If threatened with death, she goes pale and becomes very formal.
- **She defends Vesna's sincerity, not their actions.** “They believed it” is testimony and grief, not exoneration.
- **Keep the shame active.** She concealed what frightened her because she had already sacrificed too much to admit error. She understands exactly how ordinary that failure was.
- **Make the choice hers.** The party creates safety and presents the contradiction; Mara must be the one who drops the mace.

## Stats — B/X

Use one of the four acolytes in B2 room 54, changing only her loyalty and first-round behavior.

- **Cleric 1; Neutral and wavering** rather than Chaotic.
- **AC 5:** chain mail beneath rusty-red robes and black cowl.
- **hp 4; ML 8** until offered a credible surrender, then check morale immediately if needed.
- **Weapon:** mace (1d6).
- **Spells:** none. A 1st-level Basic cleric has not yet received spells; in the fiction, this is the promise attached to her coming initiation.
- **Possessions:** 10 gp; iron ring on a cord; worn wooden token from her Luln chapel; folded scrap bearing Deyan's name and the date he disappeared.

The room leader, not Mara, carries the *amulet of protection from good*.
