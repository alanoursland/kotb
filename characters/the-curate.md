# Father Branislav — the Curate of the Keep

*The keeper of the unabsorbed Church of Traladar. The one who knows what the temple actually is, can read the symbols the state church can't — and is distrusted precisely because deep knowledge of Thanatos looks like proximity to him.*

**Role:** the legitimacy that can cleanse the Shrine; the reader of the symbols; the deliverer of the post-hoc tragedy · **Stat block:** B2 Curate (Cleric 5) · **Where:** the Chapel at the Keep · **Spine:** [beats 2, 6, 8](../narrative/progression.md)

## Who he is

Branislav has tended the old faith on this frontier for longer than the Keep's officers have been alive. The chapel is Traladaran because the country is — this is native ground, whatever banner flies over the wall — and he has watched the **Church of Karameikos** absorb and smooth the old beliefs into a tidy state virtue everywhere else. He is one of the last keepers of the unabsorbed thing: the faith that still *remembers what Halav, Petra, and Zirchev were for* — that they arose to **hold Thanatos back.**

That memory is exactly why he's dangerous to be near, and why he's distrusted. He knows the shape of Entropy because his faith was *built around opposing it.* But to people who've forgotten — the assimilated younger clergy, the nervous garrison — a priest who can describe Thanatos's signs in detail looks a little too much like a priest who *consorts* with them. **He carries the campaign's whole tragedy in his own social position:** the one who understands the enemy is mistaken for the enemy.

He is old, careful, and parochial in the way a keeper of a dwindling thing becomes — he guards his flock and his resources closely (he'll spend a *cure* on a member of his congregation, not a stranger). Not warmth withheld from malice; husbandry, from a man with little left and much to protect.

## What the module gives you (B2 as written)

**The Chapel** sits opposite the Guild House: a two-story peaked hall, one large room, a 20'×8' colored-glass window over the eastern altar (worth 350 gp intact), bare wooden pews, an offering box on a heavy pedestal (1–100 cp + 1–100 sp), emptied each evening and deposited with the Banker. A stair in the NW corner leads to the cellar quarters of the Curate and his three Acolytes.

**The Curate (Cleric 5):** AC 0 (*plate mail +1*, normal shield, *ring of protection +1*), hp 24, +1 to hit, ML 10. Wields a **mace +1** (D 2–7) or a **snake staff** (+1 to hit, 2–7; on command becomes a snake and coils the struck victim helpless until recalled, up to 1d4 turns). He **rarely wears armor** unless the Keep is threatened, but is **never without ring and staff.** Spells: *cure light wounds, detect magic, bless, hold person* — **cure used only on his congregation** (an officer, a shopkeeper).

**Three Acolytes (Cleric 1):** hp 6/5/5, plate + shield (AC 2) or robes (AC 9), mace, ML 7.

**The chapel's hidden treasury:** in a secret compartment under the offering-box pedestal (found only by moving the pedestal; two locks — **the Curate and the Castellan hold the only keys**): 3 *potions of healing*, 1 *ESP*, 1 *gaseous form*, and a scroll (*cure disease*, *hold person*, ×3 *cure light wounds*).

**The planted clue:** questioned closely by a friend, the Curate **(50%)** reveals his **distrust of [Father Ariston Vale](father-ariston-vale.md)** (7b), who comes to the Keep regularly. The **Acolytes think highly** of Ariston and will say so. *(In B2 the Priest is a disguised evil cleric, and the Curate is right.)*

## His function in the campaign

- **He reads what the state church can't.** Father [Demetrios](father-demetrios-kallistos.md) — Church of Karameikos, sincere, can't place the iron-ring trappings — hits his limit exactly where Branislav begins. The Curate recognizes the [symbols as alien to the faith](../campaign/the-iron-ring.md) and supplies the name nobody in the party has: the cult is not Traladaran. It's a corruption wearing the liberation mask.
- **He is the legitimacy to cleanse the Shrine.** [Beat 6](../narrative/progression.md) turns on *who* destroys the cult. Cleanse it with the **Church of Karameikos / Demetrios alone** and it's read across the frontier as the Duke attacking native religion — Bargle's design, a martyr-factory. Cleanse it with **Branislav's** hands and word, and it's the *true* faith reclaiming a desecration. **Winning the old Curate's trust and presence is the gate on doing the climax right.**
- **He delivers the tragedy.** The post-hoc gut-punch — that [Vesna](the-hierophant.md) was a misled patriot, that the party served the Baron by killing the cult cleanly with the wrong church — comes through Branislav, weeks later, reading the evidence the party carried out without understanding. He's the voice that turns a clean win into the cold realization at the heart of [beat 8](../narrative/progression.md).

## The visiting Priest — Father Ariston Vale

The Curate's distrust points at **[Father Ariston Vale](father-ariston-vale.md)**, and he is right.

Ariston is a **foreign agent of Thanatos himself** — from neither Thyatis nor Traladara, here on Entropy's orders. He is *not* part of the cult, the Baron, or the Iron Ring. He is a **separate thread of Thanatos's plot**: a spy sent to watch and to **confound the party's success.** His claimed religion, history, methods, and table-ready betrayal are detailed in [his profile](father-ariston-vale.md).

Why it's good:

- **Thanatos works through both witting and unwitting hands.** The cult is an *unwitting* node (Iron Ring, believes it serves Traladaran liberation); the Priest is a *witting* agent (serves Entropy directly, knows exactly what he is). They're uncoordinated — two hands of the same root reaching in separately. **Killing the cult doesn't remove the Priest** — a persistent, mobile antagonist who outlives the Shrine and keeps working against the peace.
- **It sharpens Branislav.** He distrusts the Priest because he can *smell the Entropy* on him. The very knowledge that makes others distrust the Curate is what lets him correctly flag the real threat — the distrusted keeper is right about the charming outsider, and right *because* of the thing he's distrusted for.
- **It preserves the canon clue** (Curate distrusts / Acolytes adore / the Priest works against the party) while giving him a distinct allegiance: **Thanatos, not the Iron Ring.**

## Player challenges & opportunities (a design lens)

- **The man who can save them looks like the threat.** Players may instinctively distrust the grim old priest who knows too much about death-gods — and trust the charming visitor instead. That misjudgment *is* the game. Reward the table that listens to the distrusted native: it's the exact perception the whole campaign teaches.
- **His trust is a real objective with a real payoff.** He's not a quest-giver to tick off; he's the legitimacy that makes the climax clean instead of catastrophic. Earning Branislav (over the Castellan's skepticism, over the Acolytes' preference for the visitor) is a *political* win that changes how beat 6 resolves.
- **He balances the Castellan.** The Keep's two powers are **[Castellan Valerian](castellan-valerian.md)** (martial, skeptical of peace) and the **Curate** (moral, keeper of the old truth). A party can play them against or with each other — and the chapel's hidden potions/scrolls and the Curate's own power (a 5th-level cleric with a snake staff) make him a meaningful ally, not just a lore-dispenser.
- **The "holy contingent" — composition is political.** You floated a large body of clergy taking on the cult; the catch is *who's in it.* A Karameikos-led assault reads as conquest; a Traladar-led one as redemption; a mixed one is a negotiation in itself. Let the party *assemble* the coalition and let its makeup decide how the victory is read. That's a richer set-piece than a stat-block sum.

## Name & identity

Provisional **Father Branislav / Branko** (Traladaran/Slavic). Like the cast's other naming beats, there's a quiet motif available: the old man's *name and faith* are the unassimilated originals the empire never managed to absorb — he is what the Church of Karameikos is built on top of and pretends to have replaced. If you ever want the reveal, *his* is the name that proves the old faith never actually died; it just got old and went unheard. Swap freely.

## Running him at the table

- **Grim, careful, unspectacular.** No fire-and-brimstone — a tired keeper who has seen the faith hollowed and the frontier bled, and is wary of strangers because strangers have rarely meant well.
- **He won't volunteer the worst.** Getting the truth about the Shrine (and the visiting Priest) out of him takes *earned* trust — he's been disbelieved too often to lead with his deepest knowledge. The 50% distrust-reveal is a starting friction, not a freebie.
- **He guards his flock and his stores.** Cures and chapel resources go to his congregation first. Generosity is something the party earns by proving they're not just another set of outsiders passing through.
- **He is right, and used to not being believed.** Play the weariness of a man who has been correct and ignored for years. When the party finally *does* believe him, let it land on him.
