# Lysandra — The Medusa in the Cult's Cell

*The limit case of the whole campaign's thesis. The case where circumstance produced something you still cannot trust — and you can't know which until you risk it.*

**Role:** the irreducible moral test; one of two "the label was sometimes *right*" controls · **Stat block:** B2 medusa as written · **Where:** a prisoner in the cult's CELL, staged as bait

> **Role in the campaign:** Not a faction, not a fight, not a rescue — a **gamble.** She's the purest test of the campaign's claim (*circumstance, not nature*), and the hard footnote to it: circumstance can make something real that you still shouldn't bare your throat to. Keep the conspiracy's hand *off* her — she resolves on different logic than everything else in the ravine.

---

## The name (her whole character in one fact)

The cult calls her a gorgon. The caves call her the snake-woman, the thing in the cell, *it.* She remembers being **Lysandra** — a person, the last of a line nobody living recalls. That gap *is* her: the same screw Brundt turns shedding "Who-Comes-When-Paid" and Khaeros turns choosing his own name, given a final, crueler twist — **she kept her name through a lifetime of everyone insisting she didn't deserve one.** Speak it correctly and you may be the first being in years to do so. Treat it as a monster's noise and you've confirmed her entire empirical record in a single word.

---

## What the module gives you (B2 as written)

Staged as **bait**: a "fair maiden obviously in need of rescuing," visible only legs-up-to-shoulders around a corner, so rescuers commit before they see what she is.

- **AC 8, HD 4, hp 20.** Asp-hair **bite** (save vs. Poison or **die**); the **gaze** petrifies anyone who looks — *including* those fighting her from the front (save vs. Turned to Stone).
- The evil cleric planned to **remove her snakes, blind her, and eventually sacrifice her** to a demon at a special rite.
- She holds a real **potion of *stone to flesh*** (enough for **six** petrified victims) and does **not** intend to give it away.
- She'll **promise to cure** the party's petrified companions if freed — and **if freed, she'll try to stone her rescuers.** *The betrayal is in the text, not invented.*

---

## Who she is — and why she betrays

Take the warning seriously: trusting her is a genuine gamble with teeth, not set dressing. But her treachery has a *cause*, and the cause is the whole point.

She is a creature whose curse makes **proximity lethal and eye contact fatal.** Every being she has ever met has tried to kill her on sight — and *reasonably so*, because looking at her can end you. For her, striking first isn't malice; it's the only armor that has ever worked. And in B2 she's *freshly brutalized* — taken by zombies, slated for blinding and sacrifice — so her readiness to strike at anyone who approaches isn't gratuitous. It's learned, and recently reinforced.

**The heart of it:** "everyone who approaches means to kill or use me" is not paranoia — it is her **entire empirical record,** possibly across her whole life. The party freeing her is the **first counter-example she has ever had.** Whether anyone — monster or person — can update on one counter-example against a lifetime of confirmation is the real question. *Most can't.* That's why betrayal is the likely outcome and trust would be the miracle — not because she's evil, but because that's the human condition with the cruelty turned all the way up.

**She rhymes with Khaeros.** Both were *trained* into striking first by a world that never once opened with peace. The difference is the difficulty of the test: Khaeros can be reached with proof and a trusted intermediary; Lysandra is the case where **you can't tell the curse from the character until you risk it — and you may not like the answer.**

---

## Where she's from (origin — keep it legendary, keep the conspiracy out)

The cult **recently took** her with zombie guards, so she was *nearby and reachable* — living or passing through the **Cruth Lowlands / Radlebb frontier** ([the borderland](../campaign/the-borderland.md)) when their reach closed on her. Recommended origin:

**A borderland solitary — the last, or near-last, of a cursed line from some fallen ruin in the southern wilds.** Beautiful and powerful once; now a hated, hunted thing that petrifies anyone who looks. This does three things at once:

- **Explains her capture, grimly and exactly.** A being who survives by *total isolation* and *attacking first* is paradoxically defenseless against a patient enemy that **doesn't need to look at her.** The undead don't meet her eyes, feel no horror, can be sent in waves. The one defense that stops every living thing — *I will turn you to stone* — is **useless against the cult specifically.** She was safe from the whole world and helpless against the single predator in it her gaze can't stop. And her isolation, the thing that kept her alive, is *why no one noticed she was taken.*
- **Fits the ecology.** The borderland is the campaign's refuge for **intelligence gone solitary** — she belongs to the same set as Khaeros and the mad hermit: the too-dangerous and too-monstrous, washed up in the wild margin nobody patrols.
- **Rhymes with the whole campaign.** A fallen people, mistaken for a monster, whose entire condition is the residue of a history nobody remembers. The dispossessed, in miniature.

> **Keep the Iron Ring's hand off her.** A medusa is a *valuable* asset (petrification is a weapon), so there's a tempting version where she was a *targeted* acquisition pointing up the pipeline. Resist it, or use it only as a faint possibility. Khaeros and the rebel bugbear already carry the conspiracy; her job is to be the **clean moral limit-case**, and that job dies the moment she becomes another clue. *(Canon flag: Mystara's deep history has fallen reptilian/serpent legacies, but verify specifics against the gazetteers — treat her line as local legend you control, not pinned canon.)*

---

## Her place in the prisoner taxonomy

When you sort the caves' captives, she lands in **her own category** — *not* meat (hobgoblins), *not* labor/commodity (bugbears/Iron Ring), *not* a homeless freed human. She is the **"captive whose freedom might be a trap."** Don't fold her into the [slavery accounting](../campaign/captivity-and-hunger.md); she resolves on a *gamble*, not a *rescue* or a *purchase.*

---

## Her function as a control

With the **evil human "Hero"** in the bugbear pens (a fully human creature who is simply a murderer), Lysandra is one of **two cases that prove the labels aren't always slander.** If every monster turned out misunderstood-but-good, the campaign would collapse into a *different* flatness — "monsters are secretly nice." Some aren't. **Keeping her betrayal real is what makes the redemptions elsewhere mean something.**

---

## Player challenges & opportunities (a design lens)

- **A gamble with real stakes on the table.** The *stone to flesh* potion (six doses) is the lever: if the party let companions get petrified reaching her, her potion may be the only cure — so freeing her isn't abstract trust, it's "we need the thing only she has, and freeing her to get it may kill us." That's a genuine dilemma, not a morality quiz.
- **Method is the puzzle.** Reward cleverness about **non-sight contact** — blindfolds, mirrors, an intermediary, distance, negotiating through a wall. A party that engineers *genuine* safety (where betrayal gains her nothing) might discover the treachery was **contingent** (a threat-response), not **essential** (who she is). Or might discover the module was right. **Both outcomes are good — don't resolve the ambiguity for them.**
- **Don't pre-decide her.** The whole point is irreducibility. If they barrel in trusting and sight-exposed, the betrayal fires and it's *earned*. If they earn real safety, leave the door open a crack and let *their* read of her carry the risk. The campaign in miniature: *you cannot know a nature from a scar until someone bets their life on the answer.*
- **The doorway (optional, earnable).** If she's reached and *doesn't* betray, she has somewhere to go: the ruin she's the last of. A party that wins her could **escort her home** and find a vanished people hiding in plain sight in the borderland — a whole side-adventure, available only to players who earned it. She's a much bigger role as a *doorway to buried history* than as a betrayal-trap. Hold it in reserve; build it only if they get there.

---

## Running her (the upshot)

Play the bait straight — the staged maiden, the partial view, the plea, the promised cure. Then let the party's **method** decide what they learn. Trusting and exposed → the betrayal fires, earned. Clever and genuinely safe → leave it ambiguous and make *them* decide whether to believe her. She's the one creature in the caves whose answer the campaign refuses to hand you.

---

*Everything above is interpretation layered on the B2 medusa as written — the bait staging, the stats, the gaze, the potion, the betrayal. Nothing contradicts the module; it just asks why she strikes first, and notices the answer is the same one the rest of the campaign keeps giving.*
