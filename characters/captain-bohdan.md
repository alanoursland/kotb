# Captain Bohdan — "the Wall"

*The biggest man in the Keep, used to being the biggest man anywhere — until an ogre walks through his gate.*

**Role:** captain of the guard; the party's day-to-day Keep contact; [Valerian's](castellan-valerian.md) right hand at the gate and on patrol · **Stat block:** B2 guard captain (Fighter, **STR 18**) · **Heritage:** Traladaran (a local risen on strength and merit) · **Name provisional**

> **The hook:** Bohdan is *unusually large* — a head taller and a hand broader than any other man on the wall — and his authority, his self-image, and the way a room rearranges itself around him all rest on that. He's spent his whole life being the biggest thing people had to step around, and he leans into it: he *tries to be a presence.* Then [Brundt](brundt-the-ogre.md) ducks through his gate, and for the first time in his life Bohdan is the small one — in front of his own men. What he does with that is his whole arc.

## Who he is

Bohdan came up the hard way — a big Traladaran farm-boy who joined the garrison because his back and his reach were worth more there than behind a plough, and rose because he was *good,* not just large. He commands the gate and the watch under [Castellan Valerian](castellan-valerian.md): the Thyatian lord gives the orders, and Bohdan is the one who carries them out where the spears meet the road. That arrangement suits him. He's not a politician; he's the man who holds the line, and he's never been anything else.

His size is the load-bearing fact of his life. He has always been the biggest man in any room, and somewhere along the way that became *who he is* — not arrogance exactly, but a habit of standing a little too straight, speaking a little too low, filling a doorway on purpose. He *tries to be a presence,* because presence has always done half his job for him. Most of the time it works. The men respect him; strangers think twice; trouble tends to find a quieter table when Bohdan walks in.

## The ogre (his arc)

Then the party brings an ogre to the Keep, and the captain who has never been the smaller man finds himself escorting nine feet of something that makes his eighteen-strength frame look ordinary. He starts the way he always does — squaring up, projecting, trying to be the *presence* in the room. **It doesn't land.** You can't loom at a creature half again your height, and his men can see it.

And then the escort goes on, and the thing he's guarding turns out not to be a monster straining at a leash but a creature *delighted* — gawking at glazed windows, breathing in at the baker's door, buying a *bed.* Somewhere on that walk, Bohdan stops competing and starts… watching. Because here is the quiet thing only Bohdan, of everyone at the Keep, is positioned to feel: **he knows what it is to be seen as a size before a person.** He's been the big frightening shape in the doorway his whole life, the body people read before they hear the man. He recognizes it in Brundt — the patient stillness of someone who's spent a lifetime letting people decide whether he's allowed to be near them — and the recognition is what turns the wary handler into the grudging guide. By the end he might be the one telling the ogre which stall has the better cheese.

He won't say any of this. He might not have words for it. But it's the start of an arc the whole campaign is about, run on the one human whose own life rhymes with it: *the biggest isn't the point, and being a body isn't being a person.* If you want a human at the Keep who **gets it before Valerian does,** it's Bohdan.

## How to play him at the table

- **Leads with size, learning to lead with steadiness.** Early on, play the presence — the straightened back, the low voice, the doorway-filling. Let the ogre (and competent people who aren't impressed by mass) gently expose its limits, and let him grow toward authority that doesn't depend on being the biggest.
- **Proud, a little vain, wholly decent.** He likes being the big man and it's a forgivable vanity. Underneath it he's loyal, brave, and kind in the gruff way of someone who's never had to develop charm because his shoulders did the talking.
- **The gate is his.** He's the party's recurring contact — the face at the wall, the escort on Keep business, the sergeant of the muscle when Valerian lends men. Make him a *relationship,* not a function.
- **Respect his competence, not his size, and you have him.** People who are visibly unimpressed by his frame but take his *soldiering* seriously win him fast. People who treat him as just a big slab get the cold professional.

## Player challenges & opportunities (a design lens)

- **The human echo of the thesis, in one NPC.** Bohdan's arc — a man built on being the biggest, meeting something bigger, and finding his worth elsewhere — is the campaign's whole lesson at human scale and low stakes. A party watching him soften has been handed the thesis a second way.
- **The early human ally.** He can *get it* before the Castellan does — the man who, having walked Brundt through the market, will vouch for the federation to the other guards and to a doubtful Valerian. A persuade-by-results lever for the [whole political track](castellan-valerian.md).
- **Muscle when it matters.** STR 18 and a guard detail: won over, Bohdan is real military weight for the Shrine assault or a Black Eagle reprisal, and a fighter the party will be glad to have at their shoulder.
- **The minotaur may become his subordinate—but need not.** Bohdan is the first officer who must honor [Khaeros's](khaeros-the-minotaur.md) safe-conduct, brief patrols, and treat him as an armed free traveler. If Khaeros later chooses the garrison, the two trained fighters can grow into professional respect. If he chooses the Guild caravan, Bohdan's salute at the gate acknowledges that he is neither prisoner nor subordinate. → [Khaeros at the Keep — Safe Conduct](../campaign/the-minotaur-at-the-keep.md)
- **A gentle flaw to play with.** His size-vanity is a handle — bruise it (a PC who's stronger, a creature that dwarfs him) and watch how he handles being humbled; that's character revealed under pressure, and it's where his decency shows.

## Name & identity

**Bohdan** — Traladaran — whom the garrison calls **"the Wall"** (he *is* the gate's wall, and the pun isn't lost on a campaign about who gets walled out). A local strongman serving a Thyatian lord; the name and nickname are swappable, the *role* — the biggest human, built on a presence the ogre dismantles — is the point.
