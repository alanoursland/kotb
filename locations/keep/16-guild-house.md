# 16. Guild House

**Zone:** northeastern outer bailey | **Access:** public office; hospitality by status | **Primary use:** dues, permissions, contracts, and trade coordination

> The two-story Guild House looks less like a shop than a small administrative post. Clerks watch traffic, guards watch visitors, and ledgers turn every wagon and craft into a fee, permission, or obligation.

## Run this area

- Traveling guild members may receive lodging and hospitality.
- Traders pay dues equal to 5% of their merchandise's value and receive Guild protection.
- Craftspeople need Guild permission to enter or leave the region, paying 2d6 gp according to the value of their trade.
- The lower floor holds the Guild Master's quarters, two clerks' rooms, and the office.
- The upper floor has two private guest rooms and a dormitory.
- Four loyal men-at-arms are always on duty, two on each floor.

## Campaign use

This is the practical engine of a future caves-Keep market. The Guild can:

- contract wagons and drivers;
- aggregate bulk food or materials from regular ducal and private convoys;
- establish prices, inspection, and payment;
- object that cave trade evades dues or threatens members;
- recognize that safer roads and new labor create profit.

The food bridge is possible because the Keep is supplied. The conflict is who authorizes the extension, who pays, and whether the Guild treats the caves as customers, contractors, or enemies receiving tribute.

## Influence

The Guild Master is socially powerful. Favor improves treatment across the commercial Keep; hostility produces delays, scrutiny, and closed doors without requiring open lawbreaking.

## DM-only

The Guild Master carries a gold ring and coin; a strongbox under the bed holds 712 gp. The clerks and Master are normal people but keep chain, crossbows, and swords in a closet. The four guards are AC 6, Fighter 1, hp 6 each, morale 8.
