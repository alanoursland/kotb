# 18. Old River Ford

> Flat stones cross a brown side-channel between two leaning marker posts. The eastern post is wrapped in reed braid; the western bears three shallow axe cuts. Tiny clawed footprints approach from the south, pause between the signs, and turn back.

## At a glance

This is the deep cross-route between marsh, Spiderwood, and the bandits' river corridor. It demonstrates the region's working equilibrium: every living group knows someone else's boundary and ordinarily chooses avoidance.

## Discoveries

- **Stelian:** The small tracks include both kobold and goblin parties at different times. Neither approached the lizardfolk fishery or Aldric's transfer route.
- **Draconic speaker:** The reed braid is a lizardfolk warning: *trade water east; armed strangers west*.
- **Leontes:** The axe cuts are a bandit patrol sign meaning *do not cross in darkness*.
- **Alexios:** The ford links the Barony's old wet route to channels that can bypass the road, but the lizardfolk's occupied territory prevents casual use farther east.
- **At night while bandits remain:** A two-person bandit watch checks the crossing. They carry clubs and axes suited to breaking skeletons.

## After the bandits leave

This is the first place the change becomes visible. After 1d3 nights, animal tracks avoid the western bank and undead footprints cross north-south without concealment. The next night visit brings **1d4+2 skeletons** unless the party is specifically shadowing another patrol.

Turned undead flee north toward [area 15](15-broken-undead-patrol.md). The lizardfolk withdraw from the ford but do not abandon their deeper marsh; they expect outsiders to deal with dead rising from outsider ground.
