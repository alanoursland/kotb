# 9. Netmaker's Lagoon

> Sunlight lies open on a broad lagoon. Reed platforms stand above the water on forked poles. Fish dry over low smoke, woven traps hang in rows, and half a dozen canoes move between channels without wasted motion.

## At a glance

This is the best evidence that the marsh is productive, inhabited, and organized. After the necklace exchange, it becomes the agreed border market.

## Before relations

Unescorted visitors are challenged from canoes and directed toward the [Tribute Pool](06-tribute-pool.md). The lizardfolk do not invite strangers farther into the fishery.

Niko's old tracks skirt the human side of this lagoon and turn north toward [area 3](03-fishermens-path.md). He never entered the claimed platforms and never met the lizardfolk.

## After relations

Trade takes place on the northwestern reed platform. Humans remain on the firm bank; lizardfolk arrive by canoe. Expected etiquette is simple:

1. Call out before entering the marked channel.
2. Display goods openly.
3. Touch nothing not offered.
4. Finish the bargain plainly—no assumed favors or unspoken debt.

The lizardfolk offer fish, smoked eel, waterfowl, eggs, reeds, hides, leeches, and wetland herbs. They seek cordage, nets, pottery, salt, metal hooks and tools, cloth, and finished leather.

**Tavin** can turn the necklace exchange into a repeatable trade schedule. **Danica** can write terms for human authorities, but Sessrik recognizes spoken witness and fulfilled delivery, not seals on paper.
