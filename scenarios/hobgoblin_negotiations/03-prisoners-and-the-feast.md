# The Prisoners and the Feast

This is the negotiation's moral and material center. Six people are chained beside the fire while the feast hall prepares for a banquet.

## Karrgan's calculation

He does not release food while his own families go hungry. He will exchange the prisoners when living captives become more valuable than meat and when the new rule does not pay his people to take replacements.

## Workable terms

- Food equal to the immediate meal value arrives now.
- All six prisoners are released together and assessed before being armed.
- Future captives are reported and held for a short stated period.
- Return brings food, goods, lawful bounty, or reciprocal prisoners—but only when the detention arose from a real conflict, not abduction for profit.
- Taking a captive merely to claim payment violates the agreement and ends that patrol's allotment.
- Human dead and captives cease to be provisions once the first delivery is accepted.

## Make the change public

Move the scene to [area 25](../../locations/caves/25-hobgoblin-feast-hall.md). Replace the expected meat with grain, beans, cheese, or salt pork. Karrgan announces that live prisoners now provision the stronghold better than dead ones. The party does not force him to confess moral enlightenment before his command.

## The released prisoners

Use [The Prisoners](../../campaign/the-prisoners.md). Gratitude is not uniform: Murz escapes like a POW, Renk may attack, and the human “Hero” elsewhere remains dangerous. The exchange proves policy can improve without pretending every individual is safe.

## Failure and recovery

- A demand for unconditional release produces: “Who feeds the hall?” Answer with actual food or a guaranteed short delivery.
- Paying per captive without a no-replacement rule creates a kidnapping market. Karrgan accepts; the DM should state the danger clearly.
- Secretly freeing prisoners during talks ends safe conduct. Continue through [After Hostilities](09-after-hostilities.md).
