# Approach — Engineering First

This is the strongest route because it recognizes what Yiznak believes his people contribute before asking what they must stop doing.

## Give Yiznak a real problem

Present one problem the campaign already needs solved:

- drainage for the proposed ravine wagon ground;
- a safe gradient for heavy food carts;
- a washed-out section of the Riverfork road;
- a defensible market site that does not resemble a military camp;
- inspection of Cave A's food storage against damp and rats;
- warning markers and bypasses for trapped approaches.

Yiznak studies it, asks a few irritatingly precise questions, and identifies a genuine flaw. Examples:

- the wagon ground becomes a pool after the first hard rain;
- the turn radius strands the rear cart in missile range;
- a drainage cut sends runoff directly toward Cave A;
- the proposed sentry point cannot see the upper ravine path;
- grain sacks will wick moisture from the stone floor.

## The player test

The test is not whether the party compliments the answer. It is whether they use it.

- Brynja can discuss grade, supports, stone, and labor as a peer.
- Tavin can translate the design into loads and supply needs.
- Leontes can describe what guards require without converting the project into a fort pointed at the caves.
- Stelian can inspect runoff, tracks, and approach lines.
- Any character can pass by asking a serious follow-up question and changing the plan.

If the party dismisses the advice and returns to praising “clever little traps,” Yiznak becomes polite and distant.

## Yiznak's proposal

He offers a small first contract:

- kobold surveyors mark the site;
- the party supplies cord, picks, lamp oil, timber, and a stated ration allotment;
- half the food is delivered at the start and half after inspection;
- recognized workers receive safe passage while on the route;
- both sides place one observer at the work.

This project can become the target of [the Broken Culvert sabotage](09-broken-culvert-sabotage.md), but it does not have to. If time is short, let successful work stand as Proof and proceed to the agreement.
