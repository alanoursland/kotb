# Approach — Authority or Ultimatum

The Duke's commission matters because it proves the party can make the Keep listen. It does not make the Duke owner of Cave A or make Yiznak a subject who has agreed to obey.

## Authority used well

Useful claims sound like:

- “We are authorized to make terms Riverfork must honor.”
- “The Castellan will receive our agreement and account for deliveries.”
- “Attacks on the road must stop; in exchange, we can open food and paid work.”
- “We can issue recognized passage marks if you can guarantee them.”

Yiznak asks who pays when a ducal promise fails and whether the party can bind the Merchants' Guild. Honest limits improve trust more than grand assurances.

## Authority used badly

Threats sound like:

- “The Duke owns this land.”
- “Dismantle the traps before we speak.”
- “Submit or be cleared out.”
- “Karrgan has agreed for all the caves.”

Yiznak does not debate sovereignty at the mouth. The planks remain behind the pit, bows stay trained on the passage, and the answer is:

> “Then show us which part of your order crosses first.”

This is a warning, not an invitation to test the trap.

## Recovering from an ultimatum

The party can step back without losing face by distinguishing commission from demand:

> “That was what we are permitted to do, not what we came hoping to do.”

Then offer one concrete reciprocal term: food for a ceasefire, payment for a survey, or investigation of the missing residents. If the party already caused harm, continue through [After a Hostile Entry](06-after-a-hostile-entry.md).
