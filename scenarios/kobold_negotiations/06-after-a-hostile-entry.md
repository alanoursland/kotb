# Recovery — After Stealth, a Trap, or Violence

A bad entrance should have consequences without making the campaign's diplomatic premise depend on perfect player behavior.

## What may have happened

- The front rank fell into the covered pit.
- Tree ambushers fired and the party returned fire.
- The party found the pit and tried to bypass it silently.
- *Sleep* or another spell disabled guards.
- The party forced area 1 before realizing families live deeper inside.

The kobolds retreat through their alarm chain, hold the protected corner at area 4, and prepare the common chamber. They do not pursue heavily armored intruders onto the ravine floor unless they believe their young are threatened.

## Immediate routes back

### Withdraw and call out

Leaving the cave, treating wounds, and requesting a truce proves the party can stop escalating. The kobolds retrieve their own wounded before answering.

### Use the staff

The staff can break a living attack for one turn. The first question should be practical: “Can the wounded be collected?” or “What must we do to leave without further fighting?” Do not open with a speech about peace while someone bleeds in the pit.

### Surrender an advantage

Release a captured guard, return a dropped weapon, extinguish a fire, or disclose a trapped escape route the kobolds have not noticed. A voluntary concession is stronger than apology alone.

## Restitution

Yiznak expects replacement spears, medicine, food, labor to repair damage, or another proportionate act. He does not demand the party surrender itself. Deaths require a more serious provisional settlement and may prevent full agreement during the current visit.

If the party killed young or continued attacking after a clear withdrawal, negotiation is not restored by one check. They must leave, demonstrate restraint elsewhere, and return through Brundt, returned prisoners, or a respected third party.

## Yiznak's response

If the party accepts responsibility without insisting the kobolds fired first, Yiznak says:

> “Defenses worked. You survived them. Now tell me whether you came to learn or only to prove that.”

Proceed to a limited negotiation. The first agreement is usually a ceasefire, a second meeting, and one small exchange rather than full political terms.
